FAQ

Unlocking questions

The answers I give most often — so you know exactly what to expect.

Yes. Carrier unlocking is completely legal in the United States. Since 2014, it has been legal for consumers to unlock phones they own. I only perform legal carrier unlocks — not stolen devices, not blacklisted IMEIs, and not software hacks that void warranties.
Many carrier unlocks are processed same-day or within 24-72 hours, depending on the carrier. AT&T and T-Mobile unlocks are often completed within hours. Verizon phones are sometimes already unlocked after 60 days. I check eligibility immediately and give you a firm timeline before starting.
No. If a phone is blacklisted (reported lost, stolen, or has an unpaid balance), it cannot be carrier unlocked. I check IMEI status before attempting any unlock. If your phone is blacklisted, I can explain your options — but I won't charge you for an impossible unlock.
No. Official carrier unlocking does not void your warranty. I use legal, carrier-approved methods — not jailbreaking, rooting, or unauthorized software modifications. Your warranty remains intact, and your device stays exactly as secure as it was before.
Your IMEI is a 15-digit unique identifier for your phone. Find it by dialing *#06# on your phone — the number appears instantly. You can also find it in Settings > General > About (iPhone) or Settings > About Phone > Status (Android). I need this number to check unlock eligibility.
In many cases, I don't even need the physical device — I can process the unlock remotely using the IMEI number. If the device is needed, ship it to Hailey Device Repair and I'll return it the same day the unlock completes. Total turnaround is typically 2-4 days including shipping.
