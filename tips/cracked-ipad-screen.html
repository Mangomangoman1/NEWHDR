Cracked iPad screen:
safe to keep using?

Samuel, Owner & Technician · November 22, 2026

Apple says not to use an iPad with a cracked screen, because it may cause injury. If it has to last a few more days, cover the glass, keep small kids off it and back it up today.

How big the repair is depends on two things: what broke, and which iPad it is. A clean picture with touch working everywhere points to glass damage only. Lines or dark patches mean the display itself was hurt, and dead touch means the touch layer was. And iPads aren’t built alike: on some the glass is a separate part from the LCD, and on others Apple lists the display as fully laminated, one bonded piece. Two quick tests and the model name settle most of it.

For an iPad, iPad mini, iPad Air or iPad Pro with cracked front glass, whether or not it still works. For a cracked phone, or a crack that might only be in the screen protector, the guides linked below fit better.

01 / Apple’s answer first

Is it safe to use an iPad with a cracked screen?

Apple’s answer is short. The iPad User Guide says: “Don’t use iPad with a cracked screen, as it may cause injury.” It also says that if you suspect damage to the iPad or its battery, you should stop using it, because it may cause overheating or injury.

A working picture doesn’t change that. Cracked glass can have sharp edges and can shed small slivers, and you can’t tell which by looking. Don’t run a finger along the crack to find out.

In real life an iPad sometimes has to last a few more days. If it does, treat it as broken glass that happens to light up: cover it, back it up, keep small children off it and get the repair booked. The steps are under until it’s fixed.

Stop using it altogether if any of these is true.

Glass is loose, flaking or missing. The screen is lifting away from the frame, or the iPad looks bent or bulged. It gets hot. Liquid got in through the crack. A lifting screen can be a battery problem, so read screen lifting or swollen battery before you charge it again.

02 / Two tests, two minutes

Did only the glass break, or the display too?

The crack you can see is in the top glass. What matters for the repair is whether the parts under it still work. Two tests answer that, and neither needs you to press on the crack.

  • The picture test. Turn the brightness up. Look at a plain white screen, such as a new blank note, then at a dark photo. You’re looking for thin lines, dark or inky patches, blotches of color, flicker, or areas that are lit unevenly.
  • The touch test. Touch and hold an app icon until it lifts, then drag it slowly around the whole screen, into every corner and along every edge. If it drops in the same place twice, that area is dead. It’s the same slow-drag idea described in touch screen not working after a drop.
LinesDark patchColor blotchDead zone

Not sure whether a mark is in the screen or in the picture? Take a screenshot and look at it on another device. Apple says to press the top button and a volume button at the same time, or the top button and the Home button on an iPad that has one. If the mark isn’t in the screenshot, it’s in the display.

Clean picture, and touch works everywhere
As far as anyone can tell from the outside, the glass is what broke. Whether that’s a glass-only repair depends on the model, below.
Lines, dark patches, color blotches or flicker
The display itself is damaged. A dark patch can sit unchanged for a long time or grow quickly, and a damaged display can fail completely at any moment. Back up now.
A dead zone, or touch that drops in the same place
The touch layer is damaged. A dead zone can come back now and then, but it doesn’t stay fixed. What that means for the repair depends on the build, below: on an iPad with separate glass, the touch layer is part of the glass and is replaced with it. On a fully laminated iPad, it’s part of the display.
It taps or types by itself
Lock it and keep it off when you aren’t using it. Ghost touch after a cracked screen is written for iPhone, but it explains why to act before false touches enter the wrong passcode for you.
03 / Why iPads differ from phones

Why some iPads need only glass, and others a whole display

On a phone, the glass, the touch layer and the display are usually one bonded part. iPads come in two builds:

Glass + touchLCD
Separate glassThe glass and its touch layer are one part. The LCD that makes the picture is another part underneath, with a thin gap between them.
One bonded part
Fully laminatedThe glass, the touch layer and the display are bonded together with no gap. The glass can’t be swapped on its own.

“Fully laminated” is Apple’s own term. Its tech specs list it under Display for the iPad mini, iPad Air and iPad Pro models named in the sources below, and leave it off the spec pages for iPad (9th generation), iPad (10th generation) and iPad (A16).

You can often see the difference. Tilt the iPad and look at the edge of the picture. On a separate-glass iPad it sits a little below the surface. On a laminated one it looks painted onto the glass.

To check your own model, Apple’s “Identify your iPad model” page matches the model number to its name and links each one to its tech specs. Look for “Fully laminated” in the Display list.

04 / By model

Glass-only or full display, by iPad model

Two terms first. Glass-only means the cracked glass and touch layer are replaced and your LCD stays. Full assembly means the glass and display come as one bonded part, so both are replaced together.

By model · What can be replaced on its own

Glass-only is possibleThe glass and touch layer are replaced. Your LCD stays.
  • iPad (the standard model): every generation, through iPad (A16)
  • iPad mini 1, 2 and 3
  • iPad Air 1, the 2013 original
Full display assemblyGlass and display come as one bonded part.
  • iPad mini 4 and later, including iPad mini (A17 Pro)
  • iPad Air 2 and every Air since, through the M4 models
  • iPad Pro: every model, 9.7-inch to 13-inch, including the M4 and M5 OLED models

Glass-only still depends on the picture test. If the picture has lines, dark patches or color blotches, the LCD underneath is damaged too, and an iPad on the glass-only side needs a display as well. Dead touch on its own is different: on these models the touch layer comes with the new glass.

A screen repair doesn’t touch what’s stored on the iPad, but back it up anyway. And check your coverage before you pay anyone: Apple says its warranty doesn’t cover accidental damage, so if the iPad has an AppleCare plan, look at what that covers first.

05 / Hard tip, broken glass

Can you use an Apple Pencil on a cracked iPad screen?

Don’t. That isn’t Apple’s wording. It’s plain sense about dragging a hard, narrow tip across broken glass. The tip can catch in a crack, lift a flake of glass and get chewed up doing it, and pressing to write puts force exactly where the glass is weakest.

The same goes for a kid’s stylus, and for fingernails. If the Pencil stopped drawing in one area after the drop, note where, because that belongs in the quote. If it won’t pair or charge at all, that’s a different problem: see Apple Pencil not working.

06 / A stopgap, not a fix

What to do until it’s fixed

  1. Back it up today. A damaged display can fail without warning, and a dead screen can’t tap “Trust” or take a passcode. Apple’s iCloud steps: Settings, your name, iCloud, iCloud Backup, Back Up Now, and stay on Wi-Fi until it finishes. The date and time of the last backup shows under Back Up Now. Or connect it to a computer with a cable and click Back Up Now in the Finder on a Mac, or in the Apple Devices app on a Windows PC.
  2. Cover a flat, stable crack. A screen protector laid over it keeps fingers off the glass and holds small pieces in place for a while. Don’t press bubbles out across the crack. If the glass is raised or shedding, don’t cover it and carry on: stop using it.
  3. Put it in a case with a raised edge, and close the cover in a bag. Pressure from books in a backpack is the kind of new stress that can make a crack run.
  4. Keep it dry. A crack is an opening, so keep it away from drinks, the bath and wet hands.
  5. Leave the glass alone. No pressing, no picking at loose pieces, no glue, and don’t try to lift the glass off yourself.
A protector doesn’t make it safe.It lowers the chance of a cut while you arrange the repair. Apple’s advice not to use an iPad with a cracked screen still applies. Not sure whether the crack is in the protector that’s already on it? Check with cracked protector or cracked screen.
07 / Small hands, shared devices

If it’s a kid’s iPad or a school iPad

Many iPads are a child’s, a family’s or a school’s, and that changes what “a few more days” should look like.

  • Small children shouldn’t use it uncovered. They swipe fast, press hard and don’t notice a sliver of glass. Apple’s injury warning matters most here. Cover it or put it away until it’s fixed.
  • If the school issued it, tell the school first. It’s their device. Schools may have their own repair or insurance process, and may not allow an outside repair. Ask before anyone opens it.
  • If it’s a shared family iPad, find out whose Apple Account it’s signed in to and make sure that person runs the backup. Schoolwork and saved games count as data too.
  • After the repair, a rugged case with a raised lip and a screen protector are worth having before it goes back into small hands.
08 / Send the evidence, not a guess

What to send for an accurate quote

  • The model. Settings, General, About shows the Model Name. On many iPads the model number, an A followed by four digits, is also printed on the back.
  • A photo with the screen off, tilted under a light so the cracks show, and one with a white screen on.
  • What the picture test and the touch test showed.
  • Whether a corner or the frame is dented or bent.
  • Whether it’s a school iPad, and whether it’s backed up.
