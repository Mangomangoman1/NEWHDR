What does “reglass”
mean for an iPhone?

Samuel, Owner & Technician · November 23, 2026

“Reglass” is repair-shop shorthand for replacing only the cracked glass on an iPhone and keeping the phone’s original display underneath. It isn’t an Apple term, and some shops use the same word for the back glass, so ask which one a quote means.

On the front of an iPhone, the glass you touch is bonded to the display that makes the picture. A front reglass separates the two and bonds new glass onto the old display. A full screen replacement swaps both together, as one part. On the back, a reglass means new glass bonded to the phone’s original housing. All of these get called “fixing the glass,” which is why two quotes for the same crack can describe different jobs.

This page explains what the word means and what the job involves, so you can read a quote. It isn’t a how-to, and it doesn’t pick a repair for you. If you’re not sure the crack is in the phone’s own glass at all, start with the first link below.

01 / The word

What “reglass” means, front and back

“Reglass,” sometimes written “re-glass,” is repair-trade shorthand for glass-only repair. It’s a shop word, not an Apple one: Apple’s repair manual for the iPhone 16 lists Display and Back Glass as procedures and has none for the front glass on its own. Shops use the word for more than one job:

Front reglass
The cracked front glass is taken off the phone’s own display and new glass is bonded on. The display that makes the picture and senses touch stays in the phone.
A “refurbished” screen
A member-contributed guide on iFixit calls this same process “screen refurbishment.” So a screen sold as refurbished can mean an original display that has been given new glass. That display comes from another phone and goes into yours as a complete part.
Back reglass
The broken back glass is removed from the phone’s housing and new glass is bonded to that same housing.

One word, three jobs. Ask which glass the quote covers, and whether the display in your phone today is the one that will be in it afterwards.

02 / Three ways to fix a cracked front

Reglass vs. full screen replacement vs. aftermarket screen

Apple’s manual treats the front of the phone as one part, the Display: glass and panel together. A cracked front can be fixed three ways. What differs is how much of the original is left.

Aftermarket screens vary. iFixit, which sells replacement screens itself, describes three kinds in its screen repair overview: “soft” OLED, the closest to the original display’s specifications; “hard” OLED, which it says performs nearly as well but is more fragile; and LCD, an economical substitute that’s a little darker and lower in resolution. Apple, for its part, says a nongenuine display might cause touch, brightness or color problems. LCD vs. OLED covers how screen type changes a quote.

03 / At a high level

How a front reglass is done

Two member-contributed repair guides on iFixit show the whole job: one for the iPhone 11 Pro Max (last updated 2024) and one for the iPhone 12 (2021). Both rate it “Difficult.” In outline:

  1. Test, heat, and free the frameThe screen is tested, then warmed on a heated platform. The thin plastic bezel around the glass is worked loose with adhesive remover.
  2. Separate the glassA fine cutting wire is drawn between the cracked glass and the OLED panel.
  3. Remove the old adhesiveThe optically clear adhesive (OCA) left on the panel is peeled or rolled off, and the panel is cleaned.
  4. Laminate new glassNew glass carrying a fresh sheet of OCA is lined up on the panel and pressed in a laminating machine.
  5. Clear the bubbles, test, refitA bubble-removing machine clears the adhesive, picture and touch are tested again, and a bezel is glued back on.

The tools in those guides are a refurbishing bench: a heated separator, cutting wire, alignment molds, a laminating machine, a bubble remover and a UV lamp. The accepted answer to a 2023 question on iFixit’s user forum, about doing the job at home, says the same: done right, it takes vacuum and UV equipment.

04 / The panel under the glass

Why OLED iPhones make it harder

iFixit’s overview says OLED became the norm for iPhones starting with the iPhone X in 2017. On those phones, the panel under the glass is what makes a reglass delicate.

An iFixit editorial from May 2019 says OLED displays contain an ultra-thin layer of organic material, and that any exposure to moisture, oxygen or damage “can kill them completely.” That, it says, makes OLEDs hard to reuse or refurbish. A second iFixit editorial, from July 2019, reports the company’s quality-control specialist saying that soft OLED screens are easier to refurbish, while hard OLEDs are “pretty much dead the moment their glass is cracked.”

Both were written when the iPhone X and XS were current. The two guides above show the job done on later OLED models. Not sure which kind of screen your iPhone has? LCD vs. OLED shows how to tell.

05 / Glass alone won’t fix it

When a reglass isn’t possible

A reglass keeps the display, so it only works when the display is undamaged. The iPhone 12 guide tests picture and touch before anything comes apart. If the panel under the glass was hurt, new glass on top changes nothing.

Any of these means the display itself is damaged.

Lines across the picture, black or ink-like spots, a dark patch, or touch that’s dead in places. Each one rules a reglass out and calls for a complete screen.

  • Lines: see green or white line on an iPhone screen.
  • Spots and dark patches: see black spot on a phone screen. A damaged display can fail completely at any moment, so back up first.
  • Touch: dead touch after a drop generally means the whole display needs replacing. See touch screen not working after a drop.

Check at normal brightness on a plain, light background, and don’t press on the crack. If you’ve been quoted a reglass on a phone that shows any of these, ask what the plan is for the display.

06 / Where the risk sits

What can go wrong with a reglass

The sources name risks at particular steps.

  • The display can be damaged while it’s handled. The iPhone 11 Pro Max guide warns that too much force pulling off the old adhesive can damage the OLED, and that rubbing too hard at the panel’s edge during cleaning can break the touch circuit and leave touch dead.
  • Bubbles. The iPhone 12 guide shows bubbles in the adhesive straight after lamination, before the bubble-removing machine clears them.
  • The bezel. The same guide’s author breaks the plastic bezel taking it off, and calls it extremely easy to break.
  • Dust under the glass. This is one forum account, not a guide: the 2023 answer on iFixit’s forum describes home-kit attempts on two older iPhones that “basically looked like badly applied screen protectors with dust bubbles trapped inside.”

In a full replacement the old display is discarded. In a reglass, the display being handled is the one you’re keeping, which is the reason for the third question in the list below.

07 / Settings, after the repair

What the iPhone itself reports after screen work

Apple says that on iPhone 11 and later, except iPhone SE (2nd and 3rd generation), a repaired phone shows a Parts and Service History section under Settings → General → About, with a status next to Display. It lists four reasons a display can show as Unknown: it isn’t a genuine Apple part, isn’t functioning as expected, hasn’t been verified and linked to the iPhone after the repair, or “has been modified or is otherwise unable to be verified.”

Apple’s pages don’t mention glass-only repair, so they don’t say what a reglassed screen will show. That’s one to ask the shop. What each label means is in “Unknown Part” or “Important Message” after iPhone repair.

08 / The other meaning

When “reglass” means the back glass

On the back, a reglass means new glass bonded to the phone’s original housing. How the back is built depends on the model, so the job does too.

Apple’s iPhone 16 repair manual lists Back Glass as its own procedure, separate from Display and Enclosure. There the back glass is a part with its own flex cable: it’s cut free of its adhesive, unplugged and swapped whole, and Apple recommends running Repair Assistant afterwards. That’s a part replacement, not new glass on an old housing.

The iPhone 12 Pro Max is the other kind. The same 2023 forum answer describes its back glass as glued to the frame with very tough adhesive. It says professional shops burn that adhesive off with a computer-controlled laser, while a heat gun puts the parts just behind the glass at risk; that a slipped scraper can cut the wireless charging coil; and that replacement glass usually has a larger camera opening, which leaves a wider gap around the camera than the original. Its alternative: a complete rear housing, with the phone’s parts moved across.

For a back quote, ask whether it’s new glass on your housing or a new back part. Whether a cracked back is safe to keep using is in cracked iPhone back glass.

09 / Settle it for your phone

Questions to ask any shop quoting a reglass

  1. Which glass is this for, front or back?
  2. Is the display in my phone now staying in it? Or is a refurbished or aftermarket screen going in as a complete part?
  3. What happens if the display fails during the job? Who covers it, and what goes in instead?
  4. If it can’t be reglassed, what part goes in? Genuine, refurbished original or aftermarket. If aftermarket: soft OLED, hard OLED or LCD?
  5. What does the warranty cover? Ask about touch, picture, bubbles or dust under the glass, and lifting at the edges.
  6. What will Parts and Service History show afterwards?

Get the answers in writing, on the quote.

10 / A second read

Want a second read on a quote?

  • The exact iPhone model.
  • The wording of the quote: front or back, and any words like “reglass,” “refurbished,” “OLED” or “aftermarket.”
  • A photo of the front with the screen on, and one of the back.
  • Whether the screen has been replaced before.
