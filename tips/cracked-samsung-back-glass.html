Galaxy and Pixel · Cracked back glass · Safe to use?

Cracked back glass
on a Galaxy or Pixel?

Samuel, Owner & Technician · November 22, 2026

A phone with a cracked back can keep working, but you can’t count on its water resistance any more, and the glass can cut. Cover a flat, stable crack with a case, keep it dry and get the back replaced. Stop using and charging it if the back is lifting or bulging, or the phone runs hot.

The back glass is a cover. The battery sits under it, along with the wireless charging coil on models that have one, and it closes off the camera area and the back of the phone’s seal. Samsung says chips and cracks can significantly reduce a Galaxy’s ability to resist dust and water. Google goes further for Pixel: its safety guide says not to use a phone with a damaged enclosure, because a damaged phone may overheat or cause injury. So a case is a stopgap, not a fix. The fix is a new back panel, and it’s a repair I do on Samsung and Pixel phones.

For cracked, chipped or shattered glass on the back of a Samsung Galaxy or Google Pixel. For an iPhone, a cracked front screen, or a back that’s lifting away from the frame, the guides linked below fit better.

01 / Under the glass

What the back glass on a Galaxy or Pixel protects

The back glass is a separate panel, bonded to the frame with adhesive. It doesn’t run the phone, but four things sit behind it or depend on it:

The batteryIt sits just under the back panelThe charging coilA flat coil inside the glass, on models with wireless chargingThe camera areaThe lenses and their cover glass sit in or beside the panelThe sealThe adhesive around the edge helps keep water and dust out

Not every phone has all four. Samsung says the Galaxy A series, XCover series and Galaxy Tab don’t charge wirelessly, and Google lists the Pixel 3a, 4a, 5a and 6a without it. Some models have a plastic back instead of glass. A crack in either is still a break in the cover, and the same checks apply.

02 / What’s different now

What changes once the back is cracked

  • Don’t count on water resistance. Samsung says a Galaxy’s water resistance isn’t permanent to begin with, and that physical damage such as chips and cracks can significantly reduce its ability to resist dust and water. Google says the same about Pixel: dust and water resistance are diminished or lost through wear, repair or damage, and dropping the phone may be enough. Its water-damage advice starts with avoiding chips or cracks in the phone’s body.
  • The glass can cut. Cracked glass has sharp edges and can shed slivers into a pocket or a hand.
  • The crack can travel. Another drop, a tight pocket or a twist puts new stress on glass that’s already broken, and a crack can run on toward the glass over the camera. No one can tell you how soon, so don’t plan around it holding.
  • The hit may have done more than crack glass. Samsung says a drop or strong impact is what cracks the display or back glass, and recommends an inspection and repair at a service center. The checks below are how you look for the rest yourself.
03 / Five checks

Five things to check before you put a case on it

Do these once, in good light, with the case off. Look, don’t press.

1Loose glass2Lifting or bulging3Wireless charging4Camera glass5Heat
  1. Loose glass. Tilt the back under a bright light and look for raised chips or missing pieces. Don’t run a finger or a cloth across it to find out.
  2. Lifting or bulging. Look along each edge for a gap between the glass and the frame, and check that the back is still flat. Samsung’s trade-in terms describe a swollen battery as the screen or case warping around the battery and bulging around the center. Google’s Pixel 7a battery program lists a bulging back cover, and gaps or openings along the edges, as signs of swelling. A cracked back that’s also raised is a battery problem first.
  3. Wireless charging, if your model has it and no glass is loose. Set the bare phone in the center of a charger you know works. Samsung says poor alignment can make a Galaxy charge slowly, stop charging or overheat, and that there should be no debris on the back of the phone or the charger.
  4. Camera glass. Check whether any crack reaches the glass over a lens or the flash. If it does, the camera section comes before anything else.
  5. Heat. Notice whether the phone gets hot sitting idle or on an ordinary charge. Samsung says to stop using a device immediately if it or its battery begins to overheat, even while not in use.
04 / One result, one next step

Let the result decide what happens next

A flat crack, nothing loose, everything works
Back it up, put it in a case, keep it dry and book the repair. This is the least urgent result on the list.
Chips are loose or pieces are missing
Cover the area with clear tape, then a case, and get it in sooner. Open glass lets dust and moisture straight in.
The crack reaches the camera glass
Tape over that glass and don’t wipe it. See the next section.
Wireless charging stopped, or the back gets hot on the pad
Use a cable instead and mention it in the quote request. Samsung’s own steps end in a service visit when alignment, the case and an update don’t fix it.
The back is lifting, bulging or showing a gap
Stop using it and unplug it. Read screen lifting or swollen battery before you do anything else.
It runs hot when idle, or smells
Stop using it. Google says a damaged phone may overheat, and Samsung says to stop using a device immediately if it emits smoke or you smell something burning.
05 / Tape it, don’t wipe it

If the crack reaches the camera glass

The glass over each lens is a cover as well, with the camera’s own lens right behind it. When it cracks, the instinct is to wipe it clean. Don’t.

Tape over cracked camera glass, and don’t wipe it.Wiping can push glass dust and grit through the crack onto the camera’s own lens underneath, and debris on that inner lens can’t be cleaned off. A small piece of clear tape keeps the rest out until it’s repaired.

Then take one photo with each lens and see whether they’re still sharp. The cracked camera lens guide is written for iPhone, but its lens-by-lens test works on any phone. If a Galaxy shows a “Camera failed” message instead, that’s a different problem.

06 / Until it’s fixed

Sensible stopgaps: a case, clear tape and staying dry

None of this is a manufacturer’s instruction. Google’s position is not to use a damaged phone at all. This is ordinary repair sense for the days before a repair:

  • Back it up. To your Samsung or Google account, then check from another device that recent photos are there. Back up before a repair covers what to check.
  • A snug case. It holds a flat crack together and keeps the glass off your hands. Put it on once and leave it on.
  • Clear tape over loose shards. Lay a strip of clear packing tape flat over the broken area before the case goes on. Don’t press it down hard, and tell the shop it’s there.
  • Keep it dry. No rain, steamy bathrooms or ski pockets full of melting snow. A case doesn’t seal anything. If it does get wet, Google’s steps for a Pixel are to turn it off, dry it with a soft cloth and let it finish drying on a flat surface at room temperature. Phone got wet? What to do first has the full list, and a Galaxy that then shows a water-drop warning has its own guide.
07 / Leave the panel on

What not to do: peeling, heat guns and glue

A back that’s lifting or bulging is a battery warning.

Unplug it, stop using it and don’t push the back down. Google says not to press on the cover glass or back cover to push a swollen battery back into place, and not to pry the phone open. Read screen lifting or swollen battery.

  • Don’t peel the glass off. A broken panel comes away in sharp pieces, and the battery is directly underneath. Prying toward it is how a battery gets bent or punctured. Samsung’s battery warning is blunt: do not disassemble, crush, puncture or heat batteries.
  • No heat gun or hair dryer. Softening the adhesive is a bench step done with controlled heat. At home it’s heat aimed straight at a battery. Google’s Pixel guide says not to expose the phone to temperatures above 113°F (45°C).
  • No glue, resin or nail polish. None of them makes cracked glass whole or restores the seal. They can run into the camera and microphone openings, and they make the old panel harder to remove cleanly.
  • Don’t wireless charge a back that’s shedding glass or one that gets unusually hot on the pad.
08 / What the fix is

What the repair is: a new back panel

On Galaxy and Pixel phones the back glass is its own part. The repair is to take the broken panel off, clear out the old adhesive and glass, look over what’s underneath, and bond a new panel on with fresh adhesive. I replace cracked back glass on Samsung and Pixel phones.

With the panel off, more of what the drop did can be seen. Say up front if the frame looks bent, a camera is acting up or wireless charging stopped, because those change the job.

Don’t expect the factory water rating back from a repair. Google says dust and water resistance are diminished or lost through device repair and disassembly, and Samsung says water and dust resistance may be compromised when a phone is repaired outside its authorized service. A new panel closes the phone up again. It doesn’t make it a phone to take swimming.

09 / Send the evidence, not a guess

What to send for an accurate quote

  • The exact model, from Settings > About phone.
  • A photo of the whole back, a close one of the worst area, and one along the edge that shows whether the back sits flat.
  • Whether the crack reaches the camera glass, and whether photos from each lens are still sharp.
  • Whether wireless charging still works, if your model has it, and any heat.
  • Any damage to the screen or frame, and any earlier repairs.
