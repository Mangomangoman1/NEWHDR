Won’t charge?
Port or battery.

Samuel, Owner & Technician · November 27, 2026

If it charges on a wireless pad but not on a cable, or the charging sign flickers when the plug moves, suspect the port. If it charges but dies early, shuts off with charge left, or only runs while plugged in, suspect the battery.

Both problems look like “it won’t charge,” and they’re different repairs. Rule out the cable and charger first, then watch what the phone does on a cable, on a wireless pad, and off the charger. Don’t buy a battery to test the theory: a new battery can’t charge through a blocked or worn port, and cleaning a port won’t bring back a worn-out battery.

For an iPhone or Android phone that won’t charge, or won’t hold a charge. If it charges but slowly, start with phone charging slowly. If it warns about liquid, stop and read Liquid Detected or Moisture detected first. If the screen or back is lifting, stop charging it and read swollen battery.

01 / Read the sign

Plug it in and watch what it does

Use a wall outlet and a cable and charger you trust. Plug in, set the phone down, and leave it alone for a minute. What it does next falls into one of three patterns, and each one points a different way.

  • Nothing at all. No charging symbol, no sound, no vibration, no screen. A completely flat battery can do this for a while, so give it the full hour in step 03 before you decide anything.
  • The sign comes and goes. The charging symbol flickers, or the connect sound repeats, especially when the phone or cable moves. That’s a connection problem: the cable end or the port.
  • It charges, but won’t hold it. The percentage climbs, then falls fast once it’s unplugged, or the phone shuts off with charge still showing. That’s the battery’s side of the line.
No signComes and goesCharges, won’t hold

If the screen wakes up at all when you plug in, even just to a low-battery picture, power is getting through the port. That alone makes a dead port less likely.

02 / Cheap checks first

Rule out the cable and charger first

  1. Use a wall outlet. Not a laptop, car port, hub or power strip. Apple’s own checklist has you check the outlet and every connection.
  2. Swap one thing at a time. Try a cable and charger you know work, ideally ones that charge another phone with the same connector. Look the old cable over for fraying, bent prongs or a scorched tip, and stop using it if you find any.
  3. Take the case off. A thick or shifted case can stop the plug short of a full seat, which looks exactly like a bad port.
  4. Look into the port, don’t dig. Turn the phone off and shine a flashlight in from the outside. Pocket lint packs into the back of the port one layer at a time, until the plug clicks in without reaching its contacts. Don’t put anything inside it. The loose-cable guide covers what’s safe.
  5. Read any message. A liquid or moisture warning means stop charging and dry it first. Follow that guide, not this one.

If a different cable or charger fixes it, the phone was fine. Everything below is for a phone that still won’t charge on gear you trust.

03 / One safe reset

Give a flat phone the full hour

A battery that ran all the way down can sit dark on a charger for a while before it shows any sign of life. This is Apple’s sequence for an iPhone that won’t charge, and it’s a fair test for any phone:

1

Leave it on a known-good wall charger for half an hour.

2

If the screen is still dark or shows only a battery picture, force a restart. On iPhone 8 and later: press and release volume up, press and release volume down, then hold the side button until the Apple logo appears.

On Android, hold the power button to restart. If it’s frozen, use your model’s forced restart.

3

Charge for another half hour.

Stuck at 80% isn’t a fault.Newer iPhones can hold charging at 80% to slow battery wear, and pause above 80% when the battery is warm. Pixels and Galaxy phones have similar protection settings. Check those before you call it a charging problem.

If it still won’t start after the second half hour, don’t keep plugging and unplugging it. Move on to the tests.

04 / The deciding tests

Four tests that split port from battery

Each test takes a few minutes and needs nothing you don’t already have. Note the result of each one: together they usually say which part is at fault.

A

The wireless pad

If your phone supports wireless charging, put it on a pad for a few minutes.

Charges on the pad, not on any good cable: the battery is taking charge, so the cable path is the suspect, meaning the port, lint inside it, or the port’s connection to the board.

No charge either way: the port is less likely to be the only problem.

Charging on a pad proves the battery accepts a charge. It doesn’t prove the battery is healthy.

B

The computer

Plug the phone into a computer with a cable that carries data. An iPhone should ask whether to trust the computer or show up in Finder or iTunes. An Android phone should offer USB options.

It connects and stays connected: the plug is seated and the port is making contact.

It connects and drops as you touch the cable: that’s the port or the cable end.

C

Off the charger

Charge it for a while, unplug it, and use it normally.

Runs fine on the charger, dies soon after unplugging: the port did its job. The battery can’t hold or deliver the charge.

Drops in big jumps, or shuts off with charge showing, especially in the cold: same answer, the battery.

D

The battery’s own report

iPhone: Settings > Battery > Battery Health. A service message means the battery has degraded significantly. “Performance management applied” means it already shut down because the battery couldn’t deliver enough power.

Pixel 6 and later: Settings > Device health & support shows battery health.

Samsung Galaxy: Samsung Members > Support > Get Started, then Battery status.

A good reading doesn’t clear the port, and a bad one doesn’t clear it either. A phone can need both. More on reading it in battery health at 80%.

05 / One result, one next step

Let the result decide what happens next

A different cable or charger fixed it
It was the accessory. Retire it. No repair needed.
Charges on a wireless pad, not on any good cable
Port side: packed lint, worn contacts, or the port’s connection. A bench cleaning comes first. The port gets replaced only if the contacts are worn or damaged.
The charging sign flickers, or it only works at one angle
Port or cable end. Stop propping the cable, which wears the port faster, and read cable loose or charging at an angle.
Charges normally, but dies early or shuts off with charge left
Battery. Check the battery’s own report to confirm, then see iPhone battery replacement.
Runs only while it’s plugged in
The battery, or its connection inside the phone. The port is doing its job.
Nothing by cable or wireless after the full hour
Not a simple port job. A battery that’s failed or run down too far and a fault in the charging circuit look alike here. Stop power cycling it and get it on a bench.
The plug gets hot, it smells, or the screen is lifting
Unplug it and stop. Read swollen battery before anything else.
06 / Look, don’t lever

Signs it’s the port, signs it’s the battery

Port signs

  • The plug stops short, feels gritty, or wobbles.
  • Lint packed at the back of the port under a flashlight.
  • Green or white crust on the contacts.
  • Charging only works at one angle, or cuts out when the phone moves.
  • It started after a drop, or after water, snow or a spill.

Battery signs

  • The percentage falls fast, or jumps around.
  • It shuts off with charge still showing, worse in the cold.
  • It only runs while it’s plugged in.
  • Battery Health shows a service message, or the Galaxy battery check flags it.
  • The screen or back is lifting. Stop and see swollen battery.
Don’t dig, don’t force, don’t guess with parts.

Pins, needles, toothpicks, compressed air and sprays can bend the port’s contacts or push lint deeper. If it won’t come out with a gentle look and a known-good cable, leave it for a bench cleaning under magnification.

Propping the cable at an angle to get a charge strains the same contacts that are failing. Charge on a pad if the phone supports it, and back up while it still powers on.

When it’s neither

Sometimes the port is clean, the cable is good, and a healthy battery still won’t charge. Then the charging circuit on the board is next. That’s board-level micro-soldering, which is what this bench does. Liquid and drops make it more likely, so mention either one up front.

07 / Send the evidence, not a guess

What to send for an accurate quote

  • The exact phone model.
  • Which pattern you see: nothing, a sign that comes and goes, or charging that won’t hold.
  • The cables and chargers you tried.
  • The wireless pad and computer results, if you ran them.
  • The battery health reading or Galaxy battery status.
  • Any drop, water, or earlier repair.
  • A clear photo of the port with a flashlight, with nothing inserted.
