PS5 controller · DualSense · Pairing

PS5 controller
won’t connect?

Samuel, Owner & Technician · November 21, 2026

Turn the PS5 on, plug the controller in with a USB cable that carries data, and press the PS button. If the light bar blinks and the player indicator lights up, it’s paired. If not, reset the controller once and try another cable and port.

When you press the PS button, a DualSense goes back to the last device it was connected to. After it’s been used on a PC, a phone or another console, it may be trying to reach that instead, and Sony’s way back to the PS5 is a USB cable and the PS button. A charge-only cable can’t do that. If it pairs by cable but drops out wirelessly, look at the battery and the space between it and the console. If it never pairs on a good cable after a reset, the controller has a fault.

For a DualSense or DualSense Edge that won’t pair, won’t stay connected, or only works on a cable. If it shows no light at all and won’t charge, or a stick moves on its own, the guides linked below fit better.

01 / Which problem

Not pairing, dropping out, or not charging?

“Won’t connect” covers a few different problems, and they don’t share a fix. Pick the one that matches:

Blinks, then goes offNever shows upDrops out mid-gameOnly works on a cable
  • It won’t pair at all, or the light bar blinks and then goes dark: start with pairing by cable.
  • It pairs, then disconnects on its own: go to if it pairs but drops out.
  • No light at all, even plugged in: that’s a charging problem first. A flat controller can’t pair. Read PS5 controller not charging.
02 / The main fix

Pair it with a USB cable and the PS button

This is Sony’s pairing method, and it’s the first thing to try. Sony’s steps:

1PS5 on, cable plugged in2Press the PS button3Light bar blinks, player indicator lights
  1. Make sure the PS5 is switched on.
  2. Connect the controller to the console with a USB cable.
  3. Press the PS button on the controller.
  4. Watch for the light bar to blink and then the player indicator to light up. That means it’s paired.
  5. Once it has enough charge, unplug the cable and use it wirelessly.

Why it stopped connecting. Sony says pressing the PS button on a controller that’s off connects it to the device it was last connected to. So a controller used on a friend’s PS5, a PC or a phone will try to go back there. Sony’s way to reconnect it to your PS5 is the cable and the PS button. A DualSense can also hold up to four pairings in slots, and Sony says that when all four are in use, the one you haven’t connected to for the longest time gets replaced.

Try another port. Sony’s USB ports guide says DualSense, DualSense Edge and other PlayStation controllers work with the PS5’s USB ports, front and back. If one port does nothing, try another before blaming the controller.

If another controller already works on the console, Sony also describes pairing wirelessly: with the working controller, go to Settings > Accessories > General > Bluetooth Accessories, make sure the new controller’s player indicator is off, hold its create button and PS button together until its light bar blinks, and select it on screen.

03 / Cable check

Use a cable that carries data, not a charge-only one

A lot of USB-C cables only carry power. They can charge a controller, but the console can’t talk to the controller through them, so the pairing step can’t work.

“Charge and sync”“Charging and data”“Charge only”
  • Use the cable that came with the PS5 or the controller if you still have it. That’s what Sony’s steps assume.
  • Otherwise, check the label. In its charging troubleshooting, Sony says to use a cable sold as “charge and sync” or “charging and data,” and that cables marked “charge only” can’t be used.
  • Plug straight into the console. Leave hubs, extensions and front-of-TV adapters out of the test.
  • Look at the ends. Sony’s manual says to wipe dust or debris off the connectors with a dry cloth before connecting. Don’t put anything into the port.
04 / One reset

Reset the controller once, then pair it again

If a good cable and the PS button still don’t pair it, Sony’s next step is a reset. For the standard DualSense:

  1. Turn off the PS5.
  2. Unplug the USB cable from the controller.
  3. Find the reset button inside the small hole next to the Sony logo on the back.
  4. Press and hold it with a pin or similar tool for at least 5 seconds.
  5. Turn the PS5 back on, connect the controller with a USB cable, and press the PS button.

The DualSense Edge has a reset button on the back too. Sony says resetting either one won’t change its pairing settings. Sony also says it won’t reconfigure the controller.

Once is enough.If a reset and a good cable don’t pair it, resetting it again won’t either. Move on to the next checks.
05 / Software

Once it connects, update the PS5 and the controller

Sony’s troubleshooting asks you to update the PS5’s system software, and its support pages show how to update the controller too. Controller updates come with PS5 system software updates. To check by hand, go to Settings > Accessories > Controller (General) > DualSense Wireless Controller Device Software. An Edge has its own entry under Settings > Accessories > DualSense Edge Wireless Controller. Sony also offers a PlayStation Accessories app that updates the controller from a PC.

Keep it on the cable while it updates, and don’t turn anything off part-way through.

06 / Random disconnects

If it pairs but drops out

A controller that pairs fine by cable and then disconnects on its own isn’t a pairing problem anymore. Check these in order:

  • Battery. Press the PS button, open Accessories in the Control Center and check the level. A controller that runs out of charge will drop out. If it won’t hold a charge, see PS5 controller not charging.
  • Distance and what’s in the way. Sony’s PS5 safety guide says the console’s Bluetooth uses the 2.4 GHz range, which other devices share, and that interference from them can shorten the signal range or end the connection unexpectedly. So the test is simple: sit closer, with less between you and the console, and see whether it still drops.
  • Other wireless gear nearby. If it only drops in one room or at certain times, try it with other wireless devices near the console moved away or switched off.
  • Another controller. If a second controller drops out in the same spot, look at the setup. If only this one drops, look at the controller.

07 / Other devices

Pairing with a PC, Mac or phone, and back

Sony lists Windows 10 (64-bit) and Windows 11, macOS Big Sur 11.3, iOS and iPadOS 14.5, tvOS 14.5 and Android 12, or later, as supporting the DualSense over Bluetooth and USB. To pair it with one of those:

  1. Make sure the player indicator is off. If it’s on, hold the PS button until it turns off, and unplug any USB cable.
  2. Hold the create button, then press and hold the PS button until the light bar blinks.
  3. Turn on Bluetooth on the device and pick the controller from the list.

To bring it back to the PS5, Sony’s answer is the same as above: connect it with a USB cable and press the PS button. So a controller that “stopped working” after a night on a laptop may simply be paired somewhere else.

08 / When it’s the controller

When it’s the controller, not the setup

It never pairs, on a good data cable, in two ports, after a reset
And a second controller pairs fine on the same cable and port. That points at this controller: its USB-C port, the connection behind it, or its board. Sony’s own path from here is PlayStation Repairs.
It only works while plugged in
Check the charge first. If the battery is fine and it still disconnects the moment you unplug it, the controller’s wireless side or its power has a fault, and it needs a proper look.
The port feels loose, or it only connects at an angle
That points at wear or damage at the controller’s USB-C port. Stop wiggling the cable; it makes a loose port worse.
It started after a drop
A fall onto a plugged-in cable is hard on the port. If it also won’t charge, the charging guide applies too.
It got wet
Stop using it and don’t charge it. Sony’s manual says not to let liquid get into the controller, and to stop using it if it behaves abnormally, makes unusual sounds or smells, or gets too hot to touch.
Inside the first year? Start with Sony.

Sony’s limited warranty for PS5 accessories covers defects in materials and workmanship for one year from purchase, for the original buyer, with proof of purchase, and that warranty text is for the United States and Canada. Sony’s troubleshooting page sends “Pairing issue” and “Controller disconnects” to PlayStation Repairs. Sony’s manual also says never to disassemble the controller, so ask Sony first, before anyone, including me, opens it.

09 / Send the evidence, not a guess

What to send for an accurate quote

  • Which controller: a standard DualSense or an Edge, and which PS5 it’s paired to.
  • What the light bar does when you plug it in and press the PS button.
  • What you tried: which cables, which ports, the reset, and whether a second controller pairs.
  • Whether it charges, and whether it was dropped or got wet.
  • Roughly when you bought it. If it’s inside the first year, check Sony’s warranty first.
