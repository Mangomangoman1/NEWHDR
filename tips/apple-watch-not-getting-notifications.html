Apple Watch + iPhone · Follow one fresh alert

Apple Watch missing
iPhone notifications?

Hailey Device Repair · Reviewed November 1, 2026

Test with the iPhone locked and the Watch worn and unlocked.

By default, an alert normally goes to the iPhone while you are using it, or to the unlocked Apple Watch when the iPhone is locked. Ask for one fresh test message under the second condition, then check the Watch’s Notification Center. A notification that arrived silently needs different checks from one that never arrived.

For ordinary app notifications on an Apple Watch paired to its owner’s iPhone. Uses a nearby-phone comparison before changing network settings. Family-managed watches, standalone cellular use and alarms have different settings or behavior.

01 / One fresh message per comparison

Check whether the alert went where it should

Wear the Watch comfortably, unlock it with its passcode if needed, and keep the paired iPhone nearby. Ask a trusted person for two short, harmless messages. Leave the conversation closed on both devices and keep the Watch on its watch face, so you are comparing notifications rather than an open chat.

  1. First message: leave the iPhone unlocked on its Home Screen. Note which device alerts.
  2. Second, new message: lock the iPhone and leave its screen asleep. Keep the Watch worn and unlocked. Note the screen, sound and wrist tap separately.
  3. On the Watch face, swipe down to inspect Notification Center. Do not clear the list or dismiss the message on another device before checking it.

Apple explains the default iPhone-versus-Watch destination: an unlocked iPhone normally receives the notification; a locked/asleep iPhone passes it to the Watch unless the Watch is locked. Reading or dismissing a notification on the Watch also dismisses it from the iPhone. An old, already-dismissed message is therefore a poor retest.

If the item is present on the Watch but you did not notice it, go to the sound/tap checks. A summary with hidden message contents can also reflect notification privacy settings; tap to view it privately rather than assuming delivery failed. Apple describes notification display choices.

02 / Establish the nearby baseline

Reconnect before changing app preferences

Open Watch Control Center. On watchOS 10 and later, press the side button. On watchOS 9 or earlier, touch and hold the bottom of the screen, then swipe up. Apple documents both gestures in its connection guide.

  1. Check for a red iPhone or red X, then bring the Watch and paired iPhone close together. Apple uses these icons as connection warnings; a green iPhone icon indicates reconnection.
  2. On the iPhone, check that Airplane Mode is off and Wi-Fi and Bluetooth are on. In Watch Control Center, check that its Airplane Mode is off too.
  3. Repeat the locked-iPhone message test after reconnecting. Record whether the connection icon changed and whether the fresh alert appeared.

These follow Apple’s nearby reconnection checks. A watch running on Wi-Fi or cellular is using a different path from this nearby comparison. If the problem occurs only away from the iPhone, use Apple’s separate guidance for using Watch without the iPhone nearby and first establish the nearby baseline.

03 / Allow one controlled comparison

Check the active Focus on both devices

Look in Control Center on the Watch and iPhone for an active Focus, such as Do Not Disturb, Sleep or Work. If appropriate, turn that active Focus off briefly for the test, then restore the setting you want. Do not delete schedules or disable all notification controls.

On iPhone, Settings → Focus lets you inspect the relevant Focus and its permitted people/apps. Apple notes that Share Across Devices can apply a Focus across linked devices, so checking only one screen may miss the setting that mattered. See Apple’s Focus and notification explanation.

If alerts return during the comparison, adjust that Focus’s allowed people/apps or schedule deliberately. The result points to filtering in that test; it does not establish a failed Watch speaker or radio.

04 / Delivery and alerting are different options

Review the app in the iPhone’s Watch app

On the paired iPhone, open Watch → My Watch → Notifications, then select the affected app. Some apps use Mirror my iPhone; others offer Custom controls. Check the app’s own iPhone notification permission too: Settings → Notifications → the app.

Allow Notifications
The app can appear in the Watch’s Notification Center and alert you, subject to the other settings being checked.
Send to Notification Center
The notification is delivered to the list without the normal Watch alert. Finding it there silently can be expected.
Notifications Off
That app’s Watch notifications are disabled.

Apple’s per-app notification guide explains these choices. Options differ by app; review the actual selection rather than expecting every app to show all three.

It stopped after I muted an alert on the Watch. How do I undo that?

Watch notifications can offer Mute 1 hour, Mute for Today or a turn-off option. For temporary muting, open Notification Center, swipe left on that app’s notification, choose More → Unmute if available.

If it was turned off, use the iPhone’s Watch → My Watch → Notifications, choose the app and restore its intended alert setting. Apple’s Watch notification instructions explain muting and re-enabling. Send a fresh test afterward; do not expect a dismissed notification to become a new alert.

05 / An arrived alert can be quiet

Check Silent Mode, Theater Mode and haptics

Silent Mode mutes sound while still allowing haptic notifications. Theater Mode also keeps the screen from waking when you raise your wrist, while haptic notifications can still arrive. Inspect these controls rather than treating a dark or silent screen as proof that nothing was delivered. Apple compares the modes in its silencing guide.

On the Watch, open Settings → Sounds & Haptics and find the haptic-alert control, labeled Haptics on current software. If you want wrist taps, make sure alerts are enabled and choose Default or Prominent where offered rather than Off. Use a snug, comfortable fit with the Watch back touching skin; do not tighten it painfully. Then repeat the same fresh-alert test. See Apple’s haptic and sound controls.

Why does my volume menu look different from another Watch?

Apple introduced automatic alert-volume adjustment with watchOS 26 on supported models. Its current user guide lists Apple Watch SE 3, Series 9, Ultra 2 and later for this feature. If present, Automatically Adjust Volume → Level offers a range such as Louder, Default or Quieter. Other setups use manual volume controls.

Check the controls actually offered by your model and software. The media-playback volume is a separate control from alert volume. Use Apple’s model-specific volume guide; an absent automatic-volume option alone does not indicate a fault.

06 / Keep the observation precise

Use the result to narrow the next check

The Watch alerts only after the iPhone is locked
That matches the normal destination rule in this comparison. Keep the settings you prefer rather than resetting the pair.
The item appears in Watch Notification Center without a sound or tap
Delivery reached the Watch. Review the app’s alert mode, Focus and sound/haptic settings before investigating connectivity.
Only one app fails; another app alerts correctly
Focus on the affected app’s permissions, mute choices and its own service. A working second app does not prove every Watch function is healthy.
The iPhone never receives the fresh test either
Investigate the phone/app/message-delivery problem first. The Watch cannot mirror an alert that this test did not establish on the phone.
The Watch stays disconnected or every tested app fails
Keep the icon, software versions and test results for Apple support. Stop repeatedly changing unrelated settings.

These are diagnostic leads, not component diagnoses. Restore temporary Focus or privacy changes once the comparison is complete.

07 / Keep pairing and data intact

Restart normally, then stop before unpairing

If controlled checks still fail, save work, know both device passcodes and finish any update first. Take the Watch off its charger and use Apple’s normal restart steps for it and the paired iPhone, then repeat one fresh message. A responsive Watch does not need repeated forced restarts.

Unpairing erases the Apple Watch and restores factory settings.

This checklist stops before unpairing or Erase All Content and Settings. Know the Apple Account access and agree on a backup/recovery plan before either action. Apple explains the erase and Activation Lock consequences.

Apple says a nearby paired Watch backs up to its iPhone, and unpairing through that iPhone creates a current Watch backup. Backing up the iPhone includes its Watch data. To preserve Health and Fitness data, Apple specifies iCloud or an encrypted computer backup. Review what Watch backup includes and excludes; do not assume a direct erase creates the same recovery path.

Contact Apple if the Watch remains disconnected, cannot unlock normally or continues missing ordinary alerts after the comparisons. For a single app, involve its support or the device administrator first. Bring both models/software versions, the app, lock states, connection icon, active Focus and which alert parts were missing. If the paired iPhone has a hardware problem, iPhone assessment information is available; notification behavior alone does not identify a part to replace.
