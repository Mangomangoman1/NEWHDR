Samsung Galaxy · Charging blocked · Water-drop icon

“Moisture detected”
on a dry Samsung?

Samuel, Owner & Technician · November 22, 2026

Unplug the charger, shake the phone gently with the port facing down, and let it air dry. Samsung says water evaporates from the port in about 1 to 2 hours. If the warning stays on a phone that’s dry, try a different cable, a drier room, a restart and Samsung’s USB-cache step, in that order.

Samsung says a Galaxy won’t charge while moisture is present in the charging port, and that the block is there to prevent corrosion. The warning tells you the port’s sensor tripped. It doesn’t tell you what tripped it: water, a damp cable, a steamy room and debris in the port can all do it. So the job is to dry it properly once, then rule those out one at a time. If the warning never clears on a dry phone with a cable you trust, the port itself needs to be looked at.

For a Samsung Galaxy that shows “Moisture detected” or a water-drop icon and won’t charge by cable, with a short note for Google Pixel. If there’s no warning and it just won’t charge, or the phone was soaked, the guides linked below fit better.

01 / What the warning is

What “Moisture detected” means on a Galaxy

Whether yours says “Moisture detected” or only shows a water-drop icon, it’s the same protection. The exact wording varies by model. Samsung explains it this way:

  • Charging is blocked on purpose. Samsung says its devices will not charge if moisture is present in the charging port when a charger is plugged in, as a preventative measure against corrosion.
  • There’s a sensor in the port. Samsung says Galaxy devices rated IP68 and above have a moisture detection sensor, and that charging stops when it detects moisture in the USB port.
  • It isn’t only about water. One version of the pop-up, as Samsung quotes it, reads “We’ve detected moisture or foreign material in your charging/USB port.”
  • A phone that never got wet can show it. Samsung says you may get the error even if the phone did not come into contact with water, and that high humidity can trigger it.

That last point is why the steps below go further than drying.

02 / The first ten minutes

What to do first, in order

  1. Unplug the charger. Samsung says an alarm sounds until you do. Then leave it unplugged: the warning text Samsung quotes says not to plug anything in until the port is clean, the moisture is gone and the notification has cleared.
  2. Wipe the outside with a dry, soft cloth, and take off a damp case.
  3. Shake it gently, port facing down. Samsung says 5 to 10 times, and warns that excessive force may damage the phone.
  4. Let moving air do the rest. Samsung says to place the phone on a cloth in a well-ventilated area, or dry it with a fan.
  5. Power it off while it dries, if you can. Samsung’s instruction for the water-drop icon is to power off the device and leave it to dry completely.
Something other than clean water?For its water-resistant devices, Samsung says a port that took in any liquid other than fresh water must be rinsed in fresh water and dried with a soft cloth. Don’t rinse a phone with cracked glass, an earlier repair or no water-resistance rating. For a drink, pool water or salt water, follow phone got wet? what to do first.
03 / Only Samsung’s numbers

How long does “Moisture detected” take to go away?

Samsung gives two figures. Its US support page says water will naturally evaporate from the charging port within approximately 1 to 2 hours. The phone’s own warning text, as Samsung quotes it, says drying “could take several hours.”

So wait a couple of hours before the first retest, with a dry cable. If the warning comes straight back, unplug and give it several more. Samsung says a repeat alert can mean there’s still moisture inside the port.

On a Google Pixel the message is “Liquid or debris in USB port.” Google says to turn a wet phone off and let it dry at room temperature. Once the port is clean and dry, it says to wait a few minutes for “OK to use USB port,” and to contact Google if the warning shows for two hours or more.

04 / One change at a time

The phone is dry and the warning won’t go away

Once it has had its drying time, stop drying and start testing. Change one thing at a time so you know which one mattered. Every check here is on Samsung’s page for this warning; the order is cheapest first.

1Cable2Room3Restart4Update5USB cache
  1. Try a different cable. Samsung says moisture may be present in the cable, and that the cable can be what triggers the icon. Use one that’s been kept somewhere dry.
  2. Try a drier room. Samsung says that in an area of high condensation, such as a kitchen or bathroom, latent moisture may be causing the error. A cold phone carried into a warm room can collect condensation the same way.
  3. Restart the phone. Samsung’s wording: if your charging port is completely dry, restart your phone.
  4. Update the software. Samsung asks for this before its other steps: Settings > Software update > Download and install.
  5. Clear the USB cache. This is Samsung’s step for an icon that stays when the device is “completely dry and free of moisture.”

The USB-cache steps, as Samsung words them

  1. From Settings, navigate to Apps, then tap the Filter and sort icon.
  2. Move the switch to the right to enable Show system apps, then tap OK.
  3. Scroll to USB Settings and tap Storage.
  4. Tap Clear cache located on the bottom right-hand corner of the screen to clear your USB cache.

Samsung notes that menus may vary by model and software version, and says this “should resolve your system error.” It clears one system app’s cache. It isn’t a reset, and it doesn’t dry anything.

Google gives Pixel owners a similar last step, for the “USB Settings” or “Android System” app, and only once the port is clean and dry.

05 / Look, don’t dig

Lint, debris and corrosion in the port

The warning covers “foreign material” too, and Samsung says a foreign object stuck in the port or the cable can make the phone shut off charging. Power the phone off and shine a bright light straight in.

Loose lint or grit
Google’s Pixel advice is to hold the port facing down and tap the phone gently against your hand. Samsung’s pages differ: its charging tips say to remove a foreign object with a soft brush, while its moisture page says not to insert any object into the port. Reading both carefully: tap it out, brush across the opening at most, push nothing inside.
Lint packed into the bottom
Leave it. Digging can bend the contacts on the thin tab in the middle of a USB-C port.
Green, white or dark crust on the contacts
That’s corrosion or residue, and it doesn’t dry out. Samsung says a corroded USB port needs a service center.

Corrosion doesn’t need a recent soaking. It can follow an earlier exposure that seemed to end fine: rain, a damp pocket, a splash. And a port can look clean from the outside when the contacts further in aren’t.

06 / Samsung’s stopgap

Use wireless charging in the meantime

If your Galaxy supports wireless charging, Samsung says you can still charge that way: wireless charging is not affected by moisture or foreign materials in the charging port.

  • Dry the phone first. Samsung says to thoroughly dry the device before placing it on the wireless charger.
  • Center it on the pad with nothing in between. Samsung says a Galaxy Fold or Z Flip should be closed, with its back to the charger.

It keeps the phone running while you sort the port out. It doesn’t dry the port or clear the warning.

07 / What makes it worse

What not to do

  • Don’t charge past the warning. Tricks that force a cable to charge don’t dry anything, and Samsung says charging with moisture can lead to USB port corrosion and damage to the device.
  • Don’t use heat. Samsung says not to dry the device with an external heat source. A fan is fine, and so is a dryer on cool air only, per the warning text Samsung quotes.
  • Don’t put anything in the port. No pin, toothpick, cotton swab or twisted tissue. Samsung says inserting objects can damage it.
  • Skip the rice. It isn’t in Samsung’s steps or Google’s. Both describe air and time.
  • Don’t spray cleaner or alcohol into it. That’s more liquid in a port that’s already reporting liquid.
  • Don’t jump to a factory reset. None of the Samsung pages listed below asks for one for this warning, and it erases the phone.
A hot port or cable is a different problem.

Samsung says a damaged USB cable or USB port may overheat, and to replace the cable or have the phone repaired. If the plug or the bottom of the phone gets hot, unplug it and stop testing.

08 / One result, one next step

Let the result decide what happens next

The warning clearsIt was moisture, the cable or the room.
It never clearsDry phone, good cable: the port needs service.
It clears after drying and stays gone
It was moisture. Charge as normal.
It clears with a different cable
The first cable was the trigger. Stop using it.
It only shows up in the bathroom or kitchen, or right after coming in from the cold
That points to humidity or condensation. Charge somewhere drier, and let a cold phone warm up first.
It clears after the restart or the USB-cache step
Samsung calls what the cache step resolves a system error. If the warning keeps returning on a dry phone, treat it as the next line.
It never clears: dry phone, dry room, a cable you trust, restart and cache done
Stop there. This isn’t moisture you can wait out. The port, or the moisture sensing in it, needs service. Debris you can’t see and corrosion from an earlier exposure are both possible, and the warning alone doesn’t prove which. Samsung’s own last step is to request service.
The phone was soaked, or something else changed too
A foggy camera, muffled sound, heat or a screen acting up means liquid got further than the port. Go to phone got wet? what to do first, or screen black after water if the screen is out.
09 / Send the evidence, not a guess

What to send for an accurate quote

  • The exact model. It’s in Settings under About phone.
  • A screenshot or photo of the warning, so the exact wording is clear.
  • Any water, rain, steam, pool or drink it’s been near, including weeks ago.
  • How long it has dried, and which cables and rooms you tried.
  • Whether wireless charging works, and whether you did the USB-cache step.
  • A clear, well-lit photo of the port, and any earlier repairs.
