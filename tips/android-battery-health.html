Android battery · Samsung and Pixel · Read the status

Android battery
health: is yours bad?

Samuel, Owner & Technician · November 23, 2026

On a Samsung Galaxy, open Samsung Members, tap Support, then Phone diagnostics, and read Battery status: Normal, Weak or Bad. On a Google Pixel 8a or later, go to Settings, Battery, Battery health, which reads Normal or Reduced.

Google puts a number on it: Reduced means the battery holds less than 80% of what it did when new, and Google recommends replacing a Pixel battery at that point. Samsung’s support pages give the three words without a percentage. Either way the word is an estimate. How the phone behaves tells you more than the label, and swelling overrides both.

For finding and reading the battery health check on a Samsung Galaxy, Google Pixel or other Android phone, and deciding whether the battery is worn. For an iPhone, see iPhone battery health at 80%.

01 / Samsung Members

How to check battery health on a Samsung Galaxy

Samsung keeps the battery check in its diagnostics. There are two ways in:

  1. Samsung Members: open the app, tap Support, then Phone diagnostics, then Battery status. Samsung says the first run starts a full scan, which you can stop to pick a single test.
  2. Settings: Device care, Diagnostics, Phone diagnostics. Samsung says this route is on Galaxy devices running Android 12 or later. Menu names vary by model and software version.

The result is one of three words. Samsung’s US pages don’t spell them out. Its support pages for India and the Caribbean do:

Normal“No performance issues with the battery.”Weak“The battery efficiency may have decreased due to long-term use or environmental conditions.”Bad“The battery life has significantly degraded.” Samsung says to visit a support center.

That’s all a Galaxy tells you here. None of the Samsung pages reviewed for this guide gives a capacity percentage, a cycle count or a number at which the battery should be replaced. Samsung describes the diagnostic as general information about the battery’s status, and says the phone may also show a pop-up when the battery needs a check-up.

02 / Depends on the model

How to check battery health on a Google Pixel

It depends on the model. Google says the Battery health screen is only on Pixel 8a and later, and not on the Pixel Tablet.

Pixel 8a and laterSettings › Battery › Battery healthStatus, plus Battery capacity. Cycle count is under About phone › Battery information.Pixel 6a through Pixel 8 ProNo battery health statusGoogle lists the 6a, 7, 7 Pro, 7a, 8 and 8 Pro as not showing one.Pixel 6 and laterSettings › Battery › Battery DiagnosticsGoogle’s check for drain, sudden shutoffs and whether the battery needs replacing.

Google’s statuses are Normal (capacity within normal limits), Reduced (capacity below 80%) and Unavailable (the phone can’t determine it). On a Pixel that shows the status, Battery capacity gives the estimate as a percentage.

Google is specific about replacement. It recommends a new battery when capacity is under 80% or when the battery reaches its rated cycle count: about 800 cycles for Pixel 3 through Pixel 8 Pro and the Pixel Fold, and about 1,000 for Pixel 8a and later. A cycle is a full battery’s worth of use, not one plug-in. Google’s example: 100% down to 50% and back up counts as half a cycle.

03 / An estimate, not a deadline

What the reading means, and what it doesn’t

  • It’s an estimate. Google says capacity is estimated by the battery’s fuel gauge, and that early readings are based on lab results and may differ from your actual battery. It also says a Pixel needs to charge fully at every 10th cycle to keep the reading accurate.
  • It’s wear, not a fault. Google and Samsung both call the battery a consumable part. Its capacity falls with use, charging and temperature.
  • The 80% figure isn’t universal. The 80% most people quote is Apple’s figure for iPhones, covered in iPhone battery health at 80%. Google uses the same figure for Pixels. Samsung’s pages don’t give one, so a Galaxy’s “Weak” can’t be turned into a percentage.
  • An older Pixel runs a little shorter on purpose. Google says Battery health assistance, on Pixel 6a and later, lowers the battery’s maximum voltage in stages from 200 to 1,000 charge cycles, and that you may notice small decreases in runtime. On Pixel 9a and later it can’t be turned off.
04 / Behavior over labels

Signs that matter more than the word on the screen

Samsung’s own list of battery decline is about behavior, not a number: a drastic drop in battery life, unexpected shutdowns, and a phone that dies while the screen still shows plenty of charge. Samsung says this happens more in cold weather, and that games and the camera can feel slower.

  • Fast drain. Google’s test for a Pixel: charge to 100%, close your apps, unplug it and leave it idle overnight. A drop of more than 10% by morning means something is using power, and Google’s next step is Battery usage, not a new battery.
  • Shutdowns in the cold. Samsung says cold environments can temporarily reduce battery performance. Why your phone dies in the cold separates the two.
  • Heat. A phone that’s hot while idle or charging is its own problem: phone running hot.
  • Slow or patchy charging. That can be the cable, charger or port: phone charging slowly.
Swelling is a stop sign, whatever the status says.

If the screen is lifting or the back is bulging, stop charging the phone. Google says a swollen battery can lift the screen, and that an exposed battery is easier to damage and can overheat. It tells Pixel owners not to press on the glass or back cover and not to pry the phone open. Read screen lifting or swollen battery before anything else.

05 / Charge limits

Settings that slow battery wear

Samsung Galaxy: Settings, Battery, Battery protection. Samsung describes three modes:

  • Basic stops charging at 100% and resumes when the battery falls to 95%.
  • Adaptive stops at 80% once it has learned your routine, then switches to Basic before you wake up.
  • Maximum stops at the level you set. Samsung says the lower the level, the more you extend the battery’s life.

Samsung’s UK support page says these three modes are on One UI 6.1 or later, and that older software has a single option that limits charging to 85%.

Google Pixel: Settings, Battery, Battery health, Charging optimization. Adaptive Charging finishes the charge right before you usually unplug, and Google says it needs about 14 days to learn your habits. Limit to 80% is on Pixel 6a and later, and Google says it suits you if you usually end the day with charge left.

Two different 80s.An 80% charge limit is how full you let the battery get each day. 80% battery health is how much it can still hold. Turning on a limit doesn’t change the health reading.

Heat matters too. Samsung gives 32° to 95°F as the optimal usage temperature for a Galaxy, and says continuous use or charging in extreme temperatures may speed up battery deterioration. It also says to unplug the charger once the phone reaches 100%, to avoid a swollen battery.

06 / Motorola, OnePlus and the rest

Battery health on other Android phones

Other makers put battery information in their own menus, and not every phone shows a health reading. The Google help pages reviewed for this guide describe battery health for Pixels only, so check the maker’s support site for your model. The signs above apply to any phone with a lithium-ion battery.

07 / One result, one next step

New battery, or new phone?

Normal, and it lasts your day
Nothing to fix. Turn on a charge limit if you like and leave it alone.
Normal, but it drains fast
Look at battery usage by app, signal and recent updates before the battery.
Weak or Reduced, and the day is getting short
A new battery is reasonable if the rest of the phone is sound. For a Pixel at Reduced, Google recommends it outright.
Bad, or it shuts off with charge showing
Samsung says to have it checked. A worn battery can do that, and so can a charging or board fault, so it’s worth a diagnosis before buying a part.
Swelling, lifting, heat or a smell
Stop using and charging it. That isn’t a battery-health decision. Read screen lifting or swollen battery.

Google says replacing an aged Pixel battery increases runtime, improves performance in cold or warm temperatures and may prolong the phone’s useful life. Whether that’s worth doing comes down to the rest of the phone: the screen, the charging port, and whether the model still gets software updates. Repair or replace walks through that decision.

08 / Send the evidence, not a guess

What to send for an accurate quote

  • The exact model, and roughly how old it is.
  • The status word. On a Pixel 8a or later, the capacity percentage and cycle count too.
  • How long a charge lasts now, and any shutdowns.
  • Any heat, swelling or lifting, and any earlier battery replacement.
