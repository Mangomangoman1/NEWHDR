PS5 overheating? The fan roar and the dust problem.

By Samuel — owner & technician, Hailey Device Repair · Updated June 13, 2026

A PS5 that screams, runs hot, or shuts off mid-game is almost always choking on dust — not dying. Here's what's normal, what you can clear yourself, and when it needs to come apart.

The bench answer: Most PS5 overheating is a dust-clogged heatsink with the fan working overtime to compensate — made worse by a closed cabinet or aging thermal paste. Blow out the vents, leave a few inches of clearance, and keep it out of an enclosed shelf. If it still throws the “temperature is too high” warning or shuts down mid-game, it needs an internal clean and likely a repaste or liquid-metal service.

A PS5 pulls a lot of power and makes a lot of heat, and it moves that heat with a single big fan over a large heatsink. That system works well — until dust packs the heatsink fins and the fan has to roar to push air through a clogged radiator. Almost everything people call “overheating” starts there.

The good news: it's usually maintenance, not failure. Here's how to tell the difference, what's safe to do at home, and where the bench comes in.

Why PS5s overheat

  • Dust in the heatsink (by far the most common). Over a year or two, dust mats onto the fins and chokes airflow. Temperatures climb and the fan spins up to compensate — that's the roar people notice first.
  • Blocked airflow. A closed entertainment cabinet, a console pushed against a wall, or vents facing carpet all trap the hot air the PS5 is trying to expel.
  • Aging thermal paste or liquid metal. The material moving heat from the chip to the heatsink degrades over years. The launch consoles use liquid metal, which can occasionally shift or “pump out” and raise temperatures.
  • A worn fan. A fan bearing going bad changes the sound from a smooth whoosh to a grind or rattle — and a struggling fan moves less air.
  • A hot room. Ambient temperature stacks on top of everything; a warm, poorly ventilated room makes every other factor worse.

What's normal, and what's a warning

The PS5 is allowed to be loud sometimes. The pattern is what matters:

Normal

Fan ramps up during a demanding game and settles down after; warm air from the rear vents; a smooth whoosh under load.

Worth acting on

A constant roar even during menus or light games; a grinding or rattling fan; very hot exhaust; the on-screen “temperature is too high” message; shutdowns mid-game.

The temperature warning and mid-game shutdowns are the console protecting itself. If you're seeing either, don't keep pushing through it — that's the point where a cleaning or service prevents a bigger failure.

What you can safely do

  1. Give it room to breathe. A few inches of clearance on all sides, vents unobstructed, and out of any closed cabinet. Vertical or horizontal are both fine — clearance matters more than orientation.
  2. Blow out the vents. With the console off and unplugged, use short bursts of canned air across the vents and the rear ports to clear surface dust. Don't open the console if it's still under warranty.
  3. Cool the room. Better airflow and a few degrees cooler in the room genuinely helps a borderline console.
  4. Keep the system software updated. Sony has shipped fan-behavior tuning in updates; staying current can help.
Don't vacuum the inside, and don't use liquid. A vacuum can throw static into the board, and moisture and electronics don't mix. Short bursts of canned air on the outside vents only — anything deeper means opening it properly on a bench.

When it needs the bench

Hand it over if cleaning the vents didn't fix it, the temperature warning keeps appearing, the fan grinds or rattles, or it shuts down mid-game. On the bench, this is usually:

  • A full teardown deep-clean of the heatsink and fan — far beyond what canned air reaches from outside.
  • Fresh thermal paste, or a liquid-metal service on consoles that need it.
  • A fan replacement if the bearing is worn.

It's routine work, often same-day, and a lot cheaper than replacing a console that just needed cleaning.

What it costs at my bench: a full deep-clean with fresh thermal paste is $130 (or $85 added on when the console's already open for another repair); a PS5 liquid-metal service is $165. Either way it usually turns fan roar back into a whisper. And if the real problem turns out to be HDMI or no signal rather than heat, a port replacement is $140.

FAQ

Why is my PS5 fan so loud?

Dust in the heatsink and aging thermal material make it run hotter, so the fan works harder. A smooth loud whoosh under a demanding game is normal; a constant roar during light use, or a grinding or rattling sound, is not. A full internal cleaning — often with fresh thermal paste — quiets most loud PS5s.

Is it bad to stand the PS5 vertically?

No — vertical is fine and supported by Sony. The old liquid-metal worry was overstated for normal use. Clearance around the vents and staying out of a closed cabinet matter far more than orientation.

Will cleaning the dust fix it?

Often, yes — a clogged heatsink is the most common cause. Blowing out the vents helps; a full internal clean is more thorough. If it still overheats afterward, the thermal paste or liquid metal likely needs servicing.

Does a PS5 need new thermal paste?

Over several years the original paste can dry out or the factory liquid metal can shift, raising temperatures. If it still overheats after a thorough cleaning, a repaste or liquid-metal service usually fixes it — delicate work best done on a bench.

Heat check

Roar, then quiet?

A fan that ramps up under load and settles down is normal. A constant roar, a grind, or the temperature warning is the console asking for a clean.
