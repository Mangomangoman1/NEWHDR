MacBook Pro 2016–2017 · Light bars at the bottom?

MacBook Pro screen
lit like a stage?

Samuel, Owner & Technician · November 3, 2026

On a 2016 or 2017 MacBook Pro, that pattern points to a worn backlight cable.

A thin flex cable feeds the backlight and is pulled around a small board every time the lid opens. As it wears, the bottom edge lights up in uneven bright bars, the “stage light” effect. Later the backlight cuts out once the lid opens past about 40 degrees. A screenshot and an external display confirm it in a couple of minutes. Back up now, open the lid less, and don’t try to coax it back by flexing the screen.

For a 2016 or 2017 13-inch or 15-inch MacBook Pro with bright bars along the bottom edge, or a backlight that goes dark as the lid opens wider. A cracked screen, lines that show up in screenshots, or a Mac that won’t start is a different problem.

01 / Light, not picture

Recognise the stage light pattern

The name comes from what it looks like: a row of bright fans rising from the bottom edge of the screen with darker gaps between them, like footlights along the front of a stage. It usually starts faint and gets more obvious over weeks or months.

  • The bars sit along the bottom edge, behind whatever is on screen, and stay put when you move windows.
  • They grow, shrink or disappear as you move the lid.
  • Everything else looks normal: text is sharp, colours are right and the glass isn’t cracked.
  • Later on, the whole screen goes dark past a certain lid angle and lights up again when you close it a little.

Coloured lines across the picture, flickering blocks, a dark patch from a pressure point or a cracked corner are different faults. The checks below tell them apart without opening anything.

Have a 2018 or later model? iFixit measured a backlight cable about 2 mm longer in 2018 MacBook Pros, which gives it more slack around the board. The checks below still work, but the cause is less certain on those models, so lean on the results rather than the name.

03 / Two-minute proof

Rule out the image side first

Stage light is a backlight problem. The picture itself is fine; the light behind it is uneven. These three checks prove that without opening anything or changing a setting.

  1. Take a screenshot. Press Shift-Command-3, then view the file on your phone or another screen. A screenshot captures the picture, not the light behind it, so stage light bars never appear in one. If the bars or lines show up in the screenshot, this isn’t the backlight cable.
  2. Connect an external display. Use a USB-C or Thunderbolt adapter to a TV or monitor. A clean, even picture there means macOS and the graphics are fine and the fault sits in the laptop’s own screen. If the external display stays black too, start with the external monitor guide instead.
  3. If the screen is already dark, use a flashlight. Shine a phone light across the screen at an angle. A faint picture means the panel is still drawing the image and only the backlight is missing.

Nothing here stresses the cable, so repeat these as often as you like.

04 / One slow pass

Run the lid-angle test once

This shows how far the wear has gone. Do it once, slowly, with the MacBook flat on a table and plugged in.

1

Open a blank white page so the bottom edge is easy to read, then close the lid to about a third open, where the screen is fully lit.

2

Open the lid slowly in one smooth movement and watch the bottom edge.

Note where bars appear and whether the screen goes dark.

3

At the first change, stop and close it slightly to your usual working angle. Don’t rock it back and forth to find the exact spot.

Every open and close flexes the worn cable again.One slow pass is the test. Rocking the lid to bring the light back is what finishes the cable.

iFixit reported the backlight eventually giving out once the lid opens past about 40 degrees. Yours may change at a different angle. What matters is that the change follows the lid, not what’s on screen.

06 / Look, don’t lever

Why the cable fails, and what not to do

In these MacBook Pros, thin flex cables run from the display, wrap over a small display board near the hinge, and connect to the Mac. iFixit traced stage light to the backlight cable. Each time the lid opens, the cable is pulled taut around that board, and after a year or two of normal use it wears and tears. iFixit and MacRumors both report that the backlight cable usually fails first, and that the cables are built into the display assembly. That’s why Apple’s fix is a whole new display.

Don’t press the bottom bezel, flex the screen or open the display.

Pressing or twisting can bring the light back for a moment. It feels like a fix, but it’s the same wear that caused the problem.

The cables you’d be trying to save sit in the hinge area, and there’s nothing behind the bezel you can service yourself. Prying it open can tear the second cable, the one that carries the picture.

Until it’s repaired:

  • Back up now with Time Machine or iCloud. If the picture cable goes next, getting files off becomes harder. See backing up before a repair.
  • Use an external display for long sessions and keep the lid at a low, steady angle.
  • Open and close it as little as possible. Each cycle counts.
Will Apple still fix this for free?

In May 2019 Apple started a 13-inch MacBook Pro Display Backlight Service Program for the two 2016 13-inch models, later extended to five years from first sale. Apple’s program page now says it has ended, and 15-inch and 2017 models were never included. A repair through Apple today is a paid display replacement unless other coverage applies. Apple lists its current service programs if you want to check.

07 / Send the evidence, not a guess

What to send for an accurate quote

  • The model name from About This Mac, or the serial number.
  • The screenshot and external display results.
  • The lid angle where it changes, and whether it goes fully dark.
  • A photo of the bottom edge, straight on, in a dim room.
  • Any drops or liquid, and whether the display has been replaced before.

A full display assembly is the standard repair. Depending on the panel, a repair at the cable may also be possible, and the answers above help me tell which applies before you drive or ship anything. Text them to 208-450-1606, or see MacBook screen and display repair. Outside the Wood River Valley? Statewide mail-in works for MacBooks too.
