Xbox Series X|S · Console on, TV blank?

Xbox on, but the TV
says no signal?

Hailey Device Repair · Reviewed November 2, 2026

Test the path outside the console, then boot low-resolution mode once.

A power light and startup sound prove the Xbox is running, not that video is leaving it. Pick the TV input by hand, connect straight to the TV with a known-good HDMI cable, then try a second input or display. Still blank? Use the two-beep reset to start the console at 640 × 480. A picture there points to a setting or cable. Blank everywhere points to the HDMI port or the chip behind it.

For an Xbox Series X, Series S or Series X Digital that powers on while the TV shows no signal or a black screen. A console that won’t power on at all, or a black screen inside one game, is a different problem.

01 / A light is not a picture

Confirm the Xbox is actually running

Before changing anything, collect the three clues the console gives you without a screen:

  • The light behind the Xbox button on the console stays on instead of flashing once and going dark.
  • You hear the startup sound through the TV or the console.
  • A controller’s Xbox button stops blinking and stays lit, which means it found the console.

If all three happen, the Xbox is booting and the fault sits somewhere between its video output and your TV. If the light never comes on, or flashes and dies, this guide doesn’t fit. That’s a power fault, and repeated power attempts won’t tell you more.

02 / Cheapest suspects first

Rule out the TV, the input and the cable

  1. Pick the input by hand. Use the TV remote’s Input or Source button to select the exact HDMI port the Xbox is plugged into. Auto-switching and HDMI-CEC don’t always wake the right one.
  2. Go direct. Remove soundbars, AV receivers, HDMI switches, splitters and capture cards. Plug the Xbox straight into the TV.
  3. Swap to one known-good cable. Use the cable that came with the console if you still have it, or one that is working on another device right now. A long or older cable can fail at the bandwidth the Xbox asks for even when it’s fine for a streaming stick.
  4. Try a second HDMI port, then a second display. Many TVs offer their fastest HDMI modes on only one or two ports. Another port, TV or monitor separates a one-input problem from a console problem.

Change one thing at a time and give the TV a few seconds to settle on each input. If another console works through the same cable and input but the Xbox doesn’t, the fault has followed the Xbox.

03 / A real restart, once

Do one full power cycle

Waking from sleep isn’t a restart. Hold the Xbox button on the front of the console for about 10 seconds until it shuts down completely. Unplug the console’s power cord, and the TV’s if you can reach it, and wait a full minute. Reconnect, turn the TV on first and select its input, then press the Xbox button.

One power cycle is a test. Ten in a row is not more evidence.If nothing changed, move to the display reset below instead of repeating it.
04 / The two-beep reset

Boot the Xbox in low-resolution mode

If your TV can’t show the resolution or refresh rate the Xbox is set to, the screen stays black even though nothing is broken. This startup forces 640 × 480 for one boot so you can reach the settings again.

1

Remove any disc, then hold the Xbox button for about 10 seconds so the console is fully off.

2

Press and hold the Xbox button together with Eject, or Pair on models without a disc drive. Keep holding through the first beep.

3

Let go after the second beep, roughly 10 seconds later.

Letting go at the first beep just starts the console normally.

If a picture appears, open the guide and go to Profile & system → Settings → General → TV & display options. Choose a resolution your TV supports, then raise refresh rate and 4K or 120 Hz features one at a time, checking the picture after each. Xbox explains those display settings.

Picture works at 1080p but not at 4K or 120 Hz?

That usually points to bandwidth, not a broken port. Make sure the TV’s enhanced HDMI setting is turned on for that input. Brands name it differently, often something like enhanced format or deep colour. Then use the console’s included cable on the TV’s fastest port.

Xbox has a separate 4K and HDR troubleshooting page for the console side. If 1080p is rock solid, the HDMI port is very unlikely to be damaged.

05 / One result, one next step

Let the result decide what happens next

Picture in low-resolution mode, blank at your usual setting
The console and port are passing video. The TV, input or cable can’t handle the higher mode. Lower the setting, then sort out bandwidth.
Works on another TV or monitor, not on yours
The fault lives with that TV input or its settings. Try its other ports and its enhanced HDMI setting before suspecting the Xbox.
Picture flickers, shows coloured sparkles or drops out
Swap the cable first. If lifting or pressing the plug changes the picture, stop testing. That points to the port.
Sound but no picture
Usually a resolution or handshake problem, so low-resolution mode is the right test. If it still shows nothing, treat it like the row below.
Blank in low-resolution mode on two displays with a known-good cable
The problem is inside the console: the HDMI port, its solder joints or the video chip behind it. Stop here and get it diagnosed.
06 / Look, don’t lever

Signs the HDMI port or the chip behind it is damaged

Shine a light straight into the port labelled HDMI OUT on the back. Look only. Don’t probe it with metal, a pin or a toothpick.

  • The plug wobbles or sits crooked where it used to feel snug.
  • The plastic tongue in the middle is bent, pushed back, cracked or missing.
  • Pins look bent, flattened or out of line.
  • The picture comes back only while the cable is pressed up, down or sideways.
  • It stopped right after the console was moved, knocked over or the cable was yanked.

A port can also look perfect and still be the problem. The HDMI connector is soldered to the motherboard, and a small signal chip sits on the board between the processor and the port. A power surge, a storm or swapping cables between powered devices can damage that chip or the port’s solder joints without leaving a visible mark. Fixing either is board-level work: hot air, magnification, and a check of the tiny copper pads underneath before anything new goes on.

Don’t glue it, pry it or keep the cable pressed at an angle.

Side pressure can tear a loose port off its pads, turning a connector swap into a pad rebuild. Unplug the cable and leave it out until the console is looked at.

A factory reset can’t fix a physical connector, so don’t reset the console hoping to get a picture back.

Is this covered by Microsoft’s warranty?

Warranty terms are Microsoft’s to decide, and physical port damage is often treated differently from a factory fault. If the console is still within its warranty period, start with Xbox’s hardware support before anyone opens it.

07 / Send the evidence, not a guess

What to send for an accurate quote

  • The model: Series X, Series S or Series X Digital.
  • What the light, startup sound and controller do.
  • The low-resolution result, and how many cables and displays you tried.
  • Whether it followed a move, a drop, a yanked cable or a storm.
  • A straight, well-lit photo into the HDMI OUT port.

A clear port photo often tells me whether to start at the connector or go straight to board diagnosis. Text it to 208-450-1606 with the details, or see console repair and HDMI port pricing. Outside the Wood River Valley? Statewide mail-in works for consoles too.
