Home/Repair Library/White spot on phone screen

Phone screens · A bright patch that won’t wipe off

White spot on
your phone screen?

Samuel, Owner & Technician · November 13, 2026

A soft, cloudy white patch is usually a pressure mark in an LCD’s backlight. A tiny, sharp white dot is usually a stuck pixel.

An LCD lights the picture from behind with thin layers that spread the light evenly. Pressure from a pocket, a bag or something pushing from inside can disturb those layers, and the light pools into a brighter blotch that’s easiest to see on white. An OLED has no backlight, so the same kind of cloudy glow can’t come from one there. A screenshot, a quick check of your screen type and a full-screen color test tell you which you have in a few minutes. Don’t press on the spot to see what it does.

For a white or bright spot that stays in the same place on the screen while the picture changes around it. Cracked glass, colored lines, black ink-like patches or a white spot that only shows in your photos are covered by the guides linked below.

01 / Shape, size, background

Describe the spot before you test it

“White spot” covers a few different faults. Three details narrow it down before you change anything, and they’re the same details that make a quote accurate later.

  • Shape and edges. A soft, cloudy patch with blurry edges points to the light behind the picture. A pinpoint with a hard edge points to a single pixel.
  • Where it shows best. Note whether it stands out most on a white page, a gray one or a black one. A backlight patch is easiest to see on white or light gray.
  • Whether it stays put. Scroll, rotate the phone and open a different app. A fault in the screen keeps the same spot on the glass while everything else moves.

Also note when it started: after a drop, after the phone spent a day in a tight pocket or bag, after a screen repair, or with no obvious cause. That history matters more than the color of the spot.

02 / The free checks

Rule out the surface and the software

Some white spots sit on the glass, and some are part of the picture. These checks take a few minutes and don’t risk anything.

  1. Turn the screen off and look at it under a light. Dust, residue or a mark on the glass still shows on a dark screen. A backlight or pixel fault disappears when the screen is off.
  2. Wipe it with a dry microfiber cloth. Wipe gently, without pressing into one spot.
  3. Check the screen protector. A bubble, a speck of dust under it or a chip in it can look like a white spot. If you’re not sure which layer you’re looking at, see protector or cracked screen.
  4. Take the phone out of a tight case. A case that presses on the edge of the display can push on the layers behind the glass. Look again a few minutes after it’s off.

The screenshot test

A screenshot saves the picture your phone drew, not the light coming out of the screen. That makes it the cleanest way to split a screen fault from something on screen.

  1. With the spot visible, take a screenshot. On a current iPhone that’s the side button and volume up together. On most Android phones it’s power and volume down.
  2. Send it to another phone or a computer and look at it there. Don’t judge it on the same screen, because the spot would sit in front of it anyway.
  3. No spot in the copy means the phone drew a clean picture and the screen itself adds the spot. A spot in the copy means it’s part of the content: a wallpaper, an app overlay or an image.

Then restart the phone once. A screen fault comes back in the same place after a restart.

03 / LCD or OLED

Know which screen you’re looking at

This is the fork in the road, because the two screen types make white spots in different ways.

LCD

A backlight sits behind the image. Light from it passes through a light guide that spreads it across the screen, a diffuser that evens it out and a reflector at the back that sends stray light forward again. If pressure dents or shifts those layers, the light bunches up in one area and shows as a soft, brighter patch.

OLED

Each pixel makes its own light and there’s no backlight at all. A white spot on an OLED is far more likely to be a single stuck pixel, a faint leftover image from something shown for a long time, or damage from an impact, which tends to come with lines or black patches.

Apple lists the iPhone X, XS and XS Max, and the iPhone 11 Pro models and later (except the iPhone SE), as OLED. Apple’s specs list an LCD for the iPhone 11 and for the iPhone SE (2nd and 3rd generation). Many Android phones in every price range use either one.

Not sure? Open a pure black image full screen in a dark room with the brightness up. If black looks like the screen is off, it’s probably OLED. If it glows dark gray, it’s probably LCD. The LCD vs OLED guide explains the test and why the two screens are priced differently.

04 / One safe test

Do one full-screen color test

Samsung suggests checking a suspect pixel with solid-color images. It works on any phone and only takes a minute.

WhiteGrayBlackRedGreenBlue
  1. Find plain white, gray, black, red, green and blue images and open each one full screen, with brightness at the middle of the slider.
  2. For each color, note whether the spot is brighter, darker or the same as the screen around it.
  3. Photograph the spot on white and on black from straight on. Those two photos do most of the work in a quote.
Look at the spot, but don’t press on it.Pushing on the screen to see whether the spot moves is the same kind of pressure that makes a backlight mark in the first place.

On a Samsung Galaxy, Samsung also suggests restarting in Safe mode. If the spot disappears there, an app is drawing it, not the screen.

05 / One result, one next step

Let the result decide what happens next

The spot shows in the screenshot copy
It’s part of what’s on screen, not the screen. Check the wallpaper, any accessibility or screen overlay and the app it appears in.
It wipes off, moves with the protector or is gone with the screen off
It’s on the surface. Clean the glass or replace the protector.
Soft, cloudy patch on an LCD, brightest on white, same place every time
A backlight pressure mark. The layers behind the picture have been pushed out of shape. It won’t clear with a restart or an update. If it isn’t growing and nothing else is wrong, whether to repair it now is your call.
One sharp dot that stays white or one color on every background
A stuck pixel. Samsung describes stuck pixels as staying lit in one color because of an electrical fault. Watch whether more appear.
A faint shape on an OLED that matches something you leave on screen
Image persistence or burn-in. Apple says image persistence fades after a few minutes of normal use. Burn-in that stays after that is wear in the panel.
The spot is spreading, darkening or came with lines or black patches
The display is physically damaged. Back up the phone now and stop testing.
06 / Look, don’t lever

Signs it’s hardware, and what not to do

A white spot that passes the surface and screenshot checks is in the display assembly. Some causes start inside the phone, not outside it.

Stop using and charging the phone if the screen is lifting, the case is bulging or the spot came with heat.

A swollen battery can push the display up from behind and stress the layers in it. That’s a safety problem first and a screen problem second. Read screen lifting or swollen battery before anything else.

  • Don’t press, rub or flex the spot. If pressure caused it, more pressure won’t undo it.
  • Don’t open the phone to reseat anything. A spot that appeared right after a screen replacement can come from something pressing on the new display from behind, and that’s for whoever did the repair to look at.
  • Keep it out of tight pockets and the bottom of a packed bag. Outside pressure is one of the common causes.
  • Back up if anything else is off. Lines, flicker, touch problems or cracked glass alongside the spot mean the screen may get worse. See is a cracked screen safe to use and green or white line on an iPhone.

On an LCD the backlight is built into the display assembly, so the repair for a pressure mark is a new screen, not a fix to the spot itself. At HDR, screen repairs use complete display assemblies.

07 / Send the evidence, not a guess

What to send for an accurate quote

  • The exact model, from Settings → General → About on an iPhone or Settings → About phone on Android.
  • Your screenshot test result and whether you know it’s LCD or OLED.
  • Photos of the spot on a white screen and a black screen, straight on.
  • When it started, and any drop, pressure, heat, liquid or earlier screen repair.
  • Whether touch, Face ID or fingerprint unlock and the rest of the screen still work normally.
