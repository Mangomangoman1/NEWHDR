PS5 controller · DualSense · Stick drift

PS5 controller
drifting on its own?

Samuel, Owner & Technician · November 21, 2026

Put the controller down and watch. If the camera or character still moves, test it in another game, then reset the controller and update its software the way Sony describes. If it still drifts after that, the stick is worn, and a new stick is the fix that lasts.

A stick should report “centred” when you let go of it. A worn or dirty one reports a small push instead, and the game obeys it. A reset and an update rule out a software glitch in a few minutes. A dead zone setting can hide mild drift for a while. Neither one replaces worn parts. If the controller is less than a year old, start with Sony’s warranty before anyone opens it.

For a DualSense or DualSense Edge whose stick moves things when you aren’t touching it. If the controller won’t charge, won’t connect or stay paired, or it’s a Switch Joy-Con, the guides linked below fit better.

01 / What drift is

What stick drift is, and what it looks like

Drift is a stick sending input on its own. You let go, and something on screen keeps moving. It shows up in a few ways:

Camera turns by itselfCharacter walks offMenu selection jumpsAim pulls one way

Each analog stick sits on a small sensor assembly that reports where the stick is pointing. At rest it should report the centre. As the parts inside wear, or dirt works in under the cap, it can report a small push in one direction even when nobody is touching it. The game can’t tell the difference, so it moves.

That makes drift a wear problem, not something you did. Wear also doesn’t undo itself, which is why the fixes in this guide split into two kinds: things that rule out software or hide mild drift, and the one fix that replaces the worn part.

02 / Stick or game

Confirm it’s the stick, not the game

Some games have their own sensitivity and dead zone settings, and a strange setting or a bug can look like drift. Spend five minutes making sure it’s the controller:

  1. Hands off. Set the controller on a table so nothing touches the sticks, and watch for a minute.
  2. Try somewhere else. Open a second game, or move around the PS5’s own menus. If the selection jumps or the view creeps there too, it isn’t one game’s settings.
  3. Find the stick. In most games the left stick moves the character and the right stick moves the camera. Note which one is drifting and which way it pulls. That’s the most useful detail for a repair.
  4. Swap controllers. If you have a second controller, try it in the same game. If it sits still, the problem is in the first controller.

On a DualSense Edge, Sony says unexpected inputs can come from a game’s controller settings clashing with your Edge profile, so check those first. The Edge’s Stick Sensitivity/Deadzone screen also has a Playtest view that, in Sony’s words, lets you “see the sensitivity when you move the stick.” It’s there for tuning, but it’s also a handy place to watch a stick you’ve let go of.

03 / Safe first steps

Reset it and update it, once each

These are Sony’s own first steps for a controller that misbehaves. They take a few minutes, they’re safe, and they rule out a software problem before you spend anything.

Reset the controller. Sony’s steps for the standard DualSense:

  1. Turn off the PS5.
  2. Unplug the USB cable from the controller.
  3. Find the reset button inside the small hole next to the Sony logo on the back.
  4. Press and hold it with a pin or similar tool for at least 5 seconds.
  5. Connect the controller to the PS5 with a USB cable and press the PS button.

Sony says the reset won’t change your controller’s settings or its pairing. The Edge has a reset button on the back too, and Sony says resetting it won’t change pairing or your profiles.

Update the controller’s software. Sony says DualSense updates arrive with PS5 system software updates. To check by hand, go to Settings > Accessories > Controller (General) > DualSense Wireless Controller Device Software. For an Edge it’s Settings > Accessories > DualSense Edge Wireless Controller, then its Device Software entry. Sony also offers a PlayStation Accessories app for updating from a PC.

Once, not ten times.If a reset and an update were going to clear it, they will the first time. Repeating them won’t change a worn stick.
04 / Buy some time

Dead zone settings can hide mild drift

A dead zone is a small area around the centre where the game ignores the stick. Sony’s Edge guide puts it simply: with a dead zone of 10%, “you need to exceed a tilt of 10% before your game registers an input.” If the drift is smaller than the dead zone, the game stops seeing it.

Dead zone: ignoredSmall drift sits inside it

  • Standard DualSense: look in the game’s own controls or accessibility menu for a dead zone or stick sensitivity setting. Raise it a little at a time until the drift stops. Not every game has one.
  • DualSense Edge: Sony’s Edge profiles let you set a dead zone for each stick. Go to Settings > Accessories > DualSense Edge Wireless Controller > Custom Profiles, create a profile and choose Stick Sensitivity/Deadzone.

The trade-off is precision. A bigger dead zone means small, careful movements near the centre stop registering too. It’s a way to keep playing, not a repair, and if the wear keeps going, the dead zone you need keeps growing.

05 / What doesn’t last

What doesn’t last, and what not to do

  • Sony says to keep liquids out. Contact cleaner and alcohol under the stick are common internet advice. Sony’s manual says not to let liquid, small particles or other foreign objects get into the controller, and its cleaning advice stops at a soft, dry cloth on the outside. Cleaning can’t replace worn parts either way.
  • Don’t open a standard DualSense to look. Sony’s manual says never to disassemble or modify it. Inside there’s a battery and ribbon cables, and the sticks aren’t a snap-in part.
  • Don’t buy a new controller just yet. If it’s under a year old, read the box below first. If it’s older, a stick replacement is a normal repair, and worth asking about first.
Inside the first year? Start with Sony.

Sony’s limited warranty for PS5 accessories covers defects in materials and workmanship for one year from purchase, for the original buyer, with proof of purchase. Sony repairs or replaces the product, with a new or factory recertified one, at its option. You pay to ship it in; Sony pays the return shipping. It doesn’t apply to defects that come from abuse, unreasonable use, mistreatment or neglect, and that warranty text is for the United States and Canada. Sony’s troubleshooting page sends a “Button or analog stick issue” to PlayStation Repairs. Because Sony’s manual says never to disassemble the controller, ask Sony first, before anyone, including me, opens it.

06 / The real fix

The fix that lasts: a new stick

Drift that survives a reset and an update is a worn stick, and the lasting fix is replacing it. How that’s done depends on which controller you have:

DualSenseA bench job

The stick assembly is soldered to the controller’s main board. Replacing it means opening the controller, removing the old stick, fitting a new one and testing it.

DualSense EdgeA swap by design

Sony built the Edge with stick modules you can replace yourself. Replacement modules are sold separately.

For a standard DualSense, there are two kinds of replacement stick. A standard one is the same design as the original and can wear the same way again in time. A Hall-effect or TMR stick senses position with magnets instead of a contact that rubs, so it doesn’t wear in the same way. That’s what I fit for DualSense drift. See PS5 and DualSense repair.

For a DualSense Edge, Sony’s steps are:

  1. Make sure the player indicator is off. If it’s on, hold the PS button until it turns off. Unplug the USB cable.
  2. Slide the RELEASE latch on the back and remove the front cover.
  3. Raise the release lever, remove the old module, and slide the new one along the grooves until it clicks.
  4. Lower the lever, line up the cover and push it on until it clicks.

Sony’s Edge manual adds three points: touch a grounded metal object first to get rid of static, don’t touch the connectors on the module or inside the controller, and make sure the front cover is secure before you turn it on. Sony also says the left and right modules are interchangeable. That gives you a free test: swap them, and if the drift moves to the other side, it’s the module.

07 / Send the evidence, not a guess

What to send for an accurate quote

  • Which controller: a standard DualSense or an Edge.
  • Which stick drifts, which way it pulls, and whether it happens in every game.
  • What you tried: the reset, the update, a dead zone, and whether any of it helped.
  • Whether it was dropped or got wet, and whether it’s been opened before.
  • Roughly when you bought it. If it’s inside the first year, check Sony’s warranty first.
