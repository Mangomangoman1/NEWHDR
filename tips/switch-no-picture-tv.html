Nintendo Switch & OLED · Handheld works, TV is blank

Switch works handheld.
No picture on the TV?

Hailey Device Repair · Reviewed October 29, 2026

Start with the dock’s power and a direct HDMI path.

For an original Switch or OLED model, check the Nintendo HAC-002 power adapter, connect the dock straight to the TV, and select that HDMI input. Then compare one port, cable or dock at a time. A working handheld screen gives you a useful starting point; it does not identify a failed part.

For a responsive Nintendo Switch or Switch – OLED Model with a working handheld display. Switch Lite has no TV mode. Switch 2 uses different accessories; take the model branch below before unplugging anything.

01 / Similar names, different equipment

Identify the console before swapping accessories

Nintendo Switch or Switch – OLED Model
Use the original-family checklist below. Nintendo lists the HAC-007 dock and the LAN-equipped HEG-007 dock. OLED can use the older dock; original Switch can use the LAN dock with system software 11.0.0 or later. Keep the console updated. Nintendo’s OLED compatibility FAQ explains the combinations.
Nintendo Switch Lite
Stop this TV checklist. Lite does not support TV mode or any dock. An HDMI adapter cannot enable that missing mode. Continue playing on its built-in screen; a TV setting or console reset will not add TV support.
I have a Nintendo Switch 2. What changes?

Use Nintendo’s Switch 2 no-TV-picture instructions instead of the original-family sequence. Nintendo specifies the Switch 2 dock (BEE-005), NGN-01 AC adapter and an Ultra High Speed HDMI cable.

The original HAC-002 adapter can charge Switch 2 directly, but it cannot provide Switch 2 TV mode through the dock. Charging alone therefore does not prove that this accessory combination supports TV output. Do not force a console into another generation’s dock.

Read model labels on the accessories; dock color is not a reliable identifier. If the handheld display is also blank or the console will not start, use the Switch power guide first. This page assumes you can reach HOME and operate the console.

02 / Reset the adapter, not the console’s data

Establish a stable handheld starting point

Save the game and finish any update or download before disconnecting power. Note the TV’s message and the current cable arrangement. Stop if you find liquid exposure, a burnt smell, a frayed lead, a loose or visibly damaged connector, or unusual heat. Do not keep trying damaged equipment.

  1. Turn the TV off, lift the console out of the dock, and disconnect the dock’s cables.
  2. Unplug the Nintendo HAC-002 AC adapter from both ends—the outlet and dock/console—and leave it disconnected for at least 30 seconds. Unplugging only its USB-C end does not complete this adapter reset.
  3. Connect that adapter directly to the console and a wall outlet. Leave the console undocked. Wake it with POWER if needed and confirm that HOME is usable.

Nintendo describes this adapter reset in its TV connection / supplied-adapter error guide. A random USB-C charger is not the reference adapter for this test. If the console cannot display HOME here, stop the TV comparisons and follow the power guide.

With stable power and the console undocked, open System Settings → System → System Update and follow any offered update. If already current, save work, hold POWER for three seconds, then choose Power Options → Restart. This is a normal restart of a responsive console. Let it finish before reconnecting; do not interrupt an update or use a forced shutdown for this test.

03 / One TV, one HDMI cable

Rebuild a simple dock-to-TV connection

Leave out the HDMI switch, receiver, soundbar and video adapter for this comparison. Nintendo’s blank-TV checklist recommends a direct path. Temporarily losing sound through a receiver is expected while it is bypassed.

  1. With the TV off and console undocked, open the dock’s rear cover. Move the HAC-002 adapter from the console to the socket labeled AC ADAPTER; plug its other end directly into the wall outlet.
  2. Connect the dock’s HDMI OUT socket straight to a TV HDMI input. Note the number printed beside that TV socket—such as HDMI 2. Route cables without pinching them and close the cover.
  3. Remove the console’s protective case for this test. Turn the TV on and select the input matching the socket you used.
  4. Use the console’s already-paired controllers. With the console awake, lower it gently into the dock with its screen facing the same direction as the dock’s front panel. Do not push against resistance or wiggle it to force a connection.

Nintendo’s illustrated TV setup shows both dock layouts. The console’s own screen turning off when docked is normal. Look for the HOME screen on the selected TV input. If it appears, test briefly before restoring extra equipment one item at a time. If adding one device brings the failure back, record that exact connection for its manufacturer’s support.

If the handheld screen stays on, gently undock once and check alignment, the removed case and the external connections. Do not probe the USB-C socket, scrape contacts, spray cleaner inside or open the dock. A screen that stays on does not by itself prove a broken connector.

04 / Use the working handheld controls

Check output settings without erasing anything

Undock and open System Settings → TV Settings → TV Resolution. Note the existing choice, then try Automatic if it was fixed. If there is still no picture, try a lower resolution supported by the TV, such as 720p, and redock on the same input. Nintendo lists Automatic, 480p, 720p and 1080p for these models in its TV settings guide; use the TV manual to confirm supported signals.

If a lower setting works, keep that usable setting while investigating the TV/input/cable combination. Record the difference rather than declaring the dock repaired. Changing RGB range or screen-size calibration is a separate adjustment; it is not the first comparison for an entirely missing picture.

Does my dock need a firmware update?

In this original Switch/OLED family, only the LAN-equipped HEG-007 dock can be updated. The older HAC-007 dock does not gain an update by repeating this menu.

After updating the console, the menu is System Settings → System → Update Dock → Begin Update. Follow Nintendo’s dock firmware instructions and the console’s connection prompts. Allow the update to finish without disconnecting power or undocking.

If you cannot complete the prompts with the available display, skip this branch and tell support. Do not guess through an unseen menu, keep disconnecting during an update, or initialize the console because an option is unavailable.

05 / Keep the rest of the setup fixed

Let each comparison answer one question

Turn the TV off and undock before moving cables. Reselect the input afterward. Nintendo lists port, cable, TV and dock comparisons; the interpretations below are leads for your next check, not component diagnoses.

Another HDMI input works; same cable
Investigate the first input and its TV settings. Keep the working connection.
Another HDMI cable works; same input
Set the suspect cable aside. Record which change restored the picture.
The same console, dock and cable work on another TV
Check the first display’s supported signals and input configuration.
Another compatible dock works; everything else unchanged
The original dock needs attention. Swapping a whole dock-and-cable bundle cannot isolate one item.
No comparison equipment is available
Record that limitation. Assessment is reasonable without buying a test dock.

Use only undamaged equipment compatible with your console generation. Stop swapping once you have a stable, repeatable result.

06 / An indicator, not a diagnosis

A brief blink and constant flashing mean different things

Nintendo says the dock’s TV output LED briefly flashes, then goes off when the AC adapter is first connected. That alone is normal. During docked TV display, it should stay solid green. These observations come from Nintendo’s flashing dock LED guide; do not treat a brief startup blink as a failed dock.

If it keeps flashing with the console seated, complete the compatible-power, restart and applicable dock-update checks above. If another compatible dock is readily available, compare it. Persistent flashing after those checks is a stopping point, not a reason to bend contacts or repeatedly force the console down. Note whether the light was off, briefly blinking, solid or continuously flashing, and whether any TV image appeared.

07 / Preserve the playable console and its saves

Stop before a TV problem becomes data loss

Do not initialize the console to finish this checklist.

Initializing deletes console-memory data, including game saves and user information. Existing cloud backups have their own recovery conditions. Nintendo explains the data removed by initialization. Do not delete users or format a microSD card for a missing TV picture.

Stop sooner for damage, abnormal heat, unstable power, an update that will not complete or a connector that needs force. If the direct connection and available comparisons fail, contact Nintendo or arrange a console assessment. Nintendo’s no-image guidance calls for console-and-dock service if unresolved; when a second dock fixes it, the original dock is the service target.

Bring the console, dock and AC adapter, plus your notes: model labels, system version, TV model/input, resolution, exact message, LED behavior and each comparison result. Confirm what equipment the service provider needs and discuss save preservation before authorizing work. A playable handheld is a good reason to protect what works, not to promise a particular repair or data outcome.
