Phone screens · Water · The phone may still be alive

Screen went black
after water?

Samuel, Owner & Technician · November 17, 2026

Unplug it, don’t charge it, and get it in fast. If it still rings, vibrates or shows up on a computer, the phone is running and the screen is what died.

I get these jobs constantly. On iPhones, most of the time the phone itself is fine and it just needs a new display, as long as it wasn’t left sitting wet for a long time. Liquid can stop the screen from lighting while the rest of the phone keeps working. What changes that is time: the longer liquid sits inside, the more of the phone it can reach. So the plan isn’t to wait and see. It’s to stop putting power into it, run one quick test for signs of life, and bring it in.

For a phone whose screen went fully black after a spill, a drop in water or a rainy day. If it went black after a drop with no water involved, or the screen still shows a picture with lines or patches, the guides linked below fit better.

01 / Screen or whole phone

A black screen isn’t the same as a dead phone

When a phone goes black after getting wet, it looks dead. Often it isn’t. Two very different faults look identical from the outside:

  • The screen died and the phone is still running. Calls still come in, alarms still go off and texts still arrive. You just can’t see any of it.
  • The phone itself shut down. No sound, no vibration, nothing on a computer. That can be a drained battery, the charging port or the board.

Which one you have decides the repair, so it’s worth five minutes to find out. Also note what happened: what it got wet with (tap water, a pool, the lake, salt water, a drink), how long it was in, and how long it’s been since. Those details matter as much as the symptom.

A water-resistance rating doesn’t rule any of this out. Apple says splash, water and dust resistance aren’t permanent and can decrease with normal wear, and that liquid damage isn’t covered under warranty.

02 / The first minutes

Unplug it, then stop

The one thing that reliably makes this worse is putting power through a wet phone. Apple says charging with a wet connector can corrode the pins and cause permanent damage.

  1. Unplug it. Take it off any cable, charger or wireless pad, and don’t put it back on one.
  2. Take off the case and anything that traps water against the phone.
  3. Drain the port. Apple’s advice is to tap the phone gently against your hand with the charging port facing down, then leave it somewhere dry with some airflow.
  4. Leave the buttons alone. With a black screen you can’t see a power-off slider, so holding buttons to shut it down is guesswork.
Skip the restart loop.Apple’s steps for a black screen are a forced restart, then an hour on the charger. Those are written for a dry phone. On a wet one, the charger half is exactly what not to do.

03 / One safe test

Test for signs of life

This tells you whether the phone is running behind the black screen. The first two parts don’t need a cable at all.

  1. Call it from another phone. Listen for the ringtone and feel for vibration.
  2. Flip the Ring/Silent switch on an iPhone that has one. A short buzz means the phone heard you.
  3. Connect it to a computer, but only once the port is dry. On a Mac, the phone’s name shows up in the Finder sidebar. On a Windows PC, it shows up in the Apple Devices app, or in iTunes on older setups. Apple says to allow at least 5 hours after the phone gets wet before connecting a Lightning or USB-C cable.
RingsBuzzesShows on a computer

Any one of those means the phone is running. Don’t hold off bringing it in just to do the computer part. A ring or a buzz is enough to go on, and the shop can do the rest. On an Android phone, the call test works the same way.

04 / From the bench

Why the screen is usually what died

The display is its own part. It plugs into the main board with a connector and sits right under the glass, so liquid that gets inside can reach it or its connection early. When that happens the picture stops, but the board underneath can keep running, which is why a black phone can still ring.

That matches what comes across my bench. These are some of the most common jobs I see, and on iPhones it’s usually the display that failed, not the phone. A new display brings it back, as long as the phone wasn’t left sitting wet for a long time.

Brought in soonUsually the displayLeft sitting wetMore of the phone at risk

Time is the variable you control. A phone can keep working for a while and fail later as corrosion develops inside, and the longer it sits, the more likely the board needs work too. Then a new screen on its own may not be enough. That’s why “let it dry for a few days and see” is the wrong plan once the screen has already gone black.

05 / One result, one next step

Let the result decide what happens next

It rings or vibrates, and the screen stays black
The phone is running and the picture isn’t. On an iPhone that usually means the display took the water. Bring it in soon, so the inside gets checked while it’s still only the screen.
It shows up in Finder, the Apple Devices app or iTunes
Same answer, with one extra. If the photos matter and it’s a computer you’ve used with this phone before, a backup is worth trying while it’s connected. If it asks you to unlock the phone, don’t guess at the passcode on a screen you can’t see. Unplug it when you’re done.
A faint picture, lines, flicker, a tint or dark patches
The display is damaged but partly working. The same plan applies: stop charging it and bring it in.
The screen came back after it dried
Back it up right away. A phone that works after water can still have liquid or corrosion inside, so get it checked if it was more than a splash.
No ring, no buzz and nothing on a computer
There’s no way to tell the screen from the whole phone yet. It could be a drained battery, the charging port or the board. Don’t put it on a charger to find out. This one needs a bench, and if the photos matter, say so first.
Heat, swelling, a burning smell or a lifting screen
Stop. That’s a battery safety problem before it’s a screen problem. Keep it off the charger and away from anything flammable.
06 / Look, don’t lever

Signs to stop, and what not to do

Don’t charge it, even if it seems dry on the outside.

The outside dries long before the inside does. Apple’s rule is not to charge until the phone is completely dry, and a phone with a black screen after water is past the point where drying on the counter is the plan. If the case is bulging, the screen is lifting or it’s getting warm, read screen lifting or swollen battery first.

  • No heat and no compressed air. Apple says not to dry an iPhone with an external heat source or compressed air.
  • Nothing in the port. Apple says not to put a cotton swab or a paper towel into the connector.
  • No rice. Apple says small particles of rice can damage the phone. It also doesn’t reach the inside, where the problem is.
  • Don’t open it. Prying a wet phone open risks tearing the display cable and the seals, and turns a screen job into a bigger one.
  • Don’t reset or erase it. A factory reset won’t fix a dead display, and it can cost you the photos.
  • Tell the shop what it was. Salt water, pool water and sugary drinks leave more behind than tap water, which makes time matter even more.

If the screen is black but the phone never got wet, the drop-and-display guide phone screen black but still on covers the checks for that.

07 / Send the evidence, not a guess

What to send for an accurate quote

  • The exact model, if you know it. With a black screen you can find it on the original box, your carrier account or the receipt.
  • What it got wet with, roughly how long it was in, and when.
  • Your signs-of-life result: does it ring, buzz or show up on a computer?
  • Whether it’s been charged since it got wet.
  • Any heat, swelling, smell, cracked glass or an earlier screen repair.
  • Whether the photos and messages are backed up.
