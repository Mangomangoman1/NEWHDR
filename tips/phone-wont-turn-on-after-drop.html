Home/Repair Library/Phone won’t turn on after a drop

Phone power · After a drop · No signs of life

Dropped it, and now
it won’t turn on?

Samuel, Owner & Technician · November 21, 2026

Try one forced restart, then give it an hour on a charger you know works, and call it from another phone. If it still won’t ring, buzz or show a charging sign, stop there.

A phone that won’t do anything after a drop has one of a few problems: the battery happened to be flat, the phone isn’t taking a charge, the battery or its connection took the hit, or the board did. The first two you can rule out at home in about an hour. The rest need a bench, and more button presses won’t change that. Before any of it, make sure the phone is really off: one that still rings with a black screen is a different, usually simpler, problem.

For a phone that shows no signs of life at all after a drop: no ring, no vibration, no charging icon. If it rings or buzzes but the screen stays black, or it got wet, the guides linked below fit better.

01 / Is it really off

First, make sure it’s really off

A phone with a dead screen and a phone that’s dead look identical. The fix is completely different, so spend two minutes on this before anything else:

  • Call it from another phone. Listen for the ringtone and feel for a buzz.
  • Flip the Ring/Silent switch on an iPhone that has one. A short buzz means the phone is running.
  • Plug it into a charger and watch for anything: a battery icon, a lightning bolt, a light, or the phone getting slightly warm the way it normally does while charging.
No ringNo buzzNo charging sign

If it rings or buzzes, the phone is on and the screen is what failed. Stop here and read phone screen black but still on instead. That’s usually a display or display-connection job, not a power one. This guide is for when all three come back empty.

02 / One correct restart

Do one forced restart, the right way

Phones can freeze with the screen off, and a drop is a good moment for that to happen. A forced restart cuts through it. It doesn’t erase anything. The button sequence depends on the phone:

  • Any iPhone with Face ID, and iPhone SE (2nd generation and later): press and quickly release volume up, press and quickly release volume down, then press and hold the side button. Apple’s iPhone User Guide gives this one sequence for current models, and Apple says to keep holding until you see the Apple logo, which can take longer than 10 seconds.
  • iPhone 7: hold the side button and volume down together until the Apple logo appears.
  • iPhone 6s and earlier: hold the Home button and the side or top button together, about 10 seconds.
  • Samsung Galaxy: hold the side button and volume down together for at least 7 seconds, and let go when the Samsung logo appears and the phone vibrates.
  • Google Pixel: hold the power button. Google’s own restart check holds it for about 35 seconds, then waits about 2 minutes.
Once, not ten times.Do it correctly, once, and wait. If the phone was going to come back from a freeze, that’s when it does. Mashing the buttons afterwards doesn’t add anything.

If a button itself is stuck or dead, the sequence can’t work. Phone button not working after a drop covers that.

03 / Power before panic

Give it an hour on a charger you trust

A phone that was low before the drop can simply be flat, and a completely flat battery can look exactly like a broken phone. All three manufacturers say the same thing in slightly different words: charge it, and give it time.

  1. Use a wall charger and cable you know work. Test them on another phone first. A computer USB port or a frayed cable proves nothing.
  2. Leave it alone for an hour. Apple says to charge an iPhone for an hour and then try again. Samsung says a fully drained Galaxy may not show the charging icon at all and to keep charging anyway. Google says a fully drained Pixel can show no icon, no vibration and nothing on the screen for up to 25 minutes after it’s plugged in.
  3. Try the forced restart again while it’s still plugged in.
  4. Swap one thing at a time. If nothing happens, try a different cable, then a different charger. Changing everything at once tells you nothing about which part failed.
0 minPlugged in, nothing25 minA flat Pixel may still look dead60 minTry the restart again

Look at the port, don’t dig in it. Shine a light into the charging port. Pocket lint packed into the bottom can stop a cable seating fully. If you see debris, a bent pin or anything green or crusty, leave it for the bench. Metal tools in the port can short it.

04 / What a drop can break

What a drop can break when there’s no sign of life

If an hour on a good charger and a correct restart bring nothing back, the phone has a power fault, and the drop is the obvious suspect. These are the parts that can be involved:

The charging port or its connection
The port takes the cable’s leverage every day, and a fall onto a plugged-in cable is hard on it. If the phone can’t take a charge, it will look dead once the battery runs out, even though nothing else is wrong. On most phones it’s a separate, replaceable part.
The battery or its connector
The battery plugs into the board through a small connector, and the battery itself can be damaged by a hard corner hit. A battery that was hit hard is also the one to watch for heat or swelling.
The board
A hard landing can crack or knock loose something on the main board. It’s the least predictable outcome and the reason a phone that shows nothing at all needs a proper diagnosis rather than a guess at one part.

A lot depends on the drop, and on what “completely dead” really means. When a dropped phone shows no sign of life at all, it’s usually the board that took damage. Not always, though. Sometimes it’s one of the parts around it, like the battery or the charging assembly, and sometimes a phone that looks completely dead only needs a new screen.

Those can be told apart on a bench with the right tools: whether the phone draws power when it’s plugged in, and how much. That’s why a phone that’s truly dead after a drop is worth a diagnosis before you decide it’s gone.

05 / One result, one next step

Let the result decide what happens next

A charging icon or a light shows up
It was flat, or close to it. Leave it charging for a while before you try to turn it on, then check it charges normally over the next day. If it only charges at certain cable angles, read cable loose or only works at an angle.
It rings or buzzes now, but the screen stays black
The phone is alive and the display took the drop. Go to phone screen black but still on.
It shows the logo, then restarts or goes dark again
That’s a different problem: the phone powers up but can’t finish starting. On an iPhone, see iPhone stuck on the Apple logo. Don’t erase it to fix this until the data is safe.
Nothing after an hour, a known-good charger and one restart
Stop testing. That’s a power, battery or board fault, and it needs a bench. If the photos matter, say so first, because it changes how the phone gets handled.
It gets hot on the charger, the back or screen is lifting, or it smells
Unplug it now. That’s a battery safety problem, not a power problem. Read the next section.
06 / Look, don’t lever

Signs to stop, and what not to do

Heat, swelling or a burning smell means stop.

A battery that took a hard knock can fail in a dangerous way. If the phone gets hot while charging, the screen or back is lifting, or it smells like something burned, unplug it, don’t try to turn it on, and keep it away from anything that can catch fire. Then read screen lifting or swollen battery.

  • Don’t keep it on the charger overnight hoping. An hour is the test. Leaving a phone that took a hard drop charging unattended for days doesn’t add information and isn’t a good idea if the battery was hurt.
  • Don’t open it. Prying a dropped phone open risks tearing the display cable and the battery, and turns a port or battery job into a bigger one.
  • Don’t erase it to fix it. A reset can’t fix a phone that won’t power on, and Google’s last-resort manual reset for a Pixel erases everything on it. Save that decision for after the data is safe.
  • Don’t use rice or heat unless water was involved, and even then, see screen black after water instead.
07 / Before the repair

Find out whether the photos are safe

A phone that won’t turn on can’t back itself up, so check what’s already saved. From a computer or another device, sign in to the account the phone used, iCloud for an iPhone or your Google account for most Android phones, and look at whether recent photos and messages are there.

If they are, the repair decision is only about the phone. If they aren’t, the goal changes from “fix this phone” to “get the data off it,” and the shop should know that before anything is opened. Back up before a repair covers what to check.

08 / Send the evidence, not a guess

What to send for an accurate quote

  • The exact model, if you know it. With a dead phone you can find it on the box, the receipt or your carrier account.
  • What happened: the height, what it landed on, and whether it was plugged in at the time.
  • What you tried: the restart, how long it charged, and with which charger.
  • Any heat, swelling, smell or cracked glass, and any earlier repairs.
  • Whether the photos and messages are backed up.
