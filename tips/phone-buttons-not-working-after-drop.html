Phone buttons · After a drop · Stuck, mushy or dead

Dropped it, and now
a button won’t work?

Samuel, Owner & Technician · November 22, 2026

Take the case off and check that nothing is pressing on the button or blocking it. If it’s still stuck or dead, leave the phone on, back it up now, and turn on the on-screen controls so you can keep using it without that button.

It’s fairly uncommon for a drop to break a button, which is why the case comes first. If the button really is damaged, no setting will repair it, and what you control is the order you do things in. A forced restart needs working buttons, and so does turning the phone back on, so the backup comes first, while the phone is still running. A dead button is an inconvenience you can work around for a while. A power button that’s stuck down is more urgent, because the phone reads it as someone holding the button.

For a phone whose physical button stopped working, feels mushy, sticks or only works sometimes after a drop: the power or side button, the volume buttons, an iPhone’s Ring/Silent switch or Action button. If the phone won’t turn on at all, or the buttons are fine and something else failed, the guides linked below fit better.

01 / Rule out the easy causes

First, rule out the case and the software

In my experience, a button that stops working after a drop is fairly uncommon. So rule out the two things that imitate it before you assume the hardware is damaged. Start with the case: a drop can bend or shift it so it holds a button down or blocks it.

  • Take off the case and any film over the buttons. That’s Apple’s first step for a button or switch that isn’t working, and Samsung says to check whether the case or debris is blocking the keys.
  • Wipe around the button. Apple says to check for debris or dirt and clean with a soft cloth. Keep it dry, and keep anything pointed out of the gap.
  • Look at the frame at that spot. Compare the button with the one next to it. Is it sitting lower, tilted, or pinched by a dent in the frame? Does it still click?

Now test it bare. If the button works with the case off, the case was the problem.

Then the software, once the backup below is done. One ordinary restart can clear a software glitch. If the broken button is one you’d need for that, an iPhone or a Galaxy can restart from the screen (see the stand-ins below). On a Galaxy, Samsung’s check is to install any waiting update, then open Samsung Members and choose Get Help, View tests, Buttons. If it shows “Action required,” Samsung recommends a service visit.

02 / Stuck or dead

Is the button stuck down, or dead? The phone acts differently

A stuck button sends its signal all the time. A dead one sends nothing. Which you have decides how urgent this is.

Stuck downThe phone thinks you’re holding it
  • It sits low and doesn’t spring back
  • The volume runs to zero or to full by itself
  • Siri or Bixby keeps opening
  • The phone restarts by itself, or shows the power-off or SOS screen
DeadThe phone never hears the press
  • It moves, and may even click, but nothing happens
  • Or it works only sometimes, or only when pressed hard
  • Everything else on the phone behaves normally

A mushy button, or one that works only when pressed hard, counts as dead for planning: don’t rely on it for a forced restart. Apple treats them all alike. If a button or switch still isn’t working, is damaged or works intermittently, its advice is to set up a repair.

If the Ring/Silent switch or Action button works but calls still don’t ring, check the settings in iPhone not ringing before blaming the drop.

03 / While it’s still on

Back up now, while the phone is still on

This can’t wait for the repair, because two things you’d normally fall back on both need working buttons:

  • A forced restart. Apple’s sequence ends with holding the side button, Samsung’s holds the Side button and volume down for at least 7 seconds, and Google’s for a Pixel holds the power button. With one of those buttons out, a frozen phone stays frozen.
  • Turning the phone back on. Apple’s instruction for turning on an iPhone is to press and hold the side button. Samsung says you need the Side or Power button to turn a Galaxy back on, however you turned it off.
If the power button is the broken one, don’t turn the phone off.If it needs a restart, do it from the screen (iPhone and Galaxy can, next section). Keep it charged so the battery never runs flat, and back up today.

Then back up while everything still runs: iCloud or a computer for an iPhone, your Google or Samsung account for Android. Back up before a repair has the checklist.

04 / Buttons on the screen

Use the on-screen stand-ins for a broken button

iPhone, Galaxy and Pixel each have an accessibility feature that puts the buttons on the screen. Turn yours on now, before you need it.

Lock the screenVolumeScreenshotRestart: iPhone, Galaxy
iPhone: AssistiveTouch
Go to Settings → Accessibility → Touch → AssistiveTouch, or ask Siri to “Turn on AssistiveTouch.” Apple lists what its menu does in place of physical buttons: lock the screen, adjust the volume, summon Siri, restart the device and capture a screenshot. With a dead side button, restart is the one that matters.
iPhone: Back Tap and Shut Down
On an iPhone 8 or later, Back Tap (Settings → Accessibility → Touch → Back Tap) lets a double or triple tap on the back take a screenshot or open Control Center. To power off without buttons, Apple gives Settings → General → Shut Down. Use it only if the side button still works to turn the phone back on.
Samsung Galaxy: Assistant menu
Go to Settings → Accessibility → Interaction and dexterity → Assistant menu and switch it on. Samsung lists lock, volume, screenshots and a power off menu with power off and restart. Many newer Galaxy phones also have a Power icon in the Quick settings panel: swipe down with two fingers and it’s in the upper right.
Pixel and other Android phones: Accessibility Menu
Go to Settings → Accessibility → Accessibility Menu and turn on the shortcut. Google lists screenshots, lock screen, volume, brightness and Quick Settings. Its list doesn’t include power or restart. A Pixel 6 or later normally powers off with the power and volume up buttons together. If volume up is the dead one, Google says a long press of the power button can open the power menu instead: Settings → System → Gestures → Press and hold the power button.
05 / The urgent one

A stuck power button is the urgent one

Restarting by itself, or showing the SOS screen? Don’t wait on this one.

As far as the phone can tell, a stuck power button is being held, and phones do specific things when that button is held. Apple says holding the side button with a volume button brings up the power-off slider, then an Emergency SOS countdown that ends in a call. With the side button stuck down, one volume press completes that. Samsung says pressing the Side button five times in a row starts its Emergency SOS actions, and a sticking button can register as more than one press.

  • Restart loops. Samsung says holding the Side button and volume down for at least 7 seconds restarts a Galaxy, and Google says holding a Pixel’s power button long enough forces a restart. A stuck button can keep doing that. If an iPhone ends up sitting on the logo, read iPhone stuck on the Apple logo.
  • Make an accidental emergency call harder. On an iPhone, Settings → Emergency SOS has two switches, Call with Hold and Release and Call with 5 Presses, and you can still tap Emergency on the passcode screen to dial. On a Galaxy, Settings → Safety and emergency → Emergency SOS has Require swipe to call and a Countdown, which Samsung says helps if you press the Side button by accident.
  • If a call goes out anyway, don’t hang up. Apple says to wait until a responder answers, then explain that you don’t need help.
06 / Inside the button

What a drop can do to a button

A phone button is two parts: the cap you touch, which sits in a cutout in the frame, and a tiny switch behind it on a thin flex cable that runs to the main board. A drop can damage either.

From my bench: it doesn’t happen often. When it does, the button mechanism or the frame around it has usually been physically bent out of shape. How the button feels is a clue to where:

Stuck down, sitting low or crooked
A dent in the frame right at the button can pinch the cap, or the mechanism can be bent so the cap can’t spring back. The switch may be fine and simply held closed.
Moves, but there’s no click, or it feels mushy
The mechanism behind the cap can be bent or crushed by the hit, so the cap has nothing to click against.
Clicks normally, does nothing
Harder to judge from outside. The switch or its flex can be damaged while the click survives.

What’s unlikely is a cable that came unplugged. Connectors inside a modern phone are held down by covers, shields and clips, so they rarely pop loose in a drop unless the phone was opened before and a cover wasn’t refitted. When a part stops working after a drop, it’s usually the part itself.

07 / Hands off the gap

What not to do to a stuck or dead button

  • Don’t pry at the button or work a tool into the gap. The switch sits right behind the cap.
  • Don’t spray cleaner, alcohol or lubricant into it. Liquid doesn’t stop at the button. Apple’s cleaning advice ends at a soft cloth.
  • Don’t keep pressing harder. Hard presses can break what’s left of a damaged switch.
  • Don’t bend the frame back yourself. The screen and battery sit against it.
  • Don’t erase the phone to fix it. A reset can’t repair a physical button.
08 / One result, one next step

Let the result decide what happens next

Dead or hit-and-miss, and the phone is otherwise normal
Not an emergency. Turn on the stand-in, finish the backup, and book the repair when it suits you. If it’s the power button, don’t shut the phone down in the meantime. A button that’s stuck down is the urgent case: see when it’s urgent.
The phone went off and won’t come back
Follow phone won’t turn on after a drop. Its forced restart may not be possible with a broken button, so go straight to the charging test.
Something else failed in the same drop
See touch screen not working after a drop or Face ID not working after a drop, and report everything together.
09 / Send the evidence, not a guess

What to send for an accurate quote

  • The exact model.
  • Which button, and whether it’s stuck down, mushy, dead or hit-and-miss.
  • A close photo of the button and the frame around it, with the case off.
  • What the phone does by itself: restarts, volume changes, Siri or Bixby opening, the SOS screen.
  • Whether the phone has been opened or repaired before.
  • Whether it’s backed up.
