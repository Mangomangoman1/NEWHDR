Broken screen, photos
still on the phone?

Samuel, Owner & Technician · November 23, 2026

First check, from another device, what’s already in Google Photos and your Google account. Then match the method to what still works: a USB mouse if you can see the screen but can’t tap it, a TV or monitor if you can tap but can’t see.

Both of those end at the same place: the phone has to be unlocked, by you. A mouse, a keyboard and a monitor are only ways of letting you see and enter your own PIN, and none of them gets around it. If nothing gets you that far, a repaired screen is usually how you get back in, because then you can unlock it yourself.

For a Samsung Galaxy, Google Pixel or other Android phone that still turns on but has a cracked, black or unresponsive screen, when the goal is getting the photos and data off it. For an iPhone, use back up an iPhone with a broken screen.

01 / Before any adapter

First, check what’s already backed up, from another device

Before you buy anything, find out how much is already off the phone. On a computer or another phone, sign in to the accounts the broken phone used and look at the newest items.

Photos and videosGoogle PhotosIf backup was on in the appContacts and calendarGoogle Contacts, Google CalendarOn the web, same accountTexts, call history, settingsGoogle Drive › Storage › BackupsShows each device and the dateOlder Galaxy photosOneDriveOnly what synced before September 30, 2026
  • Google Photos. Google says photos and videos backed up there can be reached from any device you’re signed in to. Start at the Google Photos website.
  • The Google device backup. Google says it holds app data, call history, contacts, settings and text messages, and that Google Drive lists each device’s backup and its date. Some of it is encrypted with the old phone’s screen lock, so keep that PIN.
  • Samsung. Samsung Cloud backs up data such as call logs, messages and settings to a Samsung account, to restore on a Galaxy signed in to the same account. Photos are separate: Samsung says Gallery sync with OneDrive ended on September 30, 2026. Photos synced before then are still in your OneDrive account.
  • A memory card. If the phone has an SD card, Samsung’s UK support says you can take it out and read it in a computer.

If everything recent is there, the rest is only about the phone. If backup was on but hasn’t run lately, give it a night on its usual charger and Wi-Fi, as long as the phone isn’t hot, swollen or wet: Samsung says Google’s automatic backup runs when the phone is on Wi-Fi, idle and charging for at least two hours. Don’t leave it for months. Google says a device backup, apart from photos and videos, is erased if the device isn’t used for 57 days.

02 / See it, tap it, unlock it

What still works decides the method

Whether you can see the screen and whether it responds to touch pick the tool:

Whether you can unlock it decides if any tool works, so protect the unlock you still have:

  • Don’t restart it yet. A fingerprint or face that still opens the phone is an unlock that needs no tapping. Google says a Pixel wants the PIN, pattern or password after a restart, and Samsung says a Galaxy asks for your credentials once it reboots.
  • Know about Samsung’s USB lock. Samsung says that from One UI 7, a Galaxy restricts USB connections while the screen is locked, by default. Anything connected after a restart, or after the screen has been locked for an hour, is restricted until the phone is unlocked. So is a mouse or keyboard it hasn’t been connected to before. Google describes a similar lock on Pixel 6 and later when Advanced Protection is on.
  • Don’t guess at the PIN on a screen that misses taps or makes its own. If it’s tapping by itself, turn the screen off and read ghost touch after a cracked screen.
03 / Picture yes, touch no

Screen visible but touch dead: use a USB mouse

This is Samsung’s own method for a Galaxy:

  1. Get an adapter. Samsung names an OTG USB connector, a DeX station or a similar dock. Its DeX FAQ says the DeX Pad and DeX Station aren’t supported on Android 16 and One UI 8, so on a current Galaxy a plain USB-C OTG or multi-port adapter is the safer choice.
  2. Connect the adapter to the phone, then the mouse to the adapter. A pointer appears on the screen.
  3. Unlock it. A keyboard connects the same way for typing your PIN, or you can click the on-screen keyboard.
  4. Back up, with Smart Switch or another method. A right-click returns to the Home screen.
No pointer on a locked Galaxy?On One UI 7 or later that can be the USB lock. Samsung says the phone then shows “To use USB device, unlock your phone and reconnect the USB device.” Unlock with a fingerprint or face if you can, then reconnect the adapter. If you can’t, the mouse can’t help, and the next step is a screen repair.

Where to send the backup. The mouse is using the phone’s only port, so use Wi-Fi, not a cable to a computer. On a Galaxy, Samsung’s path is Settings, Accounts and backup, Back up data under Google Drive, Back up now. Google’s own is Settings, Google, All services, Backup, Back up now. In Google Photos, turn on backup and wait for “Backup complete.” Then check from another device that today’s photos arrived.

Pixel and other Android phones. Google’s accessibility help says only that an Android device can take an external keyboard or mouse by USB or Bluetooth. That’s not a broken-screen procedure, so it’s worth one try and no more.

04 / Touch yes, picture no

Touch works but the screen is black: use a TV or monitor

Borrow a screen. Connect the display first, and unlock once you can see the lock screen. Don’t type a PIN blind.

Samsung Galaxy. Samsung says a phone whose screen can’t be seen can be connected to a TV or monitor with a USB-C to HDMI adapter and an HDMI cable, and worked from the phone while you watch the big display. It also says not all its phones support HDMI output. Its list for a DeX or HDMI cable:

  • Galaxy S and Note: S8 series through S26 series, and Note8 through Note20 series.
  • Foldables: Fold, Z Fold2 through Z Fold8, Z TriFold, Z Flip7 and Z Flip8. The Z Flip7 FE and earlier Z Flips are not compatible.
  • Galaxy A: only the A90 5G is listed.

If yours isn’t on that list, don’t buy an adapter for it.

Google Pixel. Google says a Pixel 8 or later can mirror its screen over a cable to a display that supports DisplayPort or an HDMI to USB-C cable. But its steps include selecting a “Mirror display” dialog on the phone, and it describes the feature for sharing, not for a broken screen. You can’t reliably tap a dialog you can’t see, so don’t count on it.

Other Android phones. Wired video output depends on the maker and model. Check the maker’s support site before buying a cable.

05 / The cable question

Why a locked phone shows an empty folder on a computer

With a locked phone, a computer shows an empty device or nothing at all. Nothing was erased. That’s the lock working.

Google’s steps for moving files over USB start with “Unlock your device,” then have you choose File Transfer on the phone. Samsung’s start the same way, and its UK support page says plainly that the phone has to be unlocked to reach files this way. Smart Switch on a computer (Windows 10 or later, or macOS 10.9 or later) needs Allow tapped on the phone too. So this route opens once you can unlock the phone: with a part of the touchscreen that still works, or after a screen repair. Android photos won’t transfer to a PC has the full USB steps.

Skip any tool or video that promises to unlock the phone, switch on USB debugging from a locked screen, or “extract data from a broken screen.” Every supported route goes through your own unlock.

06 / Neither, or worse

No picture and no touch, and when to stop

No picture and no touch
Samsung says that with some devices a DeX station or multi-port adapter can connect a mouse and keyboard in addition to HDMI. The same model list and the same USB lock apply. Otherwise, a new screen is the way in.
No ring, no buzz, no charging sign
The phone is off, and no adapter changes that. Read phone won’t turn on after a drop, and data recovery if the data matters more than the phone.
The screen is lifting, the phone is hot, or it got wet
Stop charging and stop testing. Read screen lifting or swollen battery or water-damaged phone first.
07 / Read before you click

The buttons that erase the phone instead

“Erase data” and “Factory reset” are not ways in.

Samsung describes Erase data in SmartThings Find and Samsung Find as a remote factory data reset that erases the device’s data, and its SD card. Google says a factory reset from Find Hub permanently deletes all data on the device, and that a reset done with the phone’s buttons erases your data too. None of them unlocks anything.

  • Samsung’s Find website. Samsung’s US pages list finding, ringing, locking and erasing, and no remote backup or unlock. Its UK broken-screen page still describes a remote “Back up” in Find My Mobile, for a phone that’s online, signed in to your Samsung account and has remote controls enabled, and Samsung Ireland says remote unlock is no longer possible. If you sign in and see no Back up option, you don’t have one.
  • Manufacturer mail-in. Google’s instructions for sending in a Pixel say to back up first, mention Repair Mode for many Pixels, and say that if you can’t factory reset it yourself, you sign out of your Google Account and it’s reset for you. Ask any repairer first whether the phone comes back with its data.
08 / When nothing above works

When a new screen is the way back in

If you can’t see, tap or unlock your way to a backup, a repaired screen is usually how you get back in, because then you can unlock it yourself. On a phone that still runs, the data is still on it, behind your lock.

  • A screen replacement swaps the display. It doesn’t need the phone erased.
  • It doesn’t get around a forgotten PIN. A working screen lets you enter your code. It doesn’t remove it.
  • Dead touch after a drop generally means the whole display, because the touch layer and the panel are bonded together.

Tell the shop the data matters before anything is opened. Back up before a repair covers what to save once you’re in, and is my data safe during a repair covers passcodes.

09 / Send the evidence, not a guess

What to send for an accurate quote

  • The exact model. It’s on the box, the receipt or your carrier account.
  • What still works: picture, touch, both or neither, and whether it rings.
  • Whether you know the PIN, and whether a fingerprint or face still unlocks it.
  • What’s already backed up, and what isn’t.
