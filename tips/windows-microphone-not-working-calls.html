Windows 11 · Follow your voice into the call

Mic works in Windows.
Nobody hears your call?

Hailey Device Repair · Reviewed October 30, 2026

Test one input. Select that same microphone in the app.

Play back a short Windows microphone test, then check the call app’s selected input and mute control. For a browser call, check that meeting site’s microphone permission too. A successful Windows recording narrows the next check; it does not prove that your call is using the same device.

For Windows 11 PCs where calls cannot hear you, especially when a local microphone test works. Includes Teams desktop, Chrome and Edge examples. Windows 10, Mac and remote or virtual desktops have different menus and redirection rules.

01 / One voice, one named device

Decide which microphone should hear you

Is it the laptop’s built-in microphone, a USB headset, a webcam’s microphone or a Bluetooth headset? Write down that device’s name. Hearing sound through headphones confirms playback, not their microphone input. Close the live meeting before making test recordings and use a harmless phrase in a quiet space; do not record other people or confidential conversation.

Check the headset’s physical mute switch or boom position. If the laptop has a microphone-mute key, use its model manual to identify it; the key, indicator and Fn behavior vary. For a USB microphone, check the connection and compare another direct USB port if available. Dell’s basic microphone checks distinguish connection, mute and selected-input problems.

My wired headset plays sound, but its mic is missing. Does the plug matter?

Yes. A single combination headset socket and separate headphone/microphone sockets are different. Dell notes that many of its laptops use a single four-pole TRRS headset connection; a headset with two separate plugs needs a compatible combining adapter for that socket.

Check your exact laptop and headset manuals before connecting anything. A headphone-only plug does not establish microphone compatibility. If a known compatible headset is already available, compare it; you do not need to buy an adapter to finish this guide. See Dell’s connector explanation. Do not force a plug or probe a damaged socket.

02 / Listen to the local result

Test the same input you plan to use in the call

  1. Open Start → Settings → System → Sound. Under Input, choose the microphone you identified. A laptop microphone array and a headset microphone may be separate entries.
  2. Open that device’s properties. Under the microphone test, select Start test, speak normally for a few seconds, then select Stop test.
  3. Choose Play under the recorded sample and listen. If needed, adjust Input volume gradually and repeat the same phrase.

Microsoft documents this Windows 11 test. Labels vary with Windows release and audio hardware. If you cannot hear playback, first confirm that Windows is using an audible output; silence from muted speakers does not establish a failed recording.

Your voice is clear in this device’s sample
That input captured your voice in this test. Keep its exact name for the call settings below.
The device is absent, or the sample is silent
Recheck the connection, mute and Windows access below. Do not treat a successful test of a different microphone as a pass for this one.
03 / Access has more than one layer

Allow the intended app to use the microphone

Open Settings → Privacy & security → Microphone. For an app you trust and intend to use, check Microphone access and Let apps access your microphone, then its individual switch if one is listed. Microsoft shows the Windows 11 permission steps. A browser meeting also needs access for the browser itself.

Why is there no separate switch for my desktop app?

Microsoft distinguishes individual supported-app switches from Let desktop apps access your microphone. A browser or desktop call app may depend on this broader setting. Changing it affects desktop-app access generally, so understand that scope before enabling it. An absent individual switch is not proof that the microphone is missing.

Microsoft explains these privacy controls and their exceptions. This setting is not a complete security boundary for every desktop program; review the app’s own controls as well.

If access is greyed out, managed or blocked by your organization, ask the device administrator. Do not remove management, change registry policies or disable security software to force a test. Restore any optional permissions you no longer want after the comparison.

04 / Windows and the app can choose differently

Select the tested microphone inside the call app

Open the affected app’s audio/device settings and select the same named microphone that recorded your voice. Check the app’s own mute control. Dell describes independent app input selection; Windows’ default alone may not be the app’s choice.

  1. Teams desktop: open Settings and more → Settings → Devices, then select the microphone in the audio settings.
  2. If Make a test call is offered, follow its short recording/playback prompts. Microsoft says this test is unavailable in Teams for web; a missing button there is expected.
  3. In a meeting, the arrow beside Mic → More audio settings lets you review the input. Recheck after reconnecting a headset or changing audio devices.

These examples follow Microsoft’s Teams microphone guide. Teams for personal use also has desktop Settings → Devices, but features differ by app/account. Use its personal-use device settings guide; do not assume every account has the same test-call options.

For a Bluetooth mic, check charge and whether it is connected to another device, then reconnect and reselect it. Keep the laptop microphone as a separate comparison, not an assumed replacement for the headset test.

05 / Browser access plus website access

Review the permission for the actual meeting site

Windows allowing a browser to use the microphone does not automatically authorize every website. Confirm you are on the intended meeting service before allowing access.

  • Chrome: open Settings → Privacy and security → Site settings → Microphone. Check the selected microphone. Review the blocked-site list and change the intended meeting site’s microphone permission to Allow if you want that site to hear you. Google documents the current permission and input controls.
  • Edge: visit the meeting site and allow its expected microphone prompt. To review a previous block, open Settings → Privacy, search, and services → Site Permissions → All sites, choose that site and inspect its microphone permission. See Microsoft’s website-permission explanation.

Save anything being drafted in the page, leave the call, then reopen or rejoin it and check the site’s own microphone selection. Do not grant every website microphone access or clear all browser data for this comparison. Work/school Chrome settings may be controlled by an administrator; Google directs managed-browser users to that administrator.

06 / Record which stage changed

Use the difference to choose the next step

Windows sample works; one app or site still fails
Focus the next check on that app’s selected input, mute, access and meeting controls. A hardware replacement is not established by this result.
The app’s test plays back your voice; one person still cannot hear
Confirm the actual meeting uses that input and you are unmuted. Ask that person to check their playback and, with permission, compare another participant. The test does not certify the whole call connection.
Built-in mic works; a headset does not
Record each input name and connection type. Compare the headset’s mute, connection and compatibility before changing the laptop’s working audio setup.
No microphone captures your voice in Windows
The app-only path has not been established. Record missing devices or warnings and seek model-specific Windows/audio support rather than repeatedly changing meeting permissions.

These comparisons narrow an investigation; they do not identify an internal part. Stop once the intended microphone works reliably in the intended call, and keep a note of the setting that changed it.

07 / Keep the next step reversible

Restart safely, then stop before resets

If it persists, save open work and recordings, close the meeting and restart the app or browser. For a normal PC restart, use Start → Power → Restart, then repeat the same local and app tests. Dell’s restart and update guidance also covers checking offered Windows updates. On a managed PC, coordinate updates with IT.

Do not reset Windows or erase app data to finish this checklist.

Microsoft distinguishes reset options: Keep my files still removes apps and settings; Remove everything also removes personal files. Before any later reset, reinstall or driver removal, agree on a backup and recovery plan for documents, recordings and app-stored work. This guide stops before those actions.

Stop sooner for liquid damage, abnormal heat, a loose/broken socket or a plug that needs force. For persistent failure, contact the laptop/headset manufacturer or arrange a laptop assessment. Bring the model, Windows version, input names, connection type, local sample result, affected app/site and exact warning. For locked settings or a remote/virtual desktop, involve the administrator first. Do not publish a Service Tag, account details or a private voice recording in a troubleshooting screenshot.
