Laptop power diagnosis

Laptop plugged in, but not charging?

By Samuel — owner & technician · Updated November 7, 2026

“Plugged in” means the laptop sees something. It does not prove the charger has enough power, the port is sound, or the battery can accept a charge.

Direct answer: Start with the correct-wattage known-good charger. Then note whether the laptop runs from wall power, whether the percentage is frozen near 80%, and whether moving the plug changes anything. Stop if the case is bulging, the trackpad is lifting, or the battery area is unusually hot.

The useful question is not simply “is it charging?” It is: can it run from the adapter, does it recognize the adapter correctly, and can the battery store that power? Those answers point to different repairs.

Start with the charger, not the battery

  1. Plug directly into a known-good wall outlet.
  2. Use the laptop’s original charger or one with the same connector, supported voltage, and at least the required wattage.
  3. For USB-C, verify the charger and cable both support laptop-grade USB Power Delivery. A 20W phone brick may fit perfectly and still be inadequate.
  4. Inspect the cable for bent plugs, cuts, scorching, or a brick that buzzes or smells hot.
  5. Shut the laptop down and leave it connected for 20 minutes. Charging only while off often points to an underpowered adapter.

What the symptom usually means

Stuck at 78–80%Often a deliberate smart-charging or conservation limit, not a failure.
Runs plugged in, dies instantly unpluggedThe adapter path works; the battery is disconnected, deeply failed, or worn out.
Percentage drops while plugged inThe charger is underpowered, the cable cannot carry enough power, or the laptop is drawing more than it receives.
Charging starts when the plug movesPort, cable tip, or solder-joint damage is likely. Stop flexing it.
No charging light, no powerKnown-good charger first; then suspect the port or motherboard power circuit.

Three checks that are actually useful

  • Restart once. Charging controllers and operating-system status can get stuck; a full restart is worth doing.
  • Check the manufacturer utility. Lenovo Vantage, MyASUS, Dell Power Manager, HP Support Assistant, and macOS battery settings may intentionally cap charging.
  • Generate a Windows battery report. Open Terminal or Command Prompt as administrator, run powercfg /batteryreport, then compare Design Capacity with Full Charge Capacity. This tests battery wear—not the charging port.
Do not buy a battery solely because Windows says “plugged in, not charging.” That same message can come from the adapter, port, temperature, firmware, or a charge limit.

Stopped at 50, 60, or 80%? Find the charge limit

If the laptop runs fine on the charger and the percentage parks at the same number every time, look for a battery-saving limit before suspecting hardware. Laptop makers each name theirs differently:

Windows Smart chargingA heart on the battery icon; hover shows “Fully Smart charged.” Microsoft says it may stop short of 100%, and the laptop maker decides how it behaves.
Lenovo: Conservation modeLenovo Vantage → Device → My Device Settings → Battery. Stops around 55–60% on IdeaPads (“60% available, plugged in, not charging”), 75–80% on some newer models. ThinkPads use a custom charge threshold in the same place.
ASUS: Battery Health ChargingIn MyASUS. Balanced mode stops at 80%; Maximum Lifespan mode stops at 60%.
Dell: battery charge settingsDell Power Manager or Dell Optimizer, and on many models the BIOS. “Primarily AC” keeps it below 100%; Custom stops at a level you chose.
HP: Battery Health ManagerA BIOS setting on many HP business laptops. “Maximize My Battery Health” limits charging to 80%.
Microsoft SurfaceSmart charging holds at 80% when it is plugged in for long stretches or runs warm (change it in the Surface app → Battery & charging). Battery Limit in Surface UEFI stops at 50%.
Samsung Galaxy Book: Battery protectionSamsung Settings → Battery and performance (older models: Battery Life Extender). Usually 85%, 90% on some models.
MacBook: “Charging On Hold” or “Not Charging”“Charging On Hold” is Optimized Battery Charging waiting past 80%; choose Charge to Full Now in the battery menu. “Not Charging” can be a battery-health pause, an underpowered adapter, or a workload pulling more than the adapter supplies. Apple silicon Macs on macOS Tahoe 26.4 or later can also have a Charge Limit set in System Settings → Battery.

Turning a limit off is your choice; it exists to slow battery wear on a laptop that lives on the charger. If the percentage still will not move with every limit off and a correct charger, go back to the charger checks above.

Stop-now signs

  • Bottom case, keyboard, or trackpad is lifting
  • Sweet/chemical smell, smoke, hissing, or localized battery heat
  • Scorching around the port or charger plug
  • Connector is broken inside the laptop
  • Recent liquid exposure
A swollen battery is not a troubleshooting project. Shut down, unplug, avoid pressing the case flat, and arrange safe removal.

What repair may involve

If another correct charger behaves the same way, diagnosis moves inside: battery connection and health, DC-in or USB-C port, port board or harness, and the motherboard charging circuit. Some ports are modular; others are soldered. That difference affects cost far more than the symptom does.

What to send for a quote

FAQ

Can a phone USB-C charger charge my laptop?

Sometimes, but many lack the wattage or power profile the laptop requests. Use the original adapter or a verified USB-PD replacement of adequate wattage.

Is “plugged in, not charging” at 80% normal?

Often. Check for smart charging or battery conservation mode before treating it as a failure.

Should I uninstall the battery driver?

A restart can clear a status problem, but repeated driver removal rarely fixes a weak charger, damaged port, or worn battery.

Sources and notes

See Microsoft’s guidance for a PC that is charging slowly or discharging while plugged in, smart charging, and battery reports and swelling safety. Charge-limit names and caps come from each maker: Lenovo, ASUS, Dell, HP, Surface smart charging and Battery Limit, Samsung, and Apple’s Optimized Battery Charging and Charge Limit and “Not Charging” status, checked November 7, 2026. Menu names change with app and firmware versions.

Best first test

Correct charger

Matching connector shape is not enough. Wattage and charging protocol matter, especially over USB-C.

Stop now

Case lifting?

That may be a swollen battery. Power off and do not press the case or trackpad back down.
