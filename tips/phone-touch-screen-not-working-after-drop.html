Phone screens · After a drop · Picture fine, touch not

Phone fell, and the
screen won’t respond?

Samuel, Owner & Technician · November 21, 2026

Unplug it, take off the case and screen protector, wipe the glass dry and do one forced restart. If taps still do nothing, or one area stays dead, the touch layer inside the display was most likely damaged, and that’s a screen repair, not a setting.

When the picture survives a drop but touch doesn’t, there are only a few possibilities: something on top of the glass is in the way, the phone froze, or the display’s touch layer took the hit. The first two you can rule out in ten minutes with nothing but your hands. Before any of it, if touch still partly works, back up the phone. A restart will ask for your passcode, and a screen that barely responds can make that hard.

For a phone that still shows a normal picture after a drop but ignores taps, all over, in one strip or zone, or with a long lag. If it taps things by itself, the picture is gone, or there are lines or spots, the guides linked below fit better.

01 / Which kind of dead touch

Picture fine, touch not: which kind do you have?

A phone that fell and now ignores your finger, while the picture, notifications and clock all look normal, usually does one of three things:

All deadNothing responds anywhereA dead stripOne band or corner ignores tapsLaggy or jumpyTaps land late or in the wrong place

This guide covers all three. If the phone is tapping and typing by itself, that’s ghost touch, which needs a different first move. If there are lines, a spreading black patch or no picture, start with phone screen problems.

02 / Before you restart

If any of it still works, back up before anything else

After a restart, a phone wants its passcode before Face ID or a fingerprint, and typing one on a half-dead screen can be impossible. If you can still get around, even slowly, back up first.

On an iPhone, run an iCloud backup or use a computer you’ve backed up to before; back up an iPhone with a broken screen has both routes. On Android, back up to your Google account, or use Smart Switch on a Galaxy. Then check the time of the last successful backup.

If it starts tapping by itself, stop and turn it off.

03 / Ten minutes, no settings

Clear the easy causes first, using only your hands

None of these needs a working screen:

  1. Unplug everything. Apple says to disconnect Lightning or USB-C accessories, and if the screen then works, to try a different outlet, cable or charger. Samsung says a damaged charger can make touch malfunction.
  2. Take off the case. A bent case can press on an edge. Samsung says non-genuine cases and protectors can affect touch, especially around the screen edges.
  3. Look hard at the screen protector. A drop can crack a glass protector or lift it off the glass, and that can block touch over that area. Apple and Google both say to remove it and test again; Google warns it may not go back on. If it’s shattered, read cracked protector or cracked screen before you peel.
  4. Wipe the glass dry. Apple says the screen should be clean and free of debris or water, and Samsung says moisture on the screen can affect touch. Use a soft, dry cloth.
01Unplug02Case off03Protector off04Wipe dry05One restart

Then do one forced restart. A frozen phone can look like dead touch. It doesn’t erase anything:

  • iPhone with Face ID: press and quickly release volume up, press and quickly release volume down, then press and hold the side button. Apple says to hold until the Apple logo appears, which might take longer than 10 seconds.
  • iPhone 7: hold the side button and volume down together until the Apple logo appears. iPhone 6s and earlier: hold Home and the side or top button together, about 10 seconds.
  • Samsung Galaxy: Samsung says to hold the side key and volume down together for more than 7 seconds, until the phone restarts by itself.
  • Pixel and other Android phones: Google’s Android help says that on most phones you hold the power button for about 30 seconds, or until the phone restarts.
One restart, then look.If touch comes back and stays back, it was a freeze or something on the glass. If the same area is still dead, more restarts won’t change it.
04 / Where touch stops

Map the dead zone in thirty seconds

If some touch works, find out where it stops. Google’s Android help describes a simple test:

  1. Put your finger on the top-left corner and pull down the notification shade.
  2. Drag slowly toward the bottom-right corner without lifting. Google says to go slowly enough that you could count to 10 on the way.
  3. If the shade follows you all the way down, no single area is dead. If it lets go partway, repeat it and note whether it lets go in the same place.

Finger trackedDead band: the shade lets go hereSame spot twice? That’s a dead area, not a slip

If it lets go in the same spot twice, Google’s advice is to contact the manufacturer. After a drop, that spot often sits along a crack or near the corner that took the hit.

Built-in tests, if you can reach them:

  • Samsung Galaxy: Samsung Members → Support → Phone diagnostics, then the Touch screen test.
  • Pixel 9 models: Settings → Display & touch → Touch diagnostics.
  • iPhone: the slow drag works the same way with an app icon you’re moving on the Home Screen.

If touch is laggy rather than dead, Samsung suggests Touch sensitivity (Settings → Display) when a protector is fitted, and Google lists Adaptive touch or Screen protector mode under Display & touch → Touch sensitivity. If the phone reads things aloud when you tap, Samsung says TalkBack may be on: tap once to select, double-tap to open.

05 / Inside the display

Why a drop can kill touch but leave the picture

A phone screen is a stack: cover glass, a touch-sensing layer and the panel that makes the picture, usually bonded into one assembly that connects to the board with thin flex cables. Picture and touch are separate jobs in that stack, so one can fail alone.

A dead strip or corner
A hard corner hit can crack the touch layer under glass that looks fine, or along a visible crack, while the picture carries on.
Everything dead at once
Usually the display itself. Connectors inside a modern phone are held down by covers, shields and clips, so they rarely pop loose in a drop. A loose connector is more possible if the phone was opened before and a cover wasn’t put back, and a board fault is possible but unlikely.
Laggy, jumpy or late
A damaged touch layer doesn’t always fail cleanly. It can respond slowly or misread where your finger is, and that can get worse.

When touch dies after a drop, it generally means the whole display needs replacing, because the touch layer and the panel are bonded together and can’t be separated.

Can a dead zone come back?It can come and go, depending on what caused it. It doesn’t fix itself for good, though. The damage underneath is still there, and it needs dealing with eventually.
06 / One result, one next step

Let the result decide what happens next

Touch came back after the restart, or with the protector off
A freeze or an accessory was the problem. Back it up anyway, and check the phone’s own glass before fitting a new protector: cracked protector or cracked screen shows how.
One area is dead in the same place every time
That points to damage in the touch layer there. Use the parts that work to back up, then plan a display repair. It may start working again now and then, but it won’t stay fixed.
Nothing responds anywhere, and the picture is normal
The touch layer is out, which usually means a new display. Don’t reset it. Sort out the data, then have it looked at.
It starts opening things or typing on its own
Turn the screen off now and read ghost touch after a cracked screen. Random taps can enter wrong passcodes.
Lines, a black spot or the picture fading
The panel was hit too. See black spot on a phone screen, or on a Galaxy, Samsung screen issues for green lines.
07 / When you can’t tap

Getting your data off when you can’t tap

If touch is completely dead and there’s no recent backup, what you can do depends on the phone:

  • Samsung Galaxy: Samsung says that when touch doesn’t work but the screen is still visible, you can plug in a USB mouse through an OTG adapter, a DeX station or a similar dock. A pointer appears, a right-click takes you to the Home screen, and a USB keyboard connected the same way lets you type your PIN. From there you can back up with Smart Switch.
  • iPhone: try a computer you’ve backed up to before. If it asks you to unlock or tap Trust on the phone, stop there. Back up an iPhone with a broken screen walks through it.
  • Other Android phones: check your Google account from another device for recent photos and contacts.

If none of that works, a repaired screen is usually how you get back in, because then you can unlock it yourself. Tell the shop the data matters before anything is opened.

08 / Look, don’t press

What not to do while touch is dead

Don’t factory reset it to fix touch.

Samsung lists a factory reset as a last resort, and Google lists one as an advanced step. Both say it removes all your data. It can’t repair a cracked touch layer, and with dead touch you may not be able to set the phone up again.

  • Don’t press or flex the dead area. Pressure can spread the damage into the picture.
  • Don’t guess at the passcode on a screen that misses taps. Wrong attempts add up and can lock you out.
  • Don’t use it with sharp, loose glass. See is a cracked screen safe to use.
  • Stop if the screen is lifting or the phone is swelling or hot. That’s a battery problem first: read screen lifting or swollen battery.
  • If it got wet, go to water-damaged phone instead.
09 / Send the evidence, not a guess

What to send for an accurate quote

  • The exact model. On a phone you can’t tap, it’s on the box, the receipt or your carrier account.
  • Which kind of dead touch: all of it, one area (which one), or laggy. A short video of the drag test helps.
  • Any cracks, earlier screen repairs, and whether a protector was on it.
  • Whether you can still unlock it, and whether it’s backed up.
