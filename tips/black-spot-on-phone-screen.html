Phone screens · After a drop or pressure · A dark patch that grows

A black spot spreading
on your phone screen?

Samuel, Owner & Technician · November 21, 2026

A black spot or ink-like blot that shows up after a drop or pressure points to damage inside the display panel, not a setting. It usually spreads slowly, but a damaged display can fail completely at any moment, so back up the phone now, don’t press on it, and plan on a screen replacement.

Samsung says round black spots or ink-like marks inside the screen mean the panel may have been damaged by pressure or impact, even when the glass isn’t cracked, and that this damage may spread over time. The glass on top can look perfect, because the damage is in the layer that makes the picture. One screenshot, viewed on another device, confirms it in a minute. After that, waiting doesn’t change the repair. It only risks losing the screen you need to back up.

For a black or dark area that stays in the same place on the screen: a spot, an ink blot, a patch creeping in from an edge, or half the screen gone black, usually after a drop or pressure. Bright spots, lines on their own and a fully black screen have their own guides, linked below.

01 / Shape, place, timing

Black spot, half the screen black, or black with lines?

“Black spot” covers a few different pictures. Note which one you have, because it’s what a shop needs to know and it tells you how urgent this is:

Ink spotA round blot, often near a crack or the corner that hitEdge creepDark area bleeding in from a sideHalf blackA whole section of the screen is goneBlack with linesA dark patch plus colored or white lines
  • Does it stay put? Scroll, rotate the phone and open another app. Damage in the screen stays in the same place on the glass while everything else moves.
  • Is it growing? Take a photo of it today with another phone. Then you’ll know, instead of guessing.
  • When did it start? Right after a drop, after a day in a tight pocket or bag, or with no obvious cause. A black area with a lifting screen or a bulging case is a battery warning first: read screen lifting or swollen battery.
02 / Before anything else

Back up now, while you can still see enough

Don’t wait to see whether it gets better. Samsung says this kind of damage may spread over time, and damage in the panel doesn’t heal. Even a spot that barely moves can come with the rest of the display failing without warning.

  • iPhone: run an iCloud backup or back up to a computer you’ve used before. Back up an iPhone with a broken screen has the steps if part of the screen is already hard to use.
  • Android: back up to your Google or Samsung account, or with Samsung’s Smart Switch on a Galaxy, then check from another device that recent photos are there.

Touch can keep working over a black area even when you can’t see what’s under it. If you know where a button sits, you may still be able to tap it. If touch is failing too, read touch screen not working after a drop.

03 / The one test that helps

Do the screenshot test: is it the picture or the panel?

A screenshot saves the picture your phone drew, not the light coming out of the screen. That makes it the cleanest way to show the phone itself is fine and the panel isn’t.

  1. Take a screenshot with the black area visible. Apple says to press the side button and volume up together on a current iPhone, or the Home and side buttons on an iPhone SE. Google says that on many Android phones you press power and volume down together.
  2. Send it to another phone or a computer and look at it there. Don’t judge it on the damaged screen, because the black area would sit on top of it anyway.
  3. Compare. No black area in the copy means the phone drew a clean picture and the panel is what’s failing.

The screenshot, on another device

After a drop, that’s the result to expect, and it’s still worth having: it rules out the wallpaper, an app or a setting, and it means the rest of the phone is drawing normally. If you can’t take or send a screenshot, photograph the screen with another phone instead, on the lock screen and in a couple of apps.

04 / OLED or LCD

OLED or LCD: which screen is this?

Black spots happen on both kinds of screen, for different reasons.

OLED
Apple says OLED has no backlight: each pixel makes its own light. That’s why an OLED can show pure black, and why damaged pixels simply go dark. An ink-like black blot that grows from a crack or a hit corner is the typical OLED damage pattern, and it can come with green, white or colored lines at its edge. Apple lists the iPhone X, XS and XS Max, and the iPhone 11 Pro models and later (except the iPhone SE), as OLED. Many Android phones use OLED too.
LCD
An LCD lights the picture from behind with a backlight. When the LCD layer cracks, the liquid crystal can leak into a dark, inky patch, sometimes with a rainbow edge, while the backlight still glows around it.

Not sure which you have? LCD vs OLED explains the dark-room test and why the two screens are priced differently.

05 / Will it spread

Will it spread? Usually slowly, but not always

The panel under the glass is a thin, layered sheet of tiny circuits. A hard hit or steady pressure can crack those layers without cracking the glass on top. Once that happens:

  • The crack keeps going. Every flex in a pocket, every press of a thumb and every temperature change puts stress on an already cracked layer, and the damaged area can grow outward.
  • On an OLED, the seal is broken. The light-making layers are sealed in because air and moisture damage them. A crack through the panel breaks that seal, and more pixels around the damage can stop lighting.
  • On an LCD, the liquid keeps moving. Liquid crystal that has escaped its layer can keep spreading through the gap.

From my bench: black spots are often slow. They can stay basically unchanged for a very long time, or they can move quickly, but on the phones I see they’re usually slow. That doesn’t mean the display is okay. A damaged display can fail entirely at any moment, which is why “wait and see” is the wrong plan. It doesn’t change the repair; it only risks the screen you need to back up.

06 / One result, one next step

Let the result decide what happens next

Black area on the phone, clean in the screenshot copy
The panel is damaged. Back up, stop pressing on it and plan a display replacement. On most phones the panel, touch layer and glass are one assembly, so the fix is a new screen.
Black area is in the screenshot too
It’s part of what’s on screen: the wallpaper, an image or an app. Check those before anything else.
One or two tiny dark dots, no drop
Those may be dead pixels. Samsung says a couple of dead pixels isn’t unusual, and its first steps are a software update and Safe mode. Watch whether more appear.
Black area plus lines
Samsung says lines or unusual colors can appear after an impact, especially around the edges, and recommends display repair. Google says lines on a Pixel may be a hardware issue, and that dead pixels or cracks are a repair. The plan doesn’t change: back up, then repair.
Half the screen black, the rest working
Same damage, further along. Back up today using the half that works.
The whole screen went black
That’s a different job. Go to phone screen black but still on.
07 / Look, don’t press

What not to do while you wait for a repair

Don’t press on the spot.

Pushing on it to see what it does, or “massaging” it out, is the same kind of pressure that cracks the panel. It can make the spot grow while you watch.

  • Don’t wait to back up. The repair is the same next week. The amount of screen you can still use may not be.
  • Keep it out of tight pockets and packed bags. Bending and pressure are what spread it.
  • Don’t count on an update. Google and Samsung both suggest a software update for some display problems, and it’s worth installing, but it can’t fix a cracked panel.
  • Stop if the screen is lifting or the phone is bulging or hot. A swelling battery can push on the display from behind and damage the panel. That’s a safety problem first: read screen lifting or swollen battery.
  • Mind loose glass. If the glass is cracked as well, see is a cracked screen safe to use.

On a Samsung Galaxy, Samsung screen issues covers the green lines and burn-in that can show up alongside this.

08 / Send the evidence, not a guess

What to send for an accurate quote

  • The exact model, from the About screen in Settings.
  • A straight-on photo of the screen on a white background, and the date you first noticed the spot.
  • Your screenshot test result.
  • What happened: a drop, pressure, heat or liquid, and any earlier screen repair.
  • Whether touch still works everywhere, and whether the phone is backed up.
