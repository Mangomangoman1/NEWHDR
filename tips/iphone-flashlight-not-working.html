iPhone flashlight · Find the useful clue

iPhone flashlight
won’t turn on?

Samuel, Owner & Technician · October 27, 2026

Check the control. Observe the actual light.

On a cool, responsive iPhone, try Flashlight in Control Center and raise its brightness. A missing button, a dim beam, and no rear light are different results. If the phone is hot or shows a temperature warning, stop and let it cool before testing again.

For the rear LED flashlight on iPhone, including Home-button and Face ID models. A screen that lights up for a selfie, notification flashes, and a camera that shakes need different checks.

01 / Before switching it on

Check the phone, not just the icon

If the phone was recently wet, has a lifting screen or swollen battery, smells burnt, or is unusually hot while idle, stop testing and charging it. Follow the wet-phone or swollen-battery guidance and arrange assessment. Do not open the phone or press a separated screen back down.

For an otherwise safe phone, remove an easily detachable case, film, or accessory that covers the flash. Apple recommends checking for obstructing accessories. Do not pry at camera glass. Finish any recording or video call, save work, and return to the Home Screen so each test starts the same way.

02 / A temporary protection can look like failure

Let a hot iPhone cool before repeating tests

Temperature warning? Pause here.

Apple says excess internal heat can temporarily disable the camera flash. If a temperature warning appears, turn the iPhone off, move it away from sunlight to a cooler environment, and allow it to cool. Follow Apple’s temperature guidance before resuming.

End gaming, navigation, or camera use and pause charging while a warm phone returns to normal conditions. Do not use a freezer, water, or forced heating to hurry the test. There is no guaranteed number of minutes. If the light returns after cooling, record the activity and conditions; that result fits a temporary restriction, not a confirmed broken LED. Repeated warnings during light use need support. See also phone overheating checks.

03 / Test one direct control

Find Flashlight and check its brightness

  1. Unlock the phone. For Face ID models, swipe down from the top-right corner to open Control Center. For a Home-button iPhone, including iPhone SE, swipe up from the bottom edge.
  2. Tap the Flashlight control. Point the rear of the phone at a nearby wall or plain paper, away from anyone’s eyes. Check whether light actually appears.
  3. Touch and hold the Flashlight control and raise its brightness slider to a middle setting. Observe the beam briefly, then tap the control to turn it off.

These are Apple’s flashlight and brightness controls. If adjusting brightness produces a usable beam, the LED is operating in this test. If the button is gray or unresponsive, note that separately from a button that highlights with no light; neither appearance alone identifies a failed part.

What if Flashlight is missing from Control Center?

On iOS 18 and later, check the other groups of controls first. To add it, enter editing with the + button, choose Add a Control, then find and add Flashlight. Apple documents the current layout and iOS 18 layout.

On iOS 17 and earlier, use Settings → Control Center and add Flashlight from the available controls, following the older layout. Adding a shortcut is a control change; it does not repair the LED.

It works here, but not from the Lock Screen or Action button

If a Flashlight button is present on the Lock Screen, press and hold it. On an iPhone with an Action button, check Settings → Action Button: that button may be assigned to another action. If it is assigned to Flashlight, press and hold it. A working Control Center test points toward the other activation route; do not replace a flash on that evidence alone.

04 / Optional comparison, once the phone is cool

Compare the flashlight with a rear-camera photo

Turn the flashlight off, then open Apple’s Camera app in Photo mode with the rear camera. For one test photo of an ordinary object, explicitly select Flash On. Auto can choose not to fire, so an Auto photo is not a dependable failure test.

In the current Camera layout, tap the onscreen Camera Controls button or swipe up from the camera modes, then choose Flash → On. Other iOS layouts expose a lightning-bolt flash control. Use Apple’s camera-tools guide with its version selector if your screen differs. Check for a brief rear flash while taking the photo, then restore your usual flash setting. A bright selfie screen does not test the rear LED.

Flashlight works; an Auto photo does not flash
The rear light can work. Check the camera’s flash selection before seeking a hardware repair. If Flash On still fails, record that more specific result.
Rear photo flash works; Control Center flashlight does not
The LED fired in this test. Recheck the control and brightness, then try the normal restart below. This difference does not establish which software or component is responsible.
Neither works after cooling
Record both results and try one normal restart. Persistent failure warrants assessment; it does not tell you whether the LED, camera assembly, connection, or another cause needs attention.
05 / One repeatable software check

Restart normally, then retest the same way

Save work and follow Apple’s restart steps for your model. Face ID phones use a different button combination from Home-button models; the first-generation SE uses its top button. After restarting, repeat the brief Control Center test at moderate brightness.

If the problem remains and the phone is otherwise stable, make a current backup before considering an update. Check Settings → General → Software Update and follow Apple’s update instructions for the version offered to your phone. Do not start an update on a damaged or overheating phone. Stop if an unexpected erase or recovery prompt appears; get help protecting the data first.

06 / Keep the evidence and the data

Stop when the safe checks stop adding information

If the flashlight or rear flash still fails, Apple advises that service may be needed. A drop, liquid exposure, cracked camera area, or simultaneous camera failure makes professional assessment more useful than repeated toggling. For shaking, clicking, or focus trouble, use the separate camera guide.

Do not erase your phone to complete this checklist.

These comparisons do not require Reset All Settings, a factory erase, or recovery-mode restoration. Erasing can remove unbacked-up photos, messages, and app data. Agree on a backup and data-preservation plan before any later destructive step.

For an iPhone assessment, bring the model and iOS version, when the problem started, any damage or recent repair, whether cooling changed it, and the Control Center/brightness/rear-photo results. Share symptoms without your passcode or private photos. A short, specific record helps avoid a guessed part replacement.
