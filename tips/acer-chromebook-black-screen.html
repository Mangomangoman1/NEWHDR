Acer Chromebook · Black screen · Flicker or lines

Chromebook screen
gone dark or glitchy?

Samuel, Owner & Technician · November 23, 2026

Tap the brightness key, do one hard reset, then plug it into a TV or monitor. Those three checks tell a settings problem from a dead screen or cable.

A Chromebook with a black or glitching screen is often still running underneath. The charging light, a faint picture under a flashlight, or a clean image on an external display all prove it. If the outside screen works and the built-in one doesn’t, the fault is in the display, its backlight or the cable that runs through the hinge, and no reset or Powerwash will fix it. Don’t erase the Chromebook to chase a hardware fault.

For an Acer Chromebook (or any Chromebook) whose screen is black, flickering, striped or garbled while the machine seems to be on. If there’s no light, no sound and no sign of power at all, start with laptop won’t turn on. Windows shortcuts don’t apply here; ChromeOS has its own.

01 / Dark isn’t dead

Find out whether it’s on under the black

A black screen and a dead Chromebook look the same from across the room. Spend a minute telling them apart, because the fixes have nothing in common.

  • Look at the light next to the charging port. Acer says it should glow while the Chromebook is charging. A lit charging light means power is reaching the machine.
  • Shine a phone flashlight across the screen at a low angle in a dim room. A faint picture of the desktop or login screen means the panel is drawing an image and only the light behind it is missing.
  • Feel the keyboard. A backlit keyboard that lights up, or a Chromebook that’s warm in the usual spot, is running.
Charging lightFaint pictureWarm or lit keys

Any one of those means the Chromebook is alive and the screen is the question. None of them, even on a charger you know works, is a power problem instead: see laptop won’t turn on.

If it flickers or shows lines instead

A glitching picture needs one extra test. Press Ctrl + Show windows (the top-row key with a box and two lines) to take a screenshot, then look at it on another screen, such as an external display, or copy it to Google Drive and open it on your phone. A screenshot captures the picture ChromeOS made, not what the panel shows. Lines that show up in the screenshot come from software or graphics. A clean screenshot means the picture was fine and the built-in display or its cable mangled it.

02 / Cheap checks first

Try the cheap checks, in this order

  1. Plug it in and give it a few minutes. Use the charger that came with it, or one you know works. iFixit’s Acer checklist starts here: a flat battery can look exactly like a dead screen.
  2. Press the brightness-up key several times. It’s the sun on the top row. Acer points out that brightness can get knocked all the way to zero, which turns the screen fully black on a running Chromebook.
  3. Unplug everything. Pull any monitor, dock, hub, mouse or USB drive. A connected display can pull the picture away from the built-in screen, and Acer and iFixit both list external devices as a cause.
  4. Reset the screen size. If the picture is there but huge, tiny or oddly scaled, press Ctrl + Shift + 0. Google lists it as the shortcut that resets the screen resolution to default.
  5. Let a hot Chromebook cool down. Acer suggests checking whether it’s abnormally warm and giving it time before testing again.

None of these change your files or settings beyond brightness and screen size, so they’re safe to try more than once.

03 / One safe reset

Do one hard reset, the ChromeOS way

A hard reset restarts the Chromebook’s hardware, like the keyboard and touchpad. It’s the right tool for a screen that stayed black after the checks above. This is Google’s method for most Chromebook laptops:

1

Turn the Chromebook off. If the screen is black, hold the power button until any lights go out.

2

Press and hold Refresh (the circular arrow on the top row), and tap Power.

3

When the Chromebook starts up, release Refresh.

Acer’s own write-up describes holding Refresh and Power together for about 10 seconds and letting go when the screen or keyboard lights up. Either gets you to the same place.

Google says a hard reset might delete some files in your Downloads folder.Anything in Google Drive is safe. If you keep important files only in Downloads, and the Chromebook still works on an external display, copy them to Drive or a USB stick first.

Some Acer models also have a small reset pinhole on the bottom, which iFixit lists for battery-related faults. Use it only if your model’s manual shows one.

Should I Powerwash or recover ChromeOS instead?

Not for a screen problem. A Powerwash resets the Chromebook to factory settings, and Acer warns that files can be lost along the way. ChromeOS recovery from a USB stick erases everything stored on the device. Both can fix software, but neither can fix a panel, backlight or cable. Prove the screen with the external display test before you consider it.

If it’s a school or work Chromebook, check with whoever manages it before a reset or a repair. Google notes that administrators can block some of its blank-screen fixes on managed devices, and the device may not be yours to have repaired.

04 / The decisive test

Run the external display test

This is the test that settles most black screens. Acer and iFixit both put it near the top of their lists.

  1. Connect a TV or monitor with an HDMI cable or a USB-C to HDMI adapter, depending on what your Chromebook has. Pick the right input on the TV.
  2. Wait a few seconds for ChromeOS to find it. You may see the desktop, the login screen or just the wallpaper.
  3. Note what the built-in screen does while the external one is connected: stays black, flickers, or shows a faint picture under a flashlight.

If the outside screen works, you can sign in and back up anything stored only on the Chromebook while it’s connected. Then read the result below.

05 / One result, one next step

Let the result decide what happens next

The screen came back after brightness, unplugging or the hard reset
It was a setting or a frozen state. Keep an eye on it. If it goes black again on its own, run the external test next time it happens.
External display works, built-in screen black, faint picture under a flashlight
The panel is drawing an image but the backlight isn’t lighting it. That’s a backlight or display-cable fault, and it needs a bench.
External display works, built-in screen fully black, no faint picture
The picture isn’t reaching the panel, or the panel itself failed. iFixit lists a defective display and a faulty display cable for this pattern. Stop testing and get it diagnosed.
Lines or flicker on the built-in screen, clean screenshot, clean external display
The picture is fine until it reaches the screen. That’s the panel or its cable. Read the next section before you move the lid any more.
Lines show up in the screenshot or on the external display too
The problem is upstream of the screen: ChromeOS, graphics or the board. An update or a backed-up Powerwash is worth a try here, and only here.
Nothing on either screen, charging light on
The Chromebook may not be finishing startup. Don’t keep power cycling it. Send the details below and let it be looked at.
06 / Look, don’t lever

Signs it’s the hardware, and what not to do

The built-in screen depends on three parts: the LCD panel, the backlight behind it, and a thin cable that runs from the board, through the hinge, up into the lid. Each one leaves a different trail.

  • Flicker, lines or blackouts that change when the lid moves point to the display cable, which flexes every time the lid opens.
  • A spreading dark or inky patch, a spiderweb of lines from one spot, or a crack points to a damaged panel, often from pressure: a backpack, a book, or a pen closed in the lid.
  • A faint picture under a flashlight points to the backlight side.
  • A stiff or loose hinge, or a gap opening at the lid’s corner, can pinch or pull the cable. See hinge cracking or separating.
Don’t press the screen, rock the lid or pry the bezel.

Pushing on a black panel or flexing the lid to bring the picture back can work for a second. It’s the same strain that caused the problem, and it can turn a cable fault into a cracked panel.

The cable you’d be trying to reach runs through the hinge, and prying the bezel off a Chromebook can tear it. Set the lid at one comfortable angle and leave it there until it’s repaired.

If the Chromebook got wet or was dropped, say so up front. Liquid and impact change what gets checked first.

07 / Send the evidence, not a guess

What to send for an accurate quote

  • The model name from the label on the bottom of the Chromebook.
  • What you see: black, faint picture under a flashlight, flicker, lines, or a dark patch.
  • The external display result, and whether the screenshot was clean.
  • Whether it changes with the lid angle.
  • Any drop, pressure, spill or earlier screen repair, and whether it’s a school or work device.
