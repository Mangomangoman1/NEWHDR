iPhone camera · After a drop · Cracked lens

Cracked the camera
lens on your iPhone?

Samuel, Owner & Technician · November 21, 2026

Take one photo with every lens: if only the cracked lens gives hazy, streaky or flared shots, it’s usually just the cover glass. If that camera is black, shakes or can’t focus, the camera itself took the hit.

The ring of glass on the camera bump is a cover, not the camera. It sits over the lens and keeps dust and water out. A crack in it scatters light, so photos through it get a soft patch, a streak or glare around lights, while the camera underneath can be fine. A hard hit can also damage the camera behind the glass, and that shows up differently: a black view, a picture that won’t settle, or focus that never locks. Apple’s safety guidance is not to use an iPhone with cracked glass, because it can cause injury, so treat whatever you do in the meantime as temporary.

For a cracked, chipped or shattered camera lens cover on the back of an iPhone, and for a camera that went hazy, foggy or black after a drop. If the whole back is cracked, or the picture shakes and clicks, the guides linked below fit better.

01 / Protector or phone

First, is it a lens protector or the iPhone’s own glass?

Stick-on camera lens protectors and metal lens rings are common, and a cracked one looks exactly like a cracked lens. Find out which layer broke first.

Stick-on protector or lens ringAn accessory. Peel it and the problem may go with it.
Lens cover glassPart of the phone. Keeps dust and water out of the camera.
Camera moduleThe lens, sensor and moving parts behind the glass.
  • Take the case off. Apple’s first step for any camera problem is to remove any case, film or accessory that might block the camera or puts a magnet near it.
  • Look for an edge. In good light, tilt the phone. A protector has a visible rim or a slight step where it sits on top of the bump. The phone’s own glass sits flush inside the metal ring.
  • Peel the protector slowly. Lift it from one corner and look again. If it’s shattered, stop as soon as the glass underneath looks cracked too, so you don’t pull pieces of it out.

If the crack disappears with the protector, you’re done: clean the glass gently and retest. If the crack is in the phone’s glass, keep going.

02 / Every lens, one photo

Test each lens, one at a time

A crack usually sits over one camera. Find out which one, and whether the problem is the glass or what’s behind it. Use Apple’s Camera app, not a social app, in good light.

  1. Wipe the glass lightly, but only where it isn’t cracked. Apple says to clean the front and back lenses with a microfiber cloth if photos are blurry. Leave a cracked lens alone: tape it instead, as described below.
  2. Tap each zoom button. Depending on your model, that’s 0.5x, 1x and a longer zoom such as 2.5x, 3x, 5x or 8x. Take one photo of the same scene with each.
  3. Shoot a bright light. A lamp or a window, with each lens. Cracked cover glass often shows up as glare, streaks or a glow around lights before it shows up anywhere else.
  4. Get close to something. On supported models, Apple says Camera automatically switches to the Ultra Wide camera when you get close to a subject, for macro shots. So a crack over the 0.5x lens can show up in close-ups even when the screen says 1x.
  5. Flip to the front camera with the rotate button, and record a few seconds of video on the rear camera.

If the flash cover is cracked too, test the light from Control Center the way Apple suggests: on an iPhone X or later, swipe down from the upper-right corner and tap the flashlight button. If that fails, read iPhone flashlight not working.

03 / Glass or camera

Read the photos: cracked glass or a damaged camera

A soft patch, streak or glare in the same place, on one lens only
The classic cracked-cover result. The crack scatters light on its way into a camera that still works, so the other lenses stay sharp. A crack across the middle of the lens can soften the whole frame the same way.
One camera shows a black screen
Cracked glass scatters light. It doesn’t turn a working camera black. That points at the camera or its connection. See the next section.
The picture shakes, buzzes, clicks or keeps hunting for focus
That’s the moving parts inside the camera, with or without a crack on top. iPhone camera shaking, clicking or not focusing covers it.
Every lens looks hazy, not just one
Check the shared things first: a case lip, a smeared or cracked protector, or condensation. A hard hit on the bump can also reach more than one camera.
Fog or droplets you can see under the glass
Moisture got in through the crack. Skip ahead to fog under the glass.

Both can happen at once, which is why sharp photos through every other lens matter: they’re the best sign the damage stopped at the glass.

04 / One black lens

If the camera is black after the drop

A black view on one camera is a camera problem, not a glass one. Run Apple’s checks in order first:

  • Remove the case and any accessory, restart the iPhone, and update iOS.
  • Check whether one or both cameras fail. To switch between front and rear, tap the rotate button.
  • If the camera screen is still black, Apple’s next step is iOS Diagnostics. Start Recovery Assistant by turning the iPhone off, then pressing and holding the side button when it starts up. Tap Diagnostics Mode, follow the steps, then choose Mobile Resource Inspector and tap Start Diagnostic.

Apple says that if Mobile Resource Inspector finds an issue, the iPhone needs service. If it finds nothing and the camera is still black, that’s worth a bench look too.

On my bench, a camera that stops working after a drop is usually a dead or internally damaged camera module, not a loose connector.

05 / Until it’s fixed

Is it safe to keep using with a cracked camera lens?

Apple’s answer is short: don’t use an iPhone with cracked glass, as it may cause injury. A cracked lens cover is also an open path into the camera. Apple lists dropping the phone among the things to avoid to prevent liquid damage, and says water resistance isn’t permanent anyway.

So get it repaired rather than live with it. Until then, general repair sense, not a manufacturer’s instruction:

  • Keep the bump covered. A case with a raised lip around the camera keeps the cracked glass off tables and out of pocket lint.
  • Stop wiping across it. Every wipe can drag dust into the crack or lift a loose chip.
  • Tape over it. If the glass over a lens is cracked, put a small piece of clear tape over it until it’s repaired. Under the cover glass is the camera’s own lens, with an extremely delicate coating. Once glass particles or dirt get onto it, it effectively can’t be cleaned, and touching it only makes it worse. Tell the shop the tape is there, and use another lens in the meantime.
  • Don’t stick a new lens protector over the crack. It traps whatever is already in the crack, and peeling it later can pull broken pieces with it.
  • Keep it dry. No rain, steamy bathrooms or ski-pocket snow until it’s sealed again.
Glass gone, or you can see into the camera?

Cover it and stop using that lens. An open camera is exposed to dust and moisture, and that can turn a cover-glass repair into a camera replacement. Get it in soon rather than when it’s convenient.

06 / Moisture got in

Fog or haze under the glass after a crack

If the haze sits under the glass, comes and goes with temperature, or you can see tiny droplets, moisture has got in through the crack. Wiping the outside won’t touch it.

  • Don’t heat it. Apple says not to dry an iPhone with an external heat source, so no hair dryer on the lens.
  • If it actually got wet, Apple says to unplug all cables and not charge it until it’s completely dry, and to allow at least 5 hours before connecting a Lightning or USB-C cable. Phone got wet? What to do first has the full list.
  • Don’t wait for it to clear. Fog that clears can come back, because the crack that let it in is still open. Moisture in the camera is a reason to bring it in sooner.
07 / What the fix is

What the repair is: cover glass, camera, or both

The test photos decide it. A hazy patch or glare on one lens, with everything else sharp, usually means the cover glass. A black, shaking or unfocused camera means the camera itself, plus the glass if it’s cracked. Whether the lens cover comes off separately from the back glass depends on the model.

On recent iPhones, a repair shop can often replace the camera cover glass by itself when that’s all that broke. Apple’s own repair manuals don’t list it as a separate part, so ask before you book. Sometimes the camera needs replacing anyway: cracked cover glass can shed particles onto the delicate inner lens, and dirt can get in through the crack. Once debris is on that lens it can’t be cleaned off, so the camera module gets replaced too. That’s the main reason to tape it and bring it in soon.

If the phone was repaired before, look in Settings > General > About. Apple says a Parts and Service History section appears there if your iPhone has been repaired, and on iPhone 12 and later it can list the rear cameras with messages like Genuine, Used, Unknown or Finish Repair. Apple’s list of tracked parts doesn’t include the cover glass, so a past lens-glass repair may not show up there. Unknown Part and Important Message explains each label.

On an Android phone, the same two tests work: check for a protector, then take one photo with each lens. What can be replaced on its own depends on the brand and model, so send the test photos to Android repair. If a Galaxy says “Camera failed,” that’s a different guide.

08 / Send the evidence, not a guess

What to send for an accurate quote

  • The exact model, from Settings > General > About.
  • A sharp photo of the camera bump, taken with another phone in good light, from straight on and at an angle.
  • One test photo from each lens, and which zoom button gave the bad one.
  • Whether any camera is black, shakes or won’t focus, and whether there’s fog under the glass.
  • Any cracks elsewhere on the back, and any earlier camera or back-glass repairs.
