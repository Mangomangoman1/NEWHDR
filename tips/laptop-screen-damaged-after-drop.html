Laptop screens · After a drop · Windows and Mac

Lines, ink or flicker on
a dropped laptop screen?

Samuel, Owner & Technician · November 21, 2026

Plug the laptop into an external monitor or TV. If the picture there is clean, the laptop’s own screen or its cable took the hit, not the graphics.

Lines, black or coloured blotches that look like spilled ink, a crack you can see in the picture but not feel, flicker or half a picture all look like one problem. They aren’t. Some live in the panel, some in the thin cable that runs through the hinge, and a few in the part of the laptop that makes the image. An external display, then one slow pass of the lid to see whether the damage moves with it, tell most of them apart in a few minutes. Do it gently: a damaged panel can get worse with pressure and flexing.

For a laptop that still shows a picture after a drop, a knock or a lid closed on something, but the picture is damaged. If the screen is fully black while the laptop runs, it got wet, or a 2016–2017 MacBook Pro shows bright bars along the bottom, those guides fit better.

01 / Read the damage

Lines, ink, cracks or flicker: name what you see

Open a plain white page, then a dark one, and look at the screen straight on. Each pattern tends to point somewhere different, and one drop can cause more than one.

LinesThin coloured, white or black lines, vertical or horizontal, that stay put when you move windows.

Ink blotchesBlack, purple or rainbow patches, often starting where it hit and sometimes spreading.

A crack in the pictureA spiderweb or streak in the image, even though the surface feels smooth.

Flicker or dropoutsThe picture blinks, rolls or cuts out, sometimes only at certain lid angles.

Half a pictureOne side or a block is dark, frozen, doubled or garbled.

Ink blotches and a crack in the picture point to the panel itself: the layers behind the surface can break even when the top looks fine. Lines, flicker and half a picture can be the panel, its cable or, less often, the graphics. The next two checks separate those.

02 / The key test

Plug in an external monitor or TV

An external display is drawn by the same laptop and the same graphics, but it doesn’t use the built-in panel or its cable. Use a cable you know works and skip the dock if you can: Microsoft says docks, dongles and adapters can cause conflicts.

  • Windows: press Windows key + P. Microsoft lists four choices: PC screen only, Duplicate, Extend and Second screen only. Pick Duplicate so both screens show the same thing and you can compare them. If the monitor doesn’t appear, Microsoft says to go to Settings > System > Display > Multiple displays and select Detect.
  • Mac: Apple says a Mac detects a powered, properly connected display automatically. To mirror, open Apple menu > System Settings > Displays, click the display’s name and choose an option from the “Use as” pop-up. Holding Option shows a Detect Displays button if it isn’t found.

If the external display stays black too, this isn’t a damaged-screen problem yet. On Windows, screen black but fans running covers making the external test meaningful; on a Mac, see external monitor black on a MacBook.

03 / No monitor handy

No monitor? Take a screenshot

A screenshot saves the picture the laptop made, not what the panel shows. Damage in the panel or its cable doesn’t end up in the file.

  • Mac: Apple’s shortcut is Shift-Command-3, which saves a screenshot to the desktop.
  • Windows: Microsoft lists Windows logo key + PrtScn, depending on your hardware, or Fn + Windows logo key + Space bar on a laptop without a PrtScn key. The file goes in Pictures > Screenshots.

Send the file to your phone and look at it there. A clean screenshot points at the screen. Lines or blotches in the screenshot point at the image side.

On a Microsoft Surface, Microsoft adds one more test: turn it off, hold volume up and press and release power, and keep holding volume up until the UEFI screen appears. Microsoft says UEFI isn’t part of Windows, so lines that show there suggest hardware rather than a Windows driver. Other makers publish built-in screen tests for some models. If you use one, follow the steps on your manufacturer’s support page for your exact model.

04 / One slow pass

Move the lid once, slowly

The display cable runs from the base, through or beside the hinge, into the lid. A fault that changes with the lid angle points toward that cable path. Damage that sits still at every angle points toward the panel.

Skip this if anything is cracked.If the lid shell, the hinge area or the glass is cracked, or the hinge clicks, leave the lid where it is. A visible crack or ink is a panel job whatever the lid does, and flexing a cracked lid can make it worse.
  1. Put the laptop flat on a table with a plain white page open.
  2. Start from your normal working angle and close the lid slowly to about halfway, watching the screen.
  3. Open it slowly back to your normal angle and stop. That’s one pass. Don’t rock it back and forth hunting for the spot.

Lines or flicker that appear, move or vanish with the lid suggest the cable or its connection. If the hinge also feels loose or pops, or the case gapped open, read laptop hinge breaking first. Bright bars only along the bottom of a 2016–2017 MacBook Pro are their own pattern: see MacBook Pro stage light.

05 / One result, one next step

Let the result decide what happens next

External clean; built-in damage stays put at every angle
Most likely the panel. Ink, cracks and fixed lines usually mean a replacement panel or display assembly. Back it up through the external display now.
External clean; built-in damage changes with the lid
Points to the display cable or its connection, sometimes with the hinge. Stop moving the lid, keep it at one angle and work on the external display until it’s looked at.
The same damage shows on the external display or in a screenshot
The image is wrong before it reaches the screen. If it began without a knock, Microsoft’s flicker guide points to a display driver or an app. After a drop, the board can’t be ruled out, so don’t swap parts on a guess.
External display stays black as well
Check the connection first: Windows or Mac.
The hinge pops, the case gapped or the lid won’t hold its angle
Structural damage. Read laptop hinge breaking before you use it more.
06 / While it still works

Back up while you still have a picture

A cracked panel can spread, and a damaged cable can cut out for good. Either way you can lose the screen you’re using to reach your files. If the external display works, use it now: on Windows, Second screen only (Windows key + P) puts everything on the external screen; on a Mac, mirroring puts it on both.

Copy what matters to an external drive or a cloud account you can sign in to elsewhere, then check that the copies open. Back up before a repair covers what to include, and is my data safe during a repair? covers what happens to it at a shop.

07 / Don’t make it bigger

Keep the damage from spreading

  • Don’t press on the panel. Pushing on an ink blotch or a line to “see what it does” can make the damaged area larger.
  • Lift it by the base. Never carry it by the screen, and don’t twist the lid to check the damage.
  • Stop opening and closing a cracked lid. Find one angle that works and leave it there. Use the external display, a USB keyboard and a mouse if you can.
  • If the lid closed on something, a pen, earbuds, a cable, look for anything left on the keyboard before it closes again. Closing it again on the same object can crack a panel that survived the first time.
Touchscreen with cracked glass? Stop touching it.

A cracked touch layer can register taps you didn’t make, and broken glass can cut. On Windows, Microsoft’s steps to switch touch off are: right-click Start, open Device Manager, expand Human Interface Devices, right-click HID-compliant touch screen and choose Disable device. Repeat for each one listed. It’s reversible with Enable device. If glass is loose or sharp, a strip of clear packing tape keeps it in place for the trip.

08 / What the bench does

What a laptop screen repair usually involves

Which part gets replaced depends on what the checks above showed, so send those results with a quote request.

The panel
For ink, cracks and fixed lines. On many laptops the panel comes out of the lid on its own. On a touchscreen laptop the glass, touch layer and panel are often bonded together and replaced as one part.
The display cable
For faults that follow the lid. The cable can be replaced on its own, but it means opening the lid and often loosening the hinges to reach it.
Hinge or housing
When the drop cracked a hinge mount or the lid shell. It’s often done alongside the cable, because a broken mount can pinch it.
The whole display assembly
On some laptops, MacBooks among them, the screen can be replaced as a complete lid assembly with the panel, cable and hinges together rather than as separate parts.
09 / Send the evidence, not a guess

What to send for an accurate quote

  • The make and model, from the label underneath, the box or the receipt.
  • A photo of the screen showing a plain white page, taken straight on.
  • The external-display result, and whether the damage changes with the lid.
  • Whether it’s a touchscreen, and any cracks in the glass, lid or hinge area.
  • Whether your files are backed up.
