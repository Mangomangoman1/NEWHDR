MacBook + monitor · Follow the video path

MacBook on.
External monitor black?

Hailey Device Repair · Reviewed October 27, 2026

Try one display, one connection, and the correct input.

Keep the MacBook open, check the monitor’s power and input, and test one display directly from the Mac with a suitable video cable or adapter. Then check Displays settings and the Mac’s supported display count. Charging through USB-C does not establish that video is connected.

For a responsive MacBook Air or Pro whose built-in screen works, connected to a wired monitor. AirPlay, Sidecar, a broken built-in screen, and a Mac that will not start need different checks.

01 / Keep the working screen available

Save work before disconnecting a dock

Finish calls and file transfers. If a dock or monitor also connects a drive, eject that drive in Finder before unplugging its connection. Apple documents Finder’s Eject control. If ejection fails, leave the drive connected and resolve that first; do not pull a cable during a write.

Damage is a stopping point.

Stop using and charging equipment that is wet, unusually hot, smells burnt, or has damaged power cables or ports. Do not force a connector, probe a port, or open a monitor or power supply. Arrange assessment instead of repeated power tests.

02 / Start with one monitor

Check power, input, and the actual video connection

  1. Check power and input. Connect the monitor to power and use its power button if provided. On third-party monitors, use their controls to check brightness and select the connected input, such as HDMI 1 rather than HDMI 2. A “No signal” message is useful: record it. Dell’s monitor connection guide explains input selection; use your own model’s manual for its buttons.
  2. Keep the lid open and disconnect extra displays. With storage safely ejected, bypass the dock: connect the monitor to a video-capable port on the Mac, using an appropriate adapter only if needed. Apple recommends this direct-connection comparison.
  3. Check the cable’s specification. The cable and adapter must support video at the intended resolution and refresh rate. Similar-looking USB-C connectors do not establish that capability. Follow Apple’s port and connection guidance and the display’s manual.
  4. Reseat the video connection gently at both ends. If practical and safe, turn the monitor off, disconnect it from power, then reconnect it and retest. If its own menu cannot appear, follow the monitor maker’s power/support instructions before changing Mac settings.
03 / Ask what macOS can see

Unlock, approve your accessory, then detect

On an Apple silicon Mac laptop, unlock the Mac and reconnect your own trusted display or adapter. If asked, allow that accessory to connect. Apple notes that an accessory can still charge after Don’t Allow; charging alone is not a video test. See accessory approval. You do not need to change the global setting to always allow everything. On a managed Mac, involve the administrator if approval is restricted.

Open Apple menu → System Settings → Displays; older macOS versions call it System Preferences → Displays. Hold Option to reveal Detect Displays, then click it. In the current layout it replaces Night Shift. Apple shows the controls.

Record whether the external display appears in settings. If listed but dark, check that display’s brightness and selected input. Apple displays can offer brightness in Displays settings; other monitors may require their own controls. Detection is a useful result; it does not guarantee that every part of the image path works.

04 / A second screen can exceed a limit

Check the chip, not just the number of sockets

Record the model, chip and macOS version from Apple menu → About This Mac. In System Settings, the menu bar’s Help → [Mac name] Specifications opens the model’s specifications; find Display Support or Video Support. Check display count and supported resolution/refresh rate. A dock does not increase Apple’s native display limit.

Examples are model-specific.

MacBook Air with M4 or M5 supports up to two external displays alongside the built-in screen, within the listed resolution/rate limits. Closing the lid adds no extra display. MacBook Pro configurations differ by chip, including Pro and Max variants; use the exact entry. Older Intel models also need their own specifications.

My second display goes dark when I open the lid.

For the base M3 MacBook Air or MacBook Pro, Apple’s two-external-display setup uses a closed lid, external keyboard and mouse/trackpad, and power. It requires macOS Sonoma 14.3 or later on Air, 14.6 or later on Pro. Opening the lid makes the secondary external display go dark or disconnect.

Follow Apple’s M3 setup sequence once a single display works. This does not apply indiscriminately to M3 Pro/Max or other chips. Closed-lid use generally needs power and external input devices; do not close your only usable screen before those are ready.

05 / Change one thing at a time

Keep the result more specific than “no signal”

It works directly; it fails through the dock
Focus next on the dock’s supported Mac models, video ports, power and manufacturer instructions. This comparison does not identify a failed part. Do not install random display drivers or grant screen-recording access just to complete this test.
It works alone; the second display does not
Recheck the exact chip’s count, lid and resolution/rate limits before buying another adapter. More ports do not necessarily mean more independent displays.
It remains dark on the direct connection
If available, try one known-working video cable/adapter, then test the monitor with another authorized source. Record which combination works. A successful second source narrows the investigation; it does not prove the Mac’s port is broken. Extra equipment is optional.
It went black immediately after a resolution change.

Apple says to wait 15 seconds for automatic recovery, then press Esc if it has not reverted. Use the working built-in screen to select the external display in Displays settings and return to Default if available. See Apple’s resolution recovery instructions; its version selector covers other layouts.

Do not keep choosing unsupported modes. If the working screen also disappears or settings cannot be recovered, stop this checklist and get help with that new symptom.

06 / One normal software check

Save, restart, and repeat the same connection

Save work and choose Apple menu → Restart, then retest one direct display with the lid open. Apple’s restart guide warns that forced shutdown can lose unsaved changes; forcing power off is unnecessary on this responsive Mac.

If it persists, make a current backup before installing compatible macOS updates. For a non-Apple monitor, check its manufacturer’s model-specific firmware instructions. Record software versions; do not interrupt updates or apply firmware for another model.

07 / Protect the working setup

Stop before erasing or guessing at a port repair

If safe comparisons and one restart do not resolve it, ask Apple, the display/dock maker, or MacBook repair support about the result. Stop sooner for damage, a loose port, repeated disconnects, or loss of the built-in picture.

Share the Mac model/chip, macOS version, monitor and dock models, cable/adapter, input, whether Displays lists it, and direct-versus-dock results. Hide serial numbers and private screen contents. Protect important files before service. Erasing the Mac can remove unbacked-up files. No Mac erase, reinstall, recovery-mode restore, or monitor factory reset is required here; agree on a data-preservation plan before later destructive work.
