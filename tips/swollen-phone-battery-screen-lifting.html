Phone screen lifting or back separating? Treat it as a swollen battery.

By Samuel — owner & device repair technician · Published September 2, 2026

A battery can expand inside a phone and push the display or rear panel away from the frame. The gap is the warning—not something to press closed.

Direct answer: Unplug the phone, power it off if that can be done without pressing the swollen area, and stop using it. Do not charge, bend, puncture, squeeze, clamp, freeze, or mail it. Keep it away from heat and flammable material while arranging qualified battery removal.

Lithium-ion cells can generate gas as they fail. Because a phone has little empty space, the expansion often pushes on the largest movable surface: the display or back panel. Swelling is battery damage and a potential fire hazard. A phone can remain cool and functional while swollen; normal operation does not make it safe to continue using.

What battery swelling looks like

Display liftingA new gap appears along one edge, often with light visible beneath it or touch behavior changing near the separation.
Back panel separatingThe rear glass or cover rises away from the frame without a new impact.
Phone rockingThe device no longer lies flat even though the camera bump is accounted for.
Localized pressureA bright spot, color distortion, or touch problem appears where the battery presses on the display.
Chemical or sweet smellAn unusual odor, haze, hissing, or heat is an escalation. Move away and contact emergency services if fire appears imminent.

A bent frame, failed adhesive, or previous poor repair can also create a gap. Until the battery is inspected, treat unexplained separation as swelling because pressing on it carries a much larger consequence.

What to do now

  1. Disconnect power. Unplug wired charging and remove it from a wireless charger.
  2. Power it off if safe. Do not press hard on a button or screen directly over the bulge. If shutdown requires force, leave the device alone and seek help.
  3. Remove only a loose case. Do not wrestle off a tight case that compresses the phone.
  4. Place it somewhere stable. Keep the phone at room temperature, out of sunlight, away from children, pets, paper, fabric, fuel, and other flammable material.
  5. Call before transporting it. Tell the repairer or recycler that the battery is swollen so they can give location-specific handling instructions.
Do not try to “drain it to zero.” Using the phone creates heat and electrical load. The safer move is to stop using it, not to run the battery down.

Do not do these things

  • Do not press, clamp, tape tightly, or glue the display or back down
  • Do not pierce the battery to release the gas
  • Do not pry the phone open with household tools
  • Do not put it in a freezer, refrigerator, oven, car, or direct sun
  • Do not charge it “one last time” for a backup
  • Do not place it in water
  • Do not ship it through ordinary mail or a parcel service without approved hazardous-material handling
  • Do not put the phone or battery in household trash or curbside recycling

Embedded batteries sit near fragile display cables and sharp frame edges. Apple’s own repair procedures require battery training, protective equipment, specialized tools, and fire-suppression materials; they explicitly warn against scraping, puncturing, or forcibly prying a battery.

If it becomes hot, hisses, smokes, or smells

Those signs can precede thermal runaway. Do not pick it up, breathe fumes, or attempt a normal repair. Move people and pets away. If there appears to be an imminent fire risk, call 911 and follow the dispatcher’s instructions.

Your safety is more important than the phone or its data. A damaged battery can ignite after a delay, so a symptom that stops is not proof that the risk has ended.

Temporary storage and transport

There is no single transport rule for every battery condition or location. Contact the manufacturer, repair provider, electronics recycler, or local household hazardous-waste program first. Describe the device as having an embedded swollen lithium-ion battery.

Until then

Leave the device undisturbed in a cool, dry, stable location away from combustible material. The EPA lists a bucket with a fire suppressant such as sand or kitty litter as one possible safe-location approach when no fire is imminent.

Do not mail it

Damaged lithium batteries have special transportation restrictions. A normal return label is not automatic authorization to ship a visibly swollen device.

Do not trash it

Garbage trucks and recycling equipment can crush the battery and start a fire. Use a battery-capable electronics recycler or household hazardous-waste collection point.

Is the phone repairable?

Often, yes. If the battery expanded gradually without overheating or damaging other parts, careful battery replacement and inspection may return the phone to service. The technician should check the display, rear panel, frame, charging behavior, and internal connectors before calling it complete.

Replacement becomes more sensible when the phone is very old, the frame or screen was damaged by the pressure, parts are unavailable, or the repair cost approaches the value of a reliable replacement. Data needs can change the decision: a repair may still be worthwhile long enough to recover and transfer information.

What to tell the repairer

  • Phone make and exact model
  • Which panel is lifting and how wide the gap is
  • Whether it is hot, smells unusual, hisses, or has smoked
  • Whether it was recently dropped, wet, repaired, or exposed to high heat
  • Whether important data exists only on the phone

FAQ

Can I keep using it if it still works?

No. Continued operation and charging add heat and stress to an already damaged battery.

Can I back it up first?

Do not charge or continue using a visibly swollen phone for a backup. Ask a technician whether the battery can be safely disconnected or replaced before data transfer.

Will the screen go back down after battery replacement?

Sometimes, but not always. Prolonged pressure can warp the display, weaken adhesive, bend the frame, or damage touch and image layers.

Can I throw it in the regular recycling bin?

No. Use an electronics recycler or household hazardous-waste program that accepts damaged lithium-ion batteries.
