Phone sound · After a drop · Speaker

Speaker not working
after the drop?

Samuel, Owner & Technician · November 21, 2026

Turn off Silent mode and Bluetooth, take the case off and restart. If it’s still silent or crackling, play the same clip through the speaker and through headphones: clean in headphones and bad on the speaker means the speaker took the hit.

A phone has at least two speakers: an earpiece at the top for calls held to your ear, and a louder speaker at the bottom for speakerphone, ringtones and media. A drop can knock either one loose, damage it, or dent the grille in front of it. But a “broken speaker” can also be a setting, a Bluetooth connection, a case, or the other person’s signal. Five minutes of tests tells you which speaker failed, and whether it’s the speaker at all.

For a phone whose sound went silent, crackly, rattly, buzzy or tinny after a drop, on iPhone, Galaxy or Pixel. If it got wet, or the only problem is that calls don’t ring, the guides linked below fit better.

01 / Two minutes first

Rule out the settings before blaming the drop

A drop is a convincing suspect, but a phone also goes quiet for ordinary reasons. These take two minutes and they’re what Apple, Samsung and Google all list first:

Silent mode offBluetooth offCase offOne restart
  • Silent mode and Do Not Disturb. On an iPhone with a Ring/Silent switch, Apple says to move it toward the screen so no orange shows. With an Action button, check Silent mode in Control Center. Then check that Do Not Disturb is off.
  • Bluetooth. Samsung says audio can be redirected to an external device, and both Samsung and Google say to turn Bluetooth off to test. Unplug anything in the port or headphone jack too.
  • Case and protector. All three say to remove cases, films and screen protectors. A case knocked out of shape in the fall can sit over a grille.
  • Restart once. Samsung says to power off, wait 30 seconds and turn it back on. On a Pixel 6 or later, Google says to hold Power and Volume up, then tap Restart.

If music and videos play fine and the only problem is that calls don’t ring, it isn’t the speaker. Go to iPhone not ringing for incoming calls.

02 / Top, bottom, or both

Find out which speaker failed

Phones use different speakers for different jobs. Google describes it plainly for Pixel: the top speaker is the receiver and mainly works during calls, while the bottom speaker is larger, usually louder, and only plays during a call if you turn on speakerphone. Both play music. iPhones and Galaxy phones also have a top earpiece and a bottom speaker.

Call held to your earEarpiece, at the top
SpeakerphoneBottom speaker
Music and videosBottom, often with the top
  1. Test the bottom speaker. On an iPhone, Apple says to open Settings > Sounds & Haptics and drag the Ringtone and Alerts slider back and forth. On any phone, play a video with someone talking at a middle volume.
  2. Test the earpiece. Call someone and hold the phone to your ear normally.
  3. Test speakerphone on the same call.
  4. Listen at each end. Turn the volume down first. With a video playing, hold the top of the phone near your ear, then the bottom. A dead end is easy to hear this way.

Write down which one fails. “Calls at my ear are silent but speakerphone works” is a very different job from “nothing comes out of the bottom.”

03 / Speaker or the sound

Is it the speaker, the microphone, or the call?

Before blaming a speaker, prove the sound reaching it is clean. These three checks separate the speaker from everything upstream of it:

Play it through headphones
Play the same clip through wired or Bluetooth headphones, then through the phone. Clean in headphones and bad on the phone’s speaker points at the speaker or its path. Bad in both points at the file, the app or the phone’s audio, not the speaker.
Record yourself, then listen on headphones
Apple’s microphone check is to record a Voice Memo while speaking at the bottom of the phone and play it back. Listen to that recording through headphones. If your voice is clear in headphones but crackles on the speaker, the microphone is fine and the speaker isn’t. If the recording itself is crackly or muffled, it’s the microphone side: see iPhone microphone not working.
Crackling only during calls
Apple says that if you hear static or crackling on speakerphone, your network or reception could be the issue, and to try again later or somewhere else. Google’s Pixel steps start the same way: check your signal, and turn Wi-Fi off or on before you make a call.

Google adds one more: play two or three different songs or videos from different apps. If only some sound distorted, those files could be the problem. If everything sounds distorted, Google says to contact Pixel support.

04 / What a drop does

Why a speaker crackles, rattles or buzzes after a drop

A phone speaker is a small separate part with a thin moving membrane, held in place in the frame and connected to the board. A drop can affect it in a few ways, and each one sounds a little different:

Rattles or buzzes on deep notes or louder volumes
The speaker or something near it is loose, or its membrane is damaged. The buzz usually gets worse as the volume goes up. Test at a middle volume: Google notes that some music sounds distorted when played loudly anyway.
Cuts in and out, or comes back when you press the phone
That points at the speaker’s connection rather than the speaker itself. Don’t keep squeezing or twisting the phone to make it work. It can make a loose connection worse.
Quiet or tinny, but clean
Something is in front of the sound: a dented grille, lint packed in by the fall, or a case pressed against the opening. It can also be a damaged speaker that still works.
Completely silent from one end
The speaker or its connection has failed. On an iPhone, Apple says that if the Ringtone and Alerts slider makes no sound, or its speaker button is dimmed, the speaker might need service.

Look at the bottom edge too. A corner that took the hit can dent the frame right at the grille, or crack the glass near the earpiece at the top. Visible damage at the end that went quiet is good evidence the drop did it.

Galaxy after a flight or a mountain pass?Samsung says Galaxy speakers can sound quiet, crackly or distorted after a large change in air pressure, because of their water-resistant design. It usually settles on its own, and Samsung says briefly sliding the SIM tray halfway out speeds it up.
05 / Brush, don’t poke

Clean the grille, gently, and only from outside

Lint and grit in a grille can make a speaker sound quiet or muffled, and a fall can pack it in further. Apple says to check the speaker opening, and on an iPhone the receiver, for anything blocking it, and if necessary to clean it with a small, soft-bristled brush that’s clean and dry. Google says to clean a dirty Pixel speaker or microphone gently with a soft, lint-free cloth.

  • Brush across the grille, not into it. The aim is to lift lint off the mesh, not push it through.
  • No compressed air. Apple and Samsung both say not to use it.
  • No pins, needles or toothpicks. They can tear or push in the mesh, which is easy to damage and is what keeps dust out.
  • Skip it if the grille is dented or the frame is cracked there. Brushing won’t fix a bent grille, and it can push debris into the gap.

If it sounded fine before the drop and only changed at the moment it hit, cleaning is unlikely to be the whole answer. It’s still worth doing once, because it rules out the easy cause.

06 / One result, one next step

Let the result decide what happens next

Sound came back after the quick checks
It was a setting, Bluetooth or the case. Keep an ear on it for a day. Crackle that comes back points at the hardware.
Clean in headphones, bad on one speaker
That speaker, its connection or its grille took the drop. It needs a bench look. Back up before it goes in, as with any repair: back up before a repair.
Bad in headphones as well
Not a speaker problem. Try a second app and a restart. If every app sounds wrong, the phone needs a proper diagnosis.
Your voice memo is bad even in headphones
The microphone side, not the speaker. Read iPhone microphone not working.
It got wet as well
Stop testing at volume and read iPhone speaker muffled after water. Water changes the first steps.
Hot, swelling or lifting? Stop testing.

If the phone gets hot, or the screen or back is lifting after the drop, that’s a battery problem, not a speaker one. Stop charging it and read screen lifting or swollen battery. If any liquid was involved, go to speaker muffled after water first.

On a Galaxy, Samsung’s own speaker test is in Settings > Device care > Diagnostics > Phone diagnostics on Android 12 and later, or in the Samsung Members app under Diagnostics. A failed Speaker test is worth a screenshot for the quote.

07 / Send the evidence, not a guess

What to send for an accurate quote

  • The exact model.
  • Which fails: calls at your ear, speakerphone, media, or all of them.
  • Whether the same clip sounds clean through headphones.
  • A short video, recorded on another phone, of the crackle or rattle at a middle volume.
  • A photo of the corner or edge that hit, and any cracks or earlier repairs.
