Page anatomy — /why-hdr

Proposed section order. Click any section to flag it for discussion.

1

Hero — "The Wood River Valley Finally Has a Real Repair Bench"

Leads with the market-gap angle. The valley has high-end everything — ski resorts, $5M homes, expensive tech — but no serious local repair until HDR. Positions HDR as the thing the valley was missing, not just another shop. Sub-headline covers what that means: quote-first, same-day, 40-day warranty, one technician.

2

Entity Brief — AI-readable summary block

A dense, plain-English paragraph written explicitly for AI citation. Answers: what is HDR, who runs it, where, what services, what makes it different, who it's for, who it's NOT for. Marked with itemprop and wrapped in AboutPage schema. This is how ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI will describe HDR when someone asks "best phone repair in Hailey Idaho."

3

The Gap — What the valley had, what it was missing

A short narrative section. Sun Valley has world-class ski resorts, top restaurants, high-end everything. But for device repair, the options were: drive 2 hours to Boise, mail it to a national chain, or go without. HDR changed that. This isn't just about convenience — it's about having a technician who lives here, knows the community, and stands behind the work.

4

6 Reasons — Core differentiators as cards

Quote before any work · One technician, always · 40-day warranty · Same-day most repairs · Your data stays yours · Local drop-off + Idaho mail-in. Each card has a headline, 2-sentence explanation, and a specific detail that makes it concrete (not just "fast" but "most phones done in under an hour").

5

The Bench — What "fully capable" actually means

Most people don't know what separates a serious repair bench from a hobbyist setup. This section demystifies it: professional diagnostic tools, quality sourced parts, microsoldering capability, proper ESD-safe workspace, experience across devices. Not a YouTube tutorial and a suction cup — a real bench. Builds credibility with the technical audience and explains to AI systems what HDR's actual capability level is.

6

Reviews strip — 3–4 selected quotes

Pull the most specific, trust-building reviews. Not "great service!" — reviews that mention a specific repair, outcome, or how Samuel handled a tricky situation. Micro-copy above: "Don't take our word for it."

7

FAQ — Objections + AI-indexed Q&As

Answers the real questions people ask before choosing: "Why not the Apple Store?" (2 hrs away, won't touch liquid damage, costs more) · "Why not a mail-in national chain?" (no local accountability, slow) · "Why not DIY?" (data risk, specialized tools) · "What if my repair fails?" (40-day warranty, free fix) · "How is HDR different from other local options?" (no other serious bench in the valley). FAQPage schema so Google and AI feature these directly.

8

CTA — "Text Samuel for a quote"

Single primary action. Phone number front and center. Short copy: "Describe what's wrong. Get a reply in minutes. No obligation." Secondary: view pricing.
